## 2. Why it matters

Unlicensed activity is a third-degree felony, and "does this person need a license?" is one of the most common question shapes on the exam. The questions hide the decisive fact — usually *how the person is paid* — in the middle of a sentence.

## 3. Simple explanation

A Florida license is required when **all three** of these are present:

1. **A real estate service** — the activities listed in Chapter 475. The course mnemonic is **A BAR SALE**: **A**dvertise, **B**uy, **A**ppraise, **R**ent, **S**ell, **A**uction, **L**ease, **E**xchange. The statute's definition is broader and also covers acts such as negotiating, listing, and procuring buyers, sellers, tenants or landlords.
2. **For another** — for someone else, not for yourself.
3. **For compensation** — anything of value: money, a gift card, a car, a discount. Compensation that is only **promised or expected** still counts.

Remove any one element and no license is needed. A homeowner selling her own house (not for another) needs no license; a friend who shows a house with no expectation of pay (no compensation) needs no license.

## 4. Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Real estate service | An activity listed in Chapter 475 (A BAR SALE and related acts) |
| For another | On behalf of someone other than yourself |
| Compensation | Anything of value, paid, promised or expected |
| Sales associate | Licensee who performs services under a broker |
| Broker | Licensee who performs services for others in their own name |

## 5. Practical example

Carlos, unlicensed, tells his cousin he can find a tenant for her Orlando condo. She says, "If you do, I'll take you to dinner at a steakhouse." He finds a tenant and enjoys the dinner.

- Service? Yes — renting/leasing for another.
- For another? Yes — his cousin's condo.
- Compensation? Yes — a dinner is something of value, promised in exchange.

Carlos needed a license. The size or form of the payment does not matter.

## 6. Portugal comparison

**Familiar:** in both systems, intermediating property deals as a business is regulated.

**Different:** Florida's test is applied **to each individual** and turns on compensation. In Portugal the IMPIC license attaches to the mediation business, and consultants commonly work under the agency's license. In Florida, an unlicensed assistant who is paid per showing or per closing is breaking the law even though the brokerage itself is licensed.

**Exam implication:** whenever a scenario mentions pay per transaction, per showing, per closing, or a bonus tied to a deal, treat it as compensation for a real estate service.

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

**Memorize exactly**

- Three elements: service + for another + compensation.
- A BAR SALE: Advertise, Buy, Appraise, Rent, Sell, Auction, Lease, Exchange.
- Unlicensed practice = third-degree felony (Chapter 6 covers penalties).

**Understand**

- "Compensation" includes promised or expected value, not only money received.

## 9. Common misconceptions

- **"It's only illegal if you're paid in money."** Any thing of value counts.
- **"Doing it once is fine."** There is no one-time exemption.
- **"The brokerage is licensed, so its assistant can show homes for per-showing pay."** Each person performing services for compensation needs a license.

## 10. Memory sheet

- Service + for another + compensation = license.
- A BAR SALE.
- Promised or expected pay counts. Per-deal pay is the giveaway.

## 2. Why it matters

Exemptions are where the exam tests reading precision. Each exemption has a condition — usually *salary, not commission* or *within the scope of another profession* — and the question changes one word to remove it.

## 3. Simple explanation

Section 475.011 lists persons who do not need a real estate license even though they may perform real estate activities. The main ones:

- **Owners** selling or leasing their own property — unless they use commissioned agents to sell to the public in the ordinary course of business. Corporate officers selling the corporation's own property for a salary fall here.
- **Attorneys at law** acting within their duties as attorneys. Earning a brokerage **commission** takes them outside the exemption.
- **Certified public accountants** acting within their duties as CPAs.
- **Attorneys in fact** acting under a power of attorney **to execute contracts or conveyances only** (signing for the owner — not marketing or negotiating for a fee).
- **Court-appointed or will-appointed fiduciaries**: personal representatives, receivers, trustees, and others acting under a will or court order.
- **Salaried employees of public utilities, railroads and government agencies** handling their employer's real property, with no compensation beyond salary.
- **Salaried employees of an owner (or of a broker for an owner) of an apartment community** working in an **onsite rental office** in a leasing capacity.
- **Salaried condominium or cooperative managers** renting units for terms of **1 year or less**.

Two exemptions in the study manual need correction: the manual mentions "radon professionals", which does not appear in the statute, and it describes the attorney-in-fact exemption as "a single specific transaction" — the statute says "execution of contracts or conveyances only" (verification V-14).

## 4. Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Exemption | A category of person who need not hold a license |
| Attorney in fact | Someone holding a power of attorney (not necessarily a lawyer) |
| Attorney at law | A lawyer |
| Personal representative | The court-appointed administrator of a deceased person's estate |
| Onsite rental office | Leasing office located at the apartment community |

## 5. Practical example

Three people rent apartments at a 300-unit complex in Jacksonville:

1. **Maria** works in the complex's leasing office for a salary paid by the owner — **exempt**.
2. **João** is paid $250 for every lease he signs, by the owner — **license required** (compensation per transaction).
3. **Lena** manages rentals for twelve different owners across the city for 8% of rents — **license required** (managing for others for compensation; not an onsite salaried employee).

## 6. Portugal comparison

**Requires care:** In Portugal you may be used to seeing staff of a developer or an agency intermediate deals under the company's license. Florida asks a narrower question for each person: is there an exemption, and does its condition (salary only, onsite, scope of profession) still hold?

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

**Memorize exactly**

- Salaried onsite apartment leasing employees are exempt; per-lease pay removes the exemption.
- Condo/co-op salaried managers: rentals of **1 year or less**.
- Attorneys and CPAs: only within their professional duties.
- Attorneys in fact: executing contracts or conveyances only.

**Understand**

- Each exemption has a condition. The exam changes the condition.

## 8. Visual explanation

| Person | Exempt when… | Not exempt when… |
|---|---|---|
| Owner | Selling/leasing own property | Uses commissioned agents in the ordinary course of business |
| Attorney at law | Acting within legal duties | Earns a brokerage commission |
| Attorney in fact | Executing contracts or conveyances only | Markets or negotiates for a fee |
| Owner's onsite leasing employee | Salaried, onsite rental office | Paid per lease |
| Condo/co-op manager | Salaried; rentals ≤ 1 year | Paid by commission |

## 9. Common misconceptions

- **"Lawyers never need a real estate license."** They do if they earn a brokerage commission.
- **"Attorney in fact means lawyer."** It means anyone holding a power of attorney.
- **"Any employee of an owner is exempt."** Only if salaried and, for apartment leasing, working onsite.

## 10. Memory sheet

- Salary yes, commission no.
- Lawyers and CPAs: only inside their own profession.
- Power of attorney: sign the documents only.
- Condo/co-op manager: rentals ≤ 1 year.

## 2. Why it matters

These are exact-recall facts — ages, hours, scores and time limits — and the mutual recognition rule is a favourite trap because it depends on residency at the moment of application. It also affects you personally: you are a nonresident-born professional entering Florida licensing.

## 3. Simple explanation

**Applicant requirements (sales associate):**

- At least **18** years old.
- **High school diploma** or equivalent.
- A U.S. **Social Security number**.
- **Not required:** U.S. citizenship, Florida residency, a college degree.

**The path:** complete the **63-hour pre-license course** (63 hours of 50 minutes, including the course exam) → apply to the DBPR with fingerprints and fees → pass the state exam (**100 questions, 3.5 hours, 75 correct to pass**) → license issued. The course completion becomes invalid if you do not **pass** the state exam within **2 years** after completing the course, and the application expires **2 years** after the DBPR receives it if you have not passed (s. 475.181(2), verification V-31 and V-32). The license is **inactive until the licensee is registered with an employing broker**.

**Criminal history and honesty.** Applicants must disclose criminal history. A record does not automatically disqualify; the FREC looks at the nature of the offence, time elapsed, rehabilitation and honesty (good moral character). A **false answer** on the application is itself grounds for denial or revocation and is a crime. A denied applicant receives written notice and may request a hearing.

**Mutual recognition.** Florida has agreements with certain states. A licensee from one of those states who is **not a Florida resident when applying** can obtain a Florida license without the 63-hour course by passing a **40-question Florida law exam with at least 30 correct**. Florida residents cannot use it, and the other state's license must have been earned through that state's own course and exam. In October 2026 the DBPR Division home page lists nine states (Alabama, Arkansas, Connecticut, Georgia, Kentucky, Mississippi, Nebraska, Rhode Island, West Virginia) and shows the Illinois agreement as dissolved on December 31, 2025. The list changes over time, so it is low priority for the exam (verification V-22).

## 4. Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pre-license course | 63-hour FREC Course I |
| Good moral character | Honesty, trustworthiness and fair dealing required of applicants |
| Mutual recognition | Agreement letting nonresident licensees of certain states qualify by passing a Florida law exam |
| Registration with a broker | Required before a new license can be used |

## 5. Practical example

**Two applicants:**

- **Inês**, a Portuguese citizen living in Miami with a green card and a Social Security number, finished high school in Portugal (with an equivalency evaluation). She qualifies to apply: citizenship is not required. She must take the 63-hour course and pass the state exam.
- **Mark** holds an active Georgia license and lives in Savannah. If Georgia has an agreement with Florida when he applies, he can take the 40-question law exam. If he first moves to Jacksonville and then applies, he is a Florida resident and must take the full course and exam.

## 6. Portugal comparison

**Different:** Portuguese mediation licensing has no state exam or course-hours requirement (IMPIC lists good standing and insurance). Florida's requirements are educational and exam-based, and your Portuguese experience does not reduce the 63 hours. Mutual recognition covers only certain US states — not foreign countries.

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

**Memorize exactly**

- 18 · high school diploma or equivalent · SSN.
- Not required: citizenship, Florida residency.
- 63 hours; must PASS the exam within 2 years of finishing the course. Application expires 2 years after receipt without a pass.
- Exam: 100 questions, 3.5 hours, 75 to pass.
- Mutual recognition: nonresident at application; 40 questions; 30 to pass.

**Understand**

- Honesty on the application matters more than the record itself.
- A new license cannot be used until registered under a broker.

## 9. Common misconceptions

- **"You must be a U.S. citizen."** No.
- **"You must live in Florida."** No; nonresidents can be licensed.
- **"Mutual recognition is reciprocity for anyone licensed elsewhere."** Only nonresidents licensed in a state with an agreement.
- **"Once licensed you can start working."** Not until a broker registers you.

## 10. Memory sheet

- 18 · diploma · SSN — no citizenship, no Florida address.
- 63 hours → pass the exam within 2 years → 75/100 in 3.5 hours.
- Mutual recognition: nonresident · 40 Q · 30 to pass.
- Lie on the application = denial/revocation + crime.
