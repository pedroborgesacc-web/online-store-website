## 3. Simple explanation

The **promissory note** is the debt — the borrower's personal promise to repay (amount, rate, terms). The **mortgage** is the security instrument pledging the property. In US usage, "mortgage" means the document, not the loan.

Florida is a **lien-theory** state: the borrower (**mortgagor**) keeps legal title and the lender (**mortgagee**) holds a lien. In title-theory states the lender (or a trustee under a deed of trust) holds title. After full payment the lender must record a **satisfaction of mortgage within 60 days** (manual).

**Clauses:** acceleration (entire balance due on default); due-on-sale/alienation (balance due on transfer — blocks assumption without consent); prepayment (right or penalty); defeasance (lien defeated on full payment); subordination (lien accepts junior position); release (frees parcels from a blanket mortgage); receivership (receiver collects rents during foreclosure); exculpatory (no personal liability — no deficiency).

## 10. Memory sheet

-or gives, -ee receives: mortgagor = borrower. Lien theory. Satisfaction in 60 days.

## 3. Simple explanation

**Assumption:** the buyer becomes personally liable; the seller remains secondarily liable unless the lender grants a **novation**. **Subject to:** the buyer takes title and pays, but has **no personal liability**; on default only the original borrower faces a deficiency.

**Florida foreclosure is judicial** — a court action ending in a public sale. Until the sale, the borrower may redeem by paying in full (**equity of redemption**). Florida has **no statutory (post-sale) redemption**. If proceeds fall short, the lender may seek a **deficiency judgment** against personally liable parties; a surplus goes to junior lienors, then the borrower.

**Alternatives:** **short sale** (lender accepts less than owed); **deed in lieu of foreclosure** (faster, but the lender takes title **subject to junior liens**, which foreclosure would have eliminated).

## 10. Memory sheet

Assume = liable; subject to = not liable. Redeem before the sale. Deed in lieu keeps junior liens.

## 3. Simple explanation

**Qualifying ratios** (course conventions, not statutes):

- **Housing expense ratio** = monthly **PITI** ÷ **gross** monthly income — conventional benchmark **28%**.
- **Total obligations ratio** = (PITI + recurring debts) ÷ gross monthly income — conventional **36%**; FHA **31% / 43%**.

Worked example (manual): income $7,500; PITI $2,025; car and cards $675. Housing 2,025 ÷ 7,500 = 27% ✓. Total 2,700 ÷ 7,500 = 36% ✓. A $750 car payment would push the total to 37%.

**Usury (Florida):** **18%** per year simple interest on loans of **$500,000 or less**; **25%** above that; beyond 25% is criminal usury (manual; not verified).

**Predatory practices:** equity stripping, loan flipping (repeated refinancing to collect fees), packing (hidden add-ons), balloon abuse. The **SAFE Act** requires mortgage loan originators to be licensed/registered (NMLS).
