## 2. Why it matters

Section 4 of the state exam, "Authorized Relationships, Duties and Disclosure", carries **7%** of the score (verified, V-02), which is about 7 of the 100 questions (V-01). Nearly every question in that section depends on one fact: **which relationship exists**. Once you know that, the duties (L04-2) and the required notices (L04-3) follow.

In practice, Florida's default relationship is **not agency**. Dealing with both the seller and the buyer in the same sale is common in Portuguese practice (PT-04; requires verification), but in Florida a brokerage that acts as a **fiduciary for both sides** (dual agency) breaks the law.

## 3. Simple explanation

### First, four words

- A **fiduciary** is a person in a relationship of trust who must put another person's interests ahead of their own.
- A **principal** is the person a fiduciary agent represents.
- A **customer** is a buyer or seller the licensee works with but does **not** represent as a fiduciary.
- A **presumption** is what the law assumes is true unless something else is shown. Here, the "something else" must be **in writing**.

### The three authorized relationships

Florida law (s. 475.278, F.S.) allows exactly three relationships between a licensee and a buyer or seller:

1. **Transaction broker (TB).** *Limited representation* of the buyer, the seller, or both. The licensee helps the deal happen with skill and care, but is **not a fiduciary**. The parties are **customers**.
2. **Single agent.** Full **fiduciary** representation of **one** party only, buyer or seller. That party is the **principal**.
3. **No brokerage relationship.** The licensee deals with the party **without representing them** at all. A typical case: the brokerage represents the seller as single agent, and an unrepresented buyer comes to see the house.

"Subagent" and "dual agent" are not on the list.

### The presumption: transaction broker

> Unless a single agent relationship or a no brokerage relationship is **established in writing**, the licensee is **presumed to be a transaction broker**.

**Why the law works this way.** The default is a middle setting. A transaction broker owes enough duties to protect a customer (honesty, skill, limited confidentiality) but no fiduciary loyalty, which is what would make helping both sides impossible. Single agency moves the duties **up** and no brokerage relationship moves them **down**. A change like that should be deliberate and visible, so it must be in writing.

The presumption decides the **type** of relationship. A relationship can begin informally (L04-4). If nobody established a different type in writing, it is transaction broker.

### Dual agency is illegal

**Dual agency** means acting as a single agent (a fiduciary) for **both** the buyer and the seller in the **same transaction**. Florida prohibits it. The reason is simple: a fiduciary for the seller must try to get the highest price, and a fiduciary for the buyer must try to get the lowest. No one can owe both kinds of loyalty at once.

The relationship belongs to the **brokerage**, not only to the individual sales associate. Two associates of the same brokerage therefore cannot each be a single agent for opposite sides of a home sale, because the brokerage would still be acting as a dual agent. There is one narrow exception, **designated sales associates**, available only in nonresidential deals where the buyer and the seller **each have assets of $1 million or more** and both sign disclosures requesting it (L04-4). The test is the parties' assets, not the size of the deal.

**The lawful way to work both sides is to act as transaction broker for both.** If the brokerage started as single agent for one party, that principal must sign a **Consent to Transition to Transaction Broker** before the change (L04-3).

### Industry note (not Florida law)

Since August 2024, NAR (the National Association of REALTORS®) policy requires MLS participants to sign written agreements with buyers before touring homes (course note; not yet verified). This is a practice rule of a private trade association. It does not change Florida's three statutory relationships or the transaction-broker presumption.

## 5. Practical example

Sunshine Realty in Miami has four situations in one week. Associate Paulo handles them.

| Situation | Written? | Relationship | Party is a… |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mrs. Alves lists her Coral Gables house and signs a listing agreement establishing single agency | Yes | Single agent for Mrs. Alves | Principal |
| Mr. Brown, a buyer, calls Paulo, who shows him Brickell condos; nothing about the relationship is signed | No | Transaction broker (presumed) | Customer |
| Ms. Duarte, a buyer with no agent, wants to see Mrs. Alves's house; Paulo gives her the written No Brokerage Relationship Notice first | Yes | No brokerage relationship with Ms. Duarte | Customer |
| Mr. Brown now wants to buy Mrs. Alves's house | — | See below | — |

**The Brown–Alves problem.** Sunshine Realty is single agent for the seller. It may **not** also become Mr. Brown's single agent; that would be dual agency. Colleague Rita offers to "be Brown's single agent while Paulo stays the seller's agent", but this is still dual agency, because both associates belong to the same brokerage and this is a house sale. The lawful route is for Mrs. Alves to sign the **Consent to Transition to Transaction Broker** before the change. Sunshine Realty then acts as **transaction broker for both**.

## 6. Portugal comparison

**Familiar principle.** In Portuguese practice an agency often deals with both seller and buyer in the same sale, while the mediation contract is signed with one client. This description, and the agency's exact legal duties toward the party who is not its client, **require verification** (PT-04).

**Florida rule.** Florida gives each relationship a statutory name and a statutory list of duties. The default is transaction broker unless single agency or no brokerage relationship is established in writing. Fiduciary representation of both sides (dual agency) is prohibited.

**The precise difference.**

- In Florida, **representing** both sides is lawful **only** as transaction broker for both, or with designated sales associates in nonresidential deals where each party has assets of $1 million or more. (A brokerage may also be single agent for one side and have no brokerage relationship with the other; then it represents only one side.)
- A Florida relationship with legal duties can exist **without any signed contract**, because the presumption fills the gap.

**Exam implication.** Any answer choice containing "dual agent", or "dual agency with written consent", is wrong. Never answer from Portuguese both-sides practice.

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

**Memorize exactly**

- Three authorized relationships: transaction broker, single agent, no brokerage relationship (manual; not yet verified).
- Presumption: **transaction broker**, unless single agent or no brokerage relationship is **established in writing** (manual; not yet verified).
- Dual agency is prohibited. Transaction broker for both sides is lawful (manual; not yet verified).
- Single agent → **principal**. Transaction broker or no brokerage relationship → **customer** (manual; not yet verified).
- A transaction broker is **not a fiduciary** (manual; not yet verified).
- Exam weight of Section 4: 7% (verified, V-02).

**Understand**

- The default sits in the middle. Writing moves duties up (single agent) or down (no brokerage relationship).

- Moving from single agent to transaction broker requires the principal's prior written consent (verified, V-48; L04-3).

## 9. Common misconceptions

- **"Dual agency is fine with written consent."** Not in Florida. Written consent allows a **transition to transaction broker**, never dual agency.
- **"Transaction brokers owe loyalty."** Only single agents owe loyalty.
- **"No signed agreement means no relationship and no duties."** Without writing, the licensee is presumed to be a transaction broker and owes those duties.
- **"Transaction broker means the licensee represents nobody."** That describes a no brokerage relationship. A transaction broker gives limited representation.
- **"Two associates in the same brokerage can each be a single agent for one side of a home sale."** That is dual agency by the brokerage.
- **"Since NAR requires written buyer agreements, Florida now presumes single agency for buyers."** The NAR rule is trade-association practice; the statutory presumption is unchanged.

## 10. Memory sheet

- **Three relationships:** transaction broker (default) · single agent · no brokerage relationship.
- **Nothing in writing = transaction broker.**
- Single agent → **principal**. TB and no brokerage → **customer**.
- **Never dual agency.** Both sides = TB for both, after the principal's written consent to transition.
- The relationship belongs to the **brokerage**.
- NAR's buyer-agreement rule is industry practice, not Florida law.

## 2. Why it matters

The three duty lists, **3 · 7 · 9**, are some of the highest-yield memory material in the course. Exam questions come in three shapes:

- "How many duties…?"
- "Which duty is owed by a single agent but NOT by a transaction broker?"
- A short story followed by "Which duty did the licensee breach?"

In daily practice the lists decide what you may tell the other side in a negotiation, and what you must tell no matter who you represent.

## 3. Simple explanation

The lists come from s. 475.278 as summarized in the study manual; their exact statutory wording has not yet been checked (manual; not yet verified, V-26).

Build the lists as three floors of one building. Each floor builds on the one below. The top floor upgrades confidentiality from limited to full, adds three new duties, and does not list "additional duties mutually agreed".

### Floor 1: the 3 duties every licensee owes (no brokerage relationship)

1. **Deal honestly and fairly.**
2. **Disclose all known facts that materially affect the value of residential real property and are not readily observable to the buyer.**
3. **Account for all funds** entrusted to the licensee.

**Why:** these protect anyone who deals with a licensee, even a person the licensee does not represent. A fact "materially affects value" if it would change what a reasonable buyer pays or decides. It is "not readily observable" if an ordinary look at the property would not reveal it. L04-4 develops this duty.

### Floor 2: the 7 transaction-broker duties

The 3 above, **plus**:

4. **Use skill, care and diligence.**
5. **Present all offers and counteroffers in a timely manner**, unless a party has directed otherwise in writing.
6. **Limited confidentiality**, unless waived in writing by a party. The licensee may not reveal:
   - that the **seller will accept less** than the asking price;
   - that the **buyer will pay more** than the price offered;
   - the **motivation** of any party for selling or buying;
   - any other information a party asks to be kept confidential.
7. **Any additional duties** mutually agreed with a party.

**Why "limited":** a transaction broker may work for both sides, so the law protects each side's bargaining position without making the licensee take sides.

### Floor 3: the 9 single-agent duties

1. Dealing honestly and fairly
2. **Loyalty**: putting the principal's interests first
3. **Confidentiality** (full, not limited to four items)
4. **Obedience**: following the principal's **lawful** instructions only
5. **Full disclosure**: telling the principal everything the agent knows that could affect the principal's interests or decisions
6. Accounting for all funds
7. Skill, care and diligence
8. Presenting all offers and counteroffers in a timely manner
9. Disclosing known facts that materially affect the value of residential property and are not readily observable

**The four single-agent-only duties are Loyalty, Obedience, full Confidentiality and full disclOsure ("LOCO").**

**How 7 becomes 9.** Start from the transaction broker's 7. Upgrade limited confidentiality to **full** confidentiality. Drop "additional duties mutually agreed", which is not an item on the single-agent list. Add loyalty, obedience and full disclosure. That gives 5 shared duties + full confidentiality + 3 new = 9.

### What no duty can override

The material-fact disclosure duty is on all three lists. **Confidentiality never covers a hidden defect**, and **obedience never covers an unlawful instruction** such as "don't mention the foundation crack".

## 4. Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Deal honestly and fairly | Baseline duty owed to everyone in every relationship |
| Accounting | Duty to account for all funds entrusted to the licensee |
| Skill, care and diligence | The competence and effort expected of a licensee |
| Limited confidentiality | TB duty not to reveal price flexibility, motivation, or information a party asks to keep confidential; can be waived in writing |
| Loyalty | Single-agent duty to put the principal's interests above everyone else's, including the agent's own |
| Obedience | Single-agent duty to follow the principal's lawful instructions |
| Full disclosure | Single-agent duty to tell the principal all known information that could affect the principal |
| Readily observable | Visible on an ordinary look at the property |
| Waive | To give up a right voluntarily (here, in writing) |

## 5. Practical example

Carla lists her house in Seminole Heights, Tampa, with Gulf Realty. Nothing establishes single agency, so Gulf Realty is a **transaction broker**. Buyer Mr. Lee also works with Gulf Realty as a transaction-broker customer. Associate Marco handles both.

| Event | What Marco must do | Duty |
|---|---|---|
| Carla says, "I'd take $25,000 less; I start a new job in Atlanta in two weeks." Lee asks, "Is she flexible? Why is she selling?" | Reveal neither the price flexibility nor the move | Limited confidentiality |
| Lee says, "Offer $450,000, but I'd go to $480,000." | Not tell Carla that Lee would pay more | Limited confidentiality |
| Marco knows the attic ductwork leaks into a ceiling cavity, out of sight | Disclose it to Lee | Material fact, not readily observable |
| Carla directed **in writing**: "Don't present offers under $430,000." A $420,000 offer arrives | He need not present it; the written direction controls | Present offers, unless directed otherwise in writing |
| Lee hands over a $10,000 deposit check | Handle and account for it (deadlines in L05-1) | Accounting |

**What if Carla had signed as Gulf Realty's single-agent principal?** Marco would then owe Carla loyalty, obedience, full confidentiality and full disclosure. If Lee had **no brokerage relationship** with Gulf Realty (he was given the No Brokerage Relationship Notice), Marco would owe Lee only the 3 baseline duties, with **no confidentiality**. Marco's duty of full disclosure to Carla would then require him to tell her that Lee said he would pay $480,000. The ductwork leak would still have to be disclosed to Lee, because that duty is on every list.

## 6. Portugal comparison

**Familiar principle.** In Portuguese practice an agency often deals with both seller and buyer while the mediation contract is signed with one client. That practice, and the agency's exact statutory duties toward the party who is not its client, **require verification** (PT-04).

**Florida rule.** The duties are fixed by statute in three numbered lists, and the list depends on the relationship: 3 for no brokerage relationship, 7 for transaction broker, 9 for single agent.

**The precise difference.** Florida names each relationship and defines its duties by statute (3/7/9). Even the confidentiality a transaction broker owes is a defined list of protected items, and it can be waived only in writing. Fiduciary representation of both sides is unlawful.

**Exam implication.** Match the duty to the relationship using the Florida lists only. A Portuguese habit or rule never decides a Florida question.

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

**Memorize exactly**

- **No brokerage relationship, 3 duties:** honesty and fair dealing; disclosure of known material facts on residential property not readily observable; accounting for all funds (manual; not yet verified).
- **Transaction broker, 7 duties:** the 3, plus skill, care and diligence; presenting all offers and counteroffers timely (unless directed otherwise in writing); limited confidentiality (unless waived in writing); any additional duties mutually agreed (manual; not yet verified).
- **Single agent, 9 duties:** honesty and fairness; loyalty; confidentiality; obedience; full disclosure; accounting; skill, care and diligence; presenting offers and counteroffers timely; disclosing known material facts (manual; not yet verified).
- **Single agent only:** loyalty, obedience, full confidentiality, full disclosure (manual; not yet verified).
- **Limited confidentiality protects:** seller will accept less · buyer will pay more · any party's motivation · information a party asks to keep confidential (manual; not yet verified).

**Understand**

- Obedience is limited to **lawful** instructions.
- Material-fact disclosure is on all three lists. Neither confidentiality nor obedience can override it.
- A transaction broker is not a fiduciary, which is why it can owe the same limited duties to both sides at once.
- The lists come from s. 475.278 as summarized in the manual; their exact statutory wording is not yet in the verification log (V-26), hence the flags.

## 9. Common misconceptions

- **"A transaction broker owes loyalty (or obedience)."** Those belong only to the single agent.
- **"Limited confidentiality lets a licensee hide defects."** Material-defect disclosure is owed in every relationship.
- **"Obedience means doing whatever the principal says."** Lawful instructions only.
- **"A transaction broker is neutral, so it owes nothing to either side."** It owes 7 duties to each customer.
- **"With no brokerage relationship the licensee owes nothing."** It owes 3 duties.
- **"Honesty requires a transaction broker to tell the seller the buyer would pay more."** No. Limited confidentiality forbids it, and keeping that confidence is not dishonest.
- **"Single agent = the TB's 7 plus loyalty and obedience."** Count again: confidentiality becomes full, full disclosure is added, and "additional agreed duties" is not on the 9.

## 10. Memory sheet

| Relationship | Count | Signature duties |
|---|---|---|
| No brokerage | 3 | Honesty · material facts · accounting |
| Transaction broker | 7 | + skill/care · present offers · limited confidentiality · agreed duties |
| Single agent | 9 | + **L**oyalty · **O**bedience · full **C**onfidentiality · full discl**O**sure |

- Limited confidentiality: **less · more · motive · "keep it secret"**. Waived only in writing.
- Offers: present all, timely, unless a party directed otherwise **in writing**.
- Defects beat secrets. Obedience stops at the law.

## 2. Why it matters

Notice questions are timing and scope questions, and the exam builds its wrong answers from near-miss facts:

- a notice given "at closing" or "within 24 hours";
- a lease or a 6-unit building offered as a "residential sale";
- a transaction broker said to need a notice.

The core rules fit in a few lines, but each has a trigger ("whichever occurs first", "before showing", "before the change") that you must read precisely.

In practice, the notice must reach the party before the moment the law names, which for buyers is usually the **first showing**.

## 3. Simple explanation

### Why notices exist

Transaction broker is the presumed relationship (L04-1). A buyer or seller therefore needs to be told in writing when the licensee offers **more** than the default (single agent) or **less** (no brokerage relationship). The law also protects a principal who is about to lose fiduciary loyalty, by requiring written consent before a single agent becomes a transaction broker.

### The notices and when each is due

**1. Single Agent Notice** (s. 475.278(3); verified, V-48)

- **What:** a written notice of the single agent relationship and its duties.
- **When:** before, or at the time of, entering into a listing agreement or an agreement for representation, **or** before the showing of property, **whichever occurs first**.

**2. No Brokerage Relationship Notice** (s. 475.278(4); verified, V-48)

- **What:** a written notice of the duties the licensee owes with no brokerage relationship.
- **When:** **before the showing of property**.

**3. Transaction broker: no notice required.** It is the default relationship (manual; not yet verified).

**4. Consent to Transition to Transaction Broker** (s. 475.278(3); verified, V-48)

- A single agent may become a transaction broker **only if the principal gives written consent before the change**.
- Typical use: the brokerage is single agent for a seller, and one of its own buyer customers wants that listing (L04-1).
- Without the principal's consent, the brokerage may not make the change.

### Where the rules apply: "residential sale" (verified, V-20)

The notice requirements apply only to **residential sales**:

- improved residential property of **4 units or fewer**;
- unimproved residential property intended for **4 units or fewer**;
- agricultural property of **10 acres or fewer**.

**Why:** these are the transactions where ordinary consumers, who may not understand brokerage relationships, typically buy and sell.

### When no notice is required (s. 475.278; verified, V-20)

No notice is required in these situations (several apply even when the property fits the definition):

| Situation | Condition |
|---|---|
| The party is already represented | The licensee **knows** the party is represented by a single agent or a transaction broker |
| Bona fide open house or model-home showing | No confidential information, no offer and no representation agreement involved |
| Unanticipated casual encounter | — |
| General factual questions | Responses to general questions about advertised property |
| General information about the licensee | Communications limited to the licensee's qualifications or firm |
| Auctions | — |
| Appraisals | — |
| Rentals and leases | Unless the lease includes an **option to purchase** property with 4 or fewer residential units |
| Sale of a business enterprise | Except property with 4 or fewer residential units |
| Nonresidential transactions | Anything outside the "residential sale" definition |

The study manual's list of exceptions was incomplete. The list above comes from the statute and is a **verified addition** (V-20). V-20 also mentions a developer's on-site employees selling new units, but that item was **not seen in the statute excerpt checked**: treat it as unconfirmed (flagged), not as a settled exception.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Single Agent Notice | Written disclosure of a single agent's duties |
| No Brokerage Relationship Notice | Written disclosure of the duties owed with no brokerage relationship |
| Consent to Transition to Transaction Broker | The principal's prior written consent allowing a single agent to become a transaction broker |
| Residential sale | Sale of ≤4 residential units, unimproved land for ≤4 units, or agricultural property ≤10 acres |
| Representation agreement | A written agreement to represent a buyer or seller (for example, a buyer brokerage agreement) |
| Bona fide open house | A genuine open house or model-home showing with no confidential information, no offer and no representation agreement involved |
| Option to purchase | A right, given in a lease, for the tenant to buy the property later |

## 5. Practical example

Ana is a sales associate at Lakeside Realty in Orlando.

| Day | Event | Notice? |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | Mr. Reyes lists his 3-bedroom Winter Park house with Lakeside as **single agent**. The listing is signed before any showing. | **Single Agent Notice** to Mr. Reyes, before or at the signing (the earlier event) |
| Sat | Open house at the Reyes home. Ben asks about square footage and schools; no offer, no confidential or price talk. | None (bona fide open house) |
| Sat | A visitor tells Ana she has signed a buyer representation agreement with an agent at another brokerage | None (Ana knows the party is already represented) |
| Next Tue | Ben asks Ana to show him a duplex in College Park. Nothing about the relationship is signed. | None (Lakeside is a transaction broker by presumption) |
| Next Wed | Ben's sister wants a 12-month apartment lease, with no option to buy | None (lease without option) |
| Next Thu | Ben wants to buy the Reyes house | **Consent to Transition**, signed by Mr. Reyes **before** Lakeside changes to transaction broker for both |

**Two variations:**

- If Lakeside had chosen to deal with Ben with **no brokerage relationship** (it represents the seller), Ana would have given Ben the **No Brokerage Relationship Notice** before his private showing of the Reyes house.
- The same week a colleague shows a **6-unit** apartment building (not a residential sale) and a **9-acre** citrus grove (a residential sale: agricultural, ≤10 acres). Only the grove is inside the notice rules.

## 6. Portugal comparison

**Familiar principle.** In Portuguese practice the mediation contract is signed with one client, even when the agency deals with both seller and buyer (PT-04; this description **requires verification**). The comparison base has no entry on Portuguese notice rules.

**Florida rule.** Florida adds separate **statutory notices** about the *type* of relationship and its duties. They are triggered by events such as the first showing, not only by signing a contract, and they apply only to residential sales.

**The precise difference.** A Florida buyer may be owed a notice before anything is signed, because each notice is due no later than the first showing. Whether Portuguese law requires a written notice of the agent's role to the client or to the non-client party **requires verification**.

**Exam implication.** Use only the Florida triggers: **whichever comes first** for single agent, **before showing** for no brokerage, **before the change** for transition.

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

**Memorize exactly**

- **Single Agent Notice:** before or at the time of entering into a listing or representation agreement, **or** before showing property, **whichever occurs first** (verified, V-48).
- **No Brokerage Relationship Notice:** **before showing** (verified, V-48).
- **Transaction broker:** no notice (manual; not yet verified).
- **Consent to Transition:** the principal's **written consent before the change** (verified, V-48).
- **Residential sale:** ≤**4** units · unimproved land for ≤**4** units · agricultural ≤**10 acres** (verified, V-20).
- **Exceptions:** represented party; bona fide open house or model showing; casual encounter; general factual questions; general information about the licensee or firm; auctions; appraisals; leases without an option to buy ≤4 units; business sales (except ≤4 residential units); nonresidential (verified, V-20). A possible exception for a developer's on-site employees is unconfirmed (flagged, V-20).

**Understand**

- Each notice is tied to an event, not to a number of days. "At closing" is always too late.
- Designated sales associates (L04-4) carry the single-agent disclosure requirements even though their deals are nonresidential (verified, V-46).
- The 24-hour deadline is for delivering a copy of a written listing agreement to the person who signed it (verified, V-49; Chapter 11). It is not a notice deadline.

## 9. Common misconceptions

- **"Leases always need disclosures."** Only a lease with an option to buy property of ≤4 residential units.
- **"A 6-unit building is residential."** Not for this rule; the limit is 4 units.
- **"Any farm sale is covered."** Only agricultural property of 10 acres or fewer.
- **"Transaction brokers must give a notice."** No; it is the default.
- **"The Single Agent Notice can wait until the listing is signed."** If a showing happens first, the notice is due before the showing.
- **"The consent to transition can be signed after the change, or at closing."** It must come **before** the change.
- **"Every open house is exempt."** Only a bona fide open house with no confidential information, no offer and no representation agreement involved.

## 10. Memory sheet

- **Residential sale = ≤4 units · land for ≤4 · farm ≤10 acres.**
- Single agent: **agreement or showing, whichever first.** No brokerage: **before showing.** TB: **no notice.**
- Transition: principal signs **before** the change.
- No notice: represented party · bona fide open house · casual encounter · general questions · licensee/firm info · auction · appraisal · lease (unless option ≤4 units) · business sale (unless ≤4 residential units) · nonresidential.

## 2. Why it matters

The exam tests three separate topics here:

1. **Must this fact be disclosed?** The classic pair: a roof that leaks only in storms (must disclose) and a suicide in the house (need not).
2. **When can one brokerage give single-agent representation to both sides?** Only with designated sales associates.
3. **How does a relationship end, and which duties survive?**

In practice, you will meet these questions with real sellers who would rather not mention a problem.

## 3. Simple explanation

### A. The duty that never goes away: known material facts

In **every** relationship, even with no brokerage relationship, the licensee must disclose all facts that meet four conditions (L04-2):

- **known** to the licensee;
- **materially affect the value**: they would change a reasonable buyer's decision or the price;
- concern **residential** real property;
- **not readily observable** to the buyer: an ordinary look would not reveal them, as with a **latent** (hidden) defect.

Examples: hidden roof leaks, flooding, foundation cracks. The study manual traces this rule to the Florida case *Johnson v. Davis*, under which a seller must disclose known hidden defects that materially affect value (manual; not yet verified).

Consequences:

- A principal's instruction to stay silent is unlawful. **Obedience never covers it.**
- An **"AS IS"** contract shifts repair obligations. It does not remove the duty to disclose known latent defects.
- The duty does not depend on the buyer asking.

### B. Facts Florida declares NOT material (s. 689.25; verified, V-47)

The following are **not material facts that must be disclosed**:

- that an occupant **had HIV or AIDS**;
- that a **homicide, suicide or death** occurred on the property.

**Why:** these are "stigma" facts. They may disturb a buyer, but they do not change the physical property.

Two cautions:

- Do not volunteer an occupant's HIV/AIDS status. The study manual says a licensee must not disclose it (manual; not yet verified; V-47 confirms only that it is not material). Disclosing it could also raise fair housing (handicap) issues.
- The statute removes the duty to **disclose**, not the duty to **deal honestly**, which is on every duty list (L04-2). It is no excuse for a false statement.

### C. Property tax disclosure (s. 689.261; verified, V-75)

A buyer of residential property must receive a **Property Tax Disclosure Summary at or before the contract**. It warns the buyer not to rely on the seller's current taxes, because a sale or improvements can raise the assessment (Chapter 11).

### D. Designated sales associates (s. 475.2755; verified, V-46)

This is the **only** way one brokerage can give **single-agent** representation to both sides of one deal. Three conditions must all be met:

1. **Not a "residential sale"** under the L04-3 definition. Examples: an office building, a warehouse, or an apartment building of more than 4 units. Exam questions say "nonresidential" or "commercial".
2. Buyer and seller **each have assets of $1 million or more**. The test is the parties' assets, not the price.
3. The customers **request** it, and both **sign disclosures** stating that their assets meet the threshold and asking for this form of representation.

The broker then designates one associate as single agent for the buyer and another as single agent for the seller.

- Each designated associate owes a **single agent's duties** (the 9), including the single-agent disclosure requirements.
- They may not disclose a customer's confidential information except to the broker or persons the broker specifies, or as the law permits or requires.
- The broker must keep that information confidential and may not use it to the other party's detriment.

### E. How relationships begin and end (manual; not yet verified)

**Beginning.** A relationship is created by **agreement**:

- **express**: a signed listing or buyer agreement; or
- **implied** by conduct.

No particular form is needed except where the disclosure rules require writing. Its *type* defaults to transaction broker (L04-1). A written listing must have a definite expiration date (s. 475.25(1)(r); verified, V-49; Chapter 11).

**Ending.**

- performance (closing);
- expiration;
- mutual agreement;
- revocation by the principal or renunciation by the broker (either may end it, but breaching the contract can create liability for damages);
- death or incapacity of a party;
- destruction of the property;
- bankruptcy of the principal.

**What survives:** **confidentiality** and **accounting** for funds.

## 4. Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Material fact | A fact that would affect a reasonable person's decision or the property's value |
| Latent defect | A hidden defect not discoverable by ordinary inspection |
| Stigma fact | A fact (such as a death on the property or an occupant's HIV/AIDS status) that may affect feelings about a property but not its physical condition |
| AS IS | The buyer takes the property in its current condition; it does not cancel the disclosure duty |
| Designated sales associate | An associate appointed as single agent for one party in a nonresidential deal where each party has $1M+ in assets |
| Revocation / renunciation | The principal ends the relationship / the broker ends it |

## 5. Practical example

**Fort Myers: one house, four facts.** Mr. Hale sells his house "AS IS" through Palm Realty, a transaction broker. Associate Sofia knows the following:

| Fact | Disclose? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The roof leaks into the attic in heavy rain; the stain is painted over | **Yes** | Known, material, not readily observable |
| A large driveway crack anyone can see | Not under this duty, but never misrepresent it | Readily observable; honesty still applies |
| The previous owner died in the house | No duty to volunteer | s. 689.25 |
| A former tenant had AIDS | Do not volunteer | s. 689.25; fair housing risk |

Mr. Hale says, "Don't mention the roof." Sofia must still disclose it. The buyer also gets the Property Tax Disclosure Summary at or before the contract.

**Jacksonville: designated sales associates.**

- A logistics company (assets $20 million) buys a $6 million warehouse from an investor (assets $8 million). Both are customers of the same firm.
- They ask in writing and both sign the asset disclosures. The broker designates Lucas for the seller and Mia for the buyer, each as a single agent.
- Later the company's CEO wants to buy a $2.5 million oceanfront **house** listed by the same firm. No designation is possible, because it is a residential sale. The firm can act as transaction broker for both (if it is the seller's single agent, only after the seller's written consent to transition).

**After expiry.** A Naples listing expires. Asked for the seller's old bottom line, the former single agent must refuse: **confidentiality survives**.

## 6. Portugal comparison

**Familiar principle.** The comparison base has one Portuguese entry for this chapter (PT-04): in Portuguese practice an agency often deals with both seller and buyer while the mediation contract is signed with one client, and even that description **requires verification**. Two further Portuguese points also **require verification**: the agency's and seller's duties to disclose hidden defects or deaths, and whether any structure resembles designated sales associates.

**Florida rule.** A relationship may begin without any writing (express or implied agreement). Disclosure of known latent defects, the stigma exception and designated sales associates are each fixed by Florida law.

**The precise difference.** The stigma rule and the both-sides exception are specific Florida statutes. Do not assume a Portuguese practice fills either gap.

**Exam implication.** Defect = disclose; stigma = not material; designated associates = nonresidential + $1M each.

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

**Memorize exactly**

- Disclose known facts that materially affect value, are not readily observable, and concern residential property, in every relationship (manual; not yet verified, as part of the duty lists).
- *Johnson v. Davis*: the seller must disclose known latent material defects (manual; not yet verified).
- **Not material** (s. 689.25; verified, V-47): homicide, suicide or death on the property; an occupant's HIV/AIDS.
- **Property Tax Disclosure Summary:** at or before the contract, for residential property (verified, V-75).
- **Designated sales associates** (verified, V-46): not a residential sale · buyer and seller **each $1 million+** in assets · at the customers' request · both sign disclosures · each associate owes single-agent duties.
- **Ends by:** performance, expiration, mutual agreement, revocation or renunciation, death or incapacity, destruction, bankruptcy of the principal. **Survive:** confidentiality, accounting (manual; not yet verified).

**Understand**

- **Correction:** earlier course notes marked s. 689.25 "per the manual; not independently verified". It is now **verified** (V-47).
- The $1 million threshold is the same in every statute edition checked, 2000/2002 through 2026 (V-46).
- A stigma fact is "not material" only for the disclosure duty. Honesty still forbids a false answer.

## 9. Common misconceptions

- **"AS IS means no disclosure."** Known latent defects must still be disclosed.
- **"If the seller tells me to keep quiet, obedience protects me."** Obedience covers lawful instructions only.
- **"A death in the house is a material fact."** Florida law says it is not.
- **"The stigma statute lets me deny a death if asked."** It removes the duty to disclose, not the duty of honesty.
- **"Designated associates are allowed for any deal over $1 million, or any house sale both parties agree to."** The test is each party's assets, and never in a residential sale.
- **"Once the listing expires I can share the seller's bottom line."** Confidentiality survives.

## 10. Memory sheet

- **Defect = disclose. Stigma = not material.** Known + material + hidden + residential → disclose, in all relationships.
- Not material: **death / homicide / suicide · HIV/AIDS** (s. 689.25). Never lie; never volunteer HIV/AIDS.
- Tax summary: at or before the contract; don't rely on the seller's taxes.
- **Designated associates:** nonresidential + $1M each side + written request and disclosures → two single agents in one firm.
- Ends: performance · expiration · agreement · revocation or renunciation · death or incapacity · destruction · bankruptcy.
- **Confidentiality and accounting outlive the relationship.**
