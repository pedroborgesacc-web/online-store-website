## 3. Simple explanation

Florida (s. 475.278) authorizes three relationships between a licensee and a buyer or seller:

- **Transaction broker** — the **presumed** relationship unless another is established in writing. *Limited representation* of a buyer, a seller, or both; **not a fiduciary**; the party is a *customer*.
- **Single agent** — full **fiduciary** representation of one party (the *principal*).
- **No brokerage relationship** — the licensee deals with the party without representing them.

**Dual agency is illegal:** a brokerage may never be a single agent for both buyer and seller in the same transaction. The lawful way to work both sides is as a **transaction broker for both**.

Industry note (not Florida law): since August 2024 NAR policy requires MLS participants to sign written agreements with buyers before touring homes. This is a practice rule of the trade association, separate from Florida's statutory relationships.

## 6. Portugal comparison

See PT-04 in the comparison base: Florida names and limits the relationship by statute and prohibits fiduciary representation of both sides.

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

- Presumption: transaction broker.
- Dual agency prohibited; transaction broker for both is lawful.

## 9. Common misconceptions

- "Dual agency is fine with written consent." Not in Florida.
- "Transaction brokers owe loyalty." Only single agents do.

## 10. Memory sheet

Default = transaction broker. Two sides = transaction broker for both, never dual agent.

## 3. Simple explanation

**No brokerage relationship — 3 duties:** (1) deal honestly and fairly; (2) disclose all known facts that materially affect the value of residential real property and are not readily observable to the buyer; (3) account for all funds entrusted.

**Transaction broker — 7 duties:** the 3 above, plus (4) use skill, care and diligence; (5) present all offers and counteroffers in a timely manner, unless a party has directed otherwise in writing; (6) **limited confidentiality**, unless waived in writing — not revealing that the seller will accept less than the asking price, that the buyer will pay more than offered, a party's motivation, or any information a party asks to keep confidential; (7) any additional duties mutually agreed.

**Single agent — 9 duties:** dealing honestly and fairly; **loyalty**; **confidentiality**; **obedience** (lawful instructions only); **full disclosure**; accounting for all funds; skill, care and diligence; presenting all offers and counteroffers timely; disclosing known material facts on residential property.

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

3 · 7 · 9. Single-agent-only duties: loyalty, obedience, full confidentiality, full disclosure.

## 9. Common misconceptions

- Listing "loyalty" or "obedience" among transaction-broker duties.
- Thinking limited confidentiality lets a licensee hide defects. Material defect disclosure is owed in every relationship.

## 10. Memory sheet

| Relationship | Count | Signature duties |
|---|---|---|
| No brokerage | 3 | Honesty · material facts · accounting |
| Transaction broker | 7 | + skill/care · present offers · limited confidentiality · agreed duties |
| Single agent | 9 | + loyalty · obedience · full confidentiality · full disclosure |

## 3. Simple explanation

**Notices:**

- **Single Agent Notice** — before or at the time of entering into a listing or representation agreement, or before showing property, **whichever comes first**.
- **No Brokerage Relationship Notice** — **before showing** the property.
- **Transaction broker** — **no notice required** (it is the default).
- **Consent to Transition to Transaction Broker** — the principal signs it **before** the single agent changes to transaction broker (typically when the brokerage's own buyer wants its listing).

**Scope — "residential sale" (verified V-20):** improved residential property of **4 units or fewer**; unimproved residential property intended for **4 units or fewer**; agricultural property of **10 acres or fewer**.

**Not required (s. 475.278, verified V-20):** when the licensee knows the party is already represented by a single agent or transaction broker; bona fide open houses or model-home showings with no confidential information, offer, or representation agreement; unanticipated casual encounters; responses to general factual questions about advertised property; communications limited to general information about the licensee's qualifications or firm; auctions; appraisals; rentals and leases (unless they include an option to purchase property with four or fewer residential units); dispositions of business enterprises (except property with four or fewer residential units); nonresidential transactions. The study manual's list was incomplete; the full list is a verified addition.

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

4 units · 10 acres. Single agent notice: earlier of agreement or showing. No-brokerage notice: before showing. Transition consent: before the change.

## 9. Common misconceptions

- "Leases always need disclosures." Only with an option to buy ≤4 units.
- "A 6-unit building is residential." Not for this rule.

## 10. Memory sheet

Residential sale = ≤4 units · land for ≤4 · farm ≤10 acres. TB needs no notice.

## 3. Simple explanation

**Material facts.** Regardless of relationship, licensees must disclose known facts that materially affect the value of residential property and are not readily observable (*Johnson v. Davis*): hidden roof leaks, flooding, foundation cracks. An instruction to stay silent is unlawful; obedience never covers it.

**Not material by Florida law** (s. 689.25 per the manual; not independently verified): that a homicide, suicide or death occurred on the property, and that an occupant had HIV/AIDS. A licensee need not volunteer these; disclosing HIV status could also raise fair housing (handicap) issues.

**Property tax disclosure.** Buyers must be told not to rely on the seller's current taxes, because the property will be reassessed after purchase (Chapter 11).

**Designated sales associates.** In a **nonresidential** transaction where buyer and seller **each have assets of $1 million or more**, they may ask the broker to designate one associate as single agent for the buyer and another as single agent for the seller, with signed disclosures. Never in residential sales.

**Beginning and ending.** Relationships are created by agreement (express or implied). They end by performance (closing), expiration, mutual agreement, revocation/renunciation (possibly with liability for damages), death or incapacity of a party, destruction of the property, or bankruptcy of the principal. **Confidentiality and accounting survive** termination.

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

Designated sales associates: nonresidential + $1M assets each side. Surviving duties: confidentiality, accounting.

## 9. Common misconceptions

- "AS IS means no disclosure." Known latent defects must still be disclosed.
- "Once the listing expires I can share the seller's bottom line." Confidentiality survives.

## 10. Memory sheet

Defect = disclose; stigma = not material. Commercial + $1M each = designated associates. Confidentiality outlives the relationship.
