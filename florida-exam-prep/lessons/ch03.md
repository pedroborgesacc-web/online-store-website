## 2. Why it matters

Every official action on a Florida license is taken by one of two state bodies. Applications, licenses, records and investigations belong to the **DBPR**. Rules and discipline belong to the **FREC**. The exam keeps asking "Which body does X?" and tests the FREC's make-up with exact numbers: 7 members, 4-1-2 seats, 5 and 2 years of experience, one member aged 60 or older, 4-year terms and a quorum of 4.

This is Section III of the official exam outline (*License Law and Commission Rules*, **2%**, about 2 questions). The same "which body" skill comes back in Chapter 6 on complaints and penalties. In practice, it tells you who sent the letter you are holding and what that body can and cannot do.

## 3. Simple explanation

### Two bodies, two kinds of job

- The **DBPR (Department of Business and Professional Regulation)** runs the system through its **Division of Real Estate**. It takes applications, issues licenses, keeps records and investigates complaints. Chapter 455 F.S. is the general law for all the professions the DBPR regulates; Chapter 475 is the real estate license law.
- The **FREC (Florida Real Estate Commission)** makes the rules and judges licensees. The DBPR's Division of Real Estate provides its administrative support.

The logic: the people who investigate a case are not the people who decide it, and the profession's rules are written by people who know it, with consumer members at the table.

### Who sits on the FREC (s. 475.02, F.S.)

**7 members**, appointed by the **Governor**, confirmed by the **Senate**, for **4-year terms**.

| Seats | Who | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | Brokers | Active license for the **5** years before appointment |
| 1 | Broker or sales associate | Active license for the **2** years before appointment |
| 2 | Consumer members | Are not, and have **never** been, brokers or sales associates |
| (any seat) | At least one of the 7 | Aged **60 or older** |

### Quorum and term limits

A **quorum** is the minimum number of members who must be present before a body can act.

- **Quorum (s. 455.207):** **51% or more** of the appointed members. For the FREC that is **4**. Official action needs a **majority of the quorum**.
- **Term limit (s. 20.165(5)):** no member may serve more than the rest of a predecessor's term plus **two consecutive 4-year terms** of their own.
- Terms end on **October 31**.

### Three kinds of power

"Quasi" means "almost": the FREC is not a legislature or a court, but it does work that resembles both.

| Power | Meaning | Who | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Quasi-legislative** | Adopting rules with the force of law, within the statute's limits | FREC | Rule Chapter 61J2 F.A.C. |
| **Quasi-judicial** | Deciding individual cases and issuing final orders | FREC | Fining, suspending, revoking |
| **Executive / administrative** | Running the law day to day | DBPR | Applications, licenses, records, investigations, support to the FREC |

FREC discipline (s. 475.25(1)): denial, reprimand, fine up to **$5,000 per count**, probation, suspension up to **10 years**, revocation.

### The complaint path in one line

Complaint → **DBPR investigates** → FREC **probable cause panel** decides whether to go forward → **FREC final order** → review by a **District Court of Appeal** (notice within **30 days** after the order is rendered, s. 120.68). The panel has **at least two members**, including a **current FREC member**; a **former** member may serve; probable cause is decided by **majority vote** (s. 455.225(4); Rule 61J2-20.009). Chapter 6 covers the details.

### What neither body can do

Neither the FREC nor the DBPR can imprison anyone or order a licensee to pay damages to a consumer. Jail (for example, for unlicensed activity, a third-degree felony) comes only from a **criminal court**. Damages are awarded only by a **civil court**.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| FREC | Florida Real Estate Commission: 7 appointed members; makes rules, decides discipline |
| DBPR | Department of Business and Professional Regulation: applications, licenses, records, investigations |
| Division of Real Estate | The part of the DBPR that handles real estate and serves the FREC |

## 5. Practical example

**Part A: two vacancies.** The Governor must fill one broker seat and one consumer seat.

| Candidate | Broker seat? | Consumer seat? |
|---|---|---|
| **Marta**, retired teacher in Sarasota, never licensed | No | **Yes** |
| **Kevin**, a broker first licensed (as a sales associate) 3 years ago and active ever since | **No**: needs 5 years of active licensure | No |
| **Paul**, a banker who held a sales associate license 20 years ago | No | **No**: consumer members must *never* have been licensed |

Kevin could fill the "broker or sales associate" seat (2 years) if it were open.

**Part B: a complaint.** A buyer says Diego, a sales associate in Orlando, never passed her $8,000 deposit to his broker.

| Step | Who acts |
|---|---|
| Complaint filed and investigated | **DBPR** |
| Probable cause? | FREC **probable cause panel** |
| $3,000 fine and 6-month suspension | **FREC** final order (within the $5,000-per-count and 10-year limits) |
| Diego wants the order reviewed | **District Court of Appeal**, notice within 30 days |
| The buyer wants her $8,000 back | **Civil court** (the FREC cannot award damages) |
| Diego is charged with a crime | **Criminal court** (only it can impose jail) |

Five members attend the FREC meeting that hears the case, so there is a quorum. With only 3, the FREC could not act.

## 6. Portugal comparison

**Familiar principle.** In Portugal, one public institute, **IMPIC, I.P.**, both licenses and supervises real estate mediation businesses, and it approves standard mediation contract models.

**Florida rule.** The work is split three ways: the **FREC** makes rules and disciplines; the **DBPR** handles applications, licenses, records and investigations; the **courts** handle crimes, damages and review of final orders.

**Precise difference.** Florida separates investigating (DBPR) from deciding (FREC), and the deciding body's seats are fixed by statute (4 brokers, 1 broker or sales associate, 2 consumers). How IMPIC's leadership is appointed and how Portuguese sanctions are decided *requires verification*.

**Exam implication.** Never answer "the regulator". Name the body: DBPR investigates, FREC decides, courts jail and award damages.

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

**Memorize exactly**

- **7** members · **Governor** appoints · **Senate** confirms · **4-year** terms (s. 475.02).
- **4** brokers (active **5** years) · **1** broker or sales associate (active **2** years) · **2** consumers never licensed as broker or sales associate · at least one member **60+**.
- Quorum **4** (51% or more of 7, s. 455.207); action needs a majority of the quorum.
- Term limit: remainder of a predecessor's term + **two consecutive 4-year terms** (s. 20.165(5)); terms end **October 31**.
- Rules → FREC (quasi-legislative). Discipline → FREC (quasi-judicial). Applications, licenses, records, investigations → DBPR.
- FREC maximums: **$5,000 per count**, suspension **10 years**, revocation (s. 475.25(1)).
- Probable cause panel: at least **two** members, including a current FREC member; majority vote (s. 455.225(4)).
- Review of a final order: District Court of Appeal, **30 days** (s. 120.68).

**Understand**

- The quorum of 4 and the two-consecutive-term limit are verified (V-13). They come from s. 455.207 and s. 20.165(5), not from s. 475.02 itself.
- The panel's "at least two members" is verified (V-42), but the current rule's wording on panel size was seen only in fragments.
- Investigation and decision are kept apart on purpose: the DBPR gathers the facts; the FREC, as judge, decides.

## 9. Common misconceptions

- **"Licensees elect the FREC."** The Governor appoints and the Senate confirms.
- **"All members are brokers."** Only 4 must be; two seats go to consumers who were never licensed.
- **"A former licensee can be a consumer member."** No: consumer members must never have been brokers or sales associates.
- **"The FREC investigates complaints."** The DBPR investigates; the FREC decides.
- **"Quorum is 3, about half of 7."** It is 51% or more, which means 4.
- **"Two terms means 8 years at most."** A member may also first serve the rest of a predecessor's term, then two consecutive 4-year terms.
- **"The FREC can award the consumer damages or send a licensee to jail."** Only courts can.

## 10. Memory sheet

- **7 · Governor + Senate · 4-year terms.**
- **4-1-2:** four brokers (5 yrs), one broker or sales associate (2 yrs), two never-licensed consumers. One member **60+**.
- **Quorum 4.** Term limit: remainder + **two** consecutive terms (s. 20.165(5)). Terms end **Oct 31**.
- Rules = quasi-legislative · discipline = quasi-judicial · both FREC. Paperwork and investigation = DBPR.
- FREC max: **$5,000 per count · 10-year suspension · revocation.**
- **DBPR digs, FREC decides, courts jail and award damages.** Appeal: 30 days.

## 2. Why it matters

You will start as a sales associate, and many violations happen when an associate does what only a broker may do: keep a deposit, take money directly from a customer, or work for a second company on the side. The exam tests look-alike terms (broker associate, group license, multiple licenses) and the exact broker path: **24 months in the preceding 5 years → 72-hour course → broker exam → 60 hours of post-license education**. It is also your own route to becoming a broker.

## 3. Simple explanation

### Two levels of license

Chapter 475 issues two license types: **sales associate** and **broker**. The difference is independence: a broker works for the public in their own name, while a sales associate works only through an employer.

| | Sales associate | Broker |
|---|---|---|
| Works for the public | Only under an employing broker (or an owner-developer) | In their own name |
| Opens an office | No | Yes |
| Holds escrow deposits | No. Deposits go to the broker by the end of the next business day (Rule 61J2-14.009) | Yes |
| Paid directly by the public | No. Only a broker may be | Yes |
| Officer or director of a brokerage corporation | No | Yes |

<p class="caption">Source note: apart from the deposit deadline (verified, V-36), the rows of this table come from the study manual and are not yet verified.</p>

A **broker associate** holds a broker license but chooses to work under another broker. It is not a third level of qualification: the person met every broker requirement.

### Licensure vs registration

Both must be current before you may work (manual; not yet verified):

- **Licensure** is the DBPR's certification that you met the qualifications (course, exam, background).
- **Registration** is the DBPR's record of your license: name, address, employer and status.

Licensure is like a diploma; registration records who you work for today.

### One employer, and the two "more than one" situations

A sales associate may hold **one** license and work for **one** employer at a time.

- **Group license.** A sales associate works for several **connected** entities of **one owner-developer** (a business that develops and sells its own property). Example: a developer that sells homes through three related companies, one per subdivision.
- **Multiple licenses.** A **broker** holds separate licenses for separate, **unconnected** brokerages. Only brokers may do this.

### The broker path (s. 475.17 and s. 475.181, F.S.)

1. **Experience.** During the **preceding 5 years**, at least **24 months** of one of the following:
   - an **active sales associate license** in the office of one or more brokers licensed in Florida, another US state, territory or jurisdiction, or a foreign national jurisdiction;
   - a current, valid **sales associate license while employed by a government agency for a salary**, performing licensee duties;
   - a current, valid **broker license** in another US state, territory or jurisdiction, or a foreign jurisdiction.

   Anyone licensed as a Florida sales associate in the preceding 5 years must also have **completed the 45-hour post-license education**.
2. **Course.** The broker pre-license course (FREC Course II): up to **72 classroom hours** of 50 minutes, **including the exam** (sales associates take 63). If absences exceed **8 classroom hours**, no completion notice is issued.
3. **Exam.** You must **pass** the state exam within **2 years** after completing the pre-license course, or the course completion becomes invalid. The application also expires **2 years** after the DBPR receives it if you have not passed.
4. **Broker post-license.** Once licensed, you open your own office or register under another broker as a **broker associate**. Then, before the **first broker renewal**, complete **60 hours** of Commission-approved broker post-license courses (60 classroom hours of 50 minutes, exam included). DBPR guidance says this may be taken as two 30-hour courses, **Broker Investment** and **Broker Management**, in a classroom or by distance learning. The only exemption is a **4-year degree in real estate**; attorneys are **not** exempt.

**If the 60 hours are missed,** the broker license "shall be considered **null and void**" (s. 475.17(4)(c)). *Flagged:* what happens next was not confirmed in the current statute text. Statute and law-book sources say that to be a broker again the person must retake the broker pre-license course and pass the broker exam, and that a way back to a **sales associate** license exists by completing the 14-hour continuing education course within 6 months after the broker license expired. Treat both as unverified.

## 4. Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Sales associate | Licensee who performs services only under an employing broker or owner-developer |
| Broker | Licensee who performs services for others in their own name |
| Broker associate | A person with a broker license who works under another broker |
| Owner-developer | A business that develops and sells its own property and may employ sales associates |
| Licensure | The DBPR's certification that a person is qualified |
| Registration | The DBPR record of a license: name, address, employer, status |
| Group license | Lets a sales associate work for connected entities of one owner-developer |
| Multiple licenses | Separate licenses a broker holds for unconnected brokerages |

## 5. Practical example

**Beatriz** is a sales associate in Tampa. She completed her 45-hour post-license course before her first renewal, worked actively for Bay Shore Realty for 30 months, then went inactive for a year. Now she wants to be a broker. After the first row, each row is a separate "what if".

| Situation | Rule | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 30 active months, all within the last 5 years | 24 months in the preceding 5 years | **Qualifies** |
| She missed 10 classroom hours of Course II | Absences over 8 hours → no completion notice | **Course not completed** |
| She completes the course but takes 26 months to pass the exam | Must pass within 2 years of completion | **Course completion invalid** |
| She passes in time and rejoins Bay Shore Realty | Broker license, working under another broker | **Broker associate** |
| No 60 hours by her first broker renewal | Broker post-license missed | Broker license **null and void** |

Three colleagues:

- **Rui** was active from 2018 to 2020, then left. Applying in 2027, he has no active months in the 5-year window: **not qualified**.
- **Lena** sells for an owner-developer's three connected companies: **group license**. She may not also work for an unrelated broker.
- **Carlos** is broker of two unconnected brokerages: **multiple licenses** (brokers only).

## 6. Portugal comparison

**Familiar principle.** In Portugal, IMPIC licenses and supervises the **mediation business**. Consultants inside a licensed agency usually work under the agency's license (*requires verification*).

**Florida rule.** Florida licenses each **person** who performs real estate services. The closest match to the Portuguese licensed agency is the Florida **broker**, who runs the office, holds escrow and is paid by the public.

**Precise difference.** Florida has no "working under the agency's license": a sales associate needs a personal license, usable only while registered under one employer.

**Your experience and the broker path.** The statute counts some **foreign** experience (an active sales associate license held in the office of a broker licensed in a foreign jurisdiction, or a foreign broker license). Whether a Portuguese career qualifies, when the Portuguese license belongs to the company, *requires verification with the DBPR*.

**Exam implication.** Use only the Florida categories (sales associate, broker, broker associate). A Portuguese rule never answers a Florida question.

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

**Memorize exactly**

- Broker experience: **24 months** within the **preceding 5 years**, in one of the three listed ways; Florida sales associates must also have completed the **45-hour** post-license course (s. 475.17(1)).

- Broker post-license: **60 hours** before the **first broker renewal**. Missed → **null and void** (s. 475.17(4)(c)). Only a 4-year real estate degree exempts; attorneys do not.
- Sales associate → deposit to broker by the **end of the next business day** (Rule 61J2-14.009).
- Two license types; broker associate = broker license under another broker (manual; not yet verified).
- Only brokers open offices, hold escrow and are paid directly by the public (manual; not yet verified).
- A sales associate may not be an officer or director of a brokerage corporation; partners and officers who deal with the public must be brokers (manual; not yet verified).
- Group license = connected entities of one owner-developer; multiple licenses = brokers only (manual; not yet verified).
- Licensure = DBPR certification of qualification; registration = name, address, employer and status on DBPR records; both must be current to operate (manual; not yet verified).

- The 24-month rule is in s. 475.17(**1**), not s. 475.17(2) as the study manual cites.
- After a voided broker license, the routes back (retake the broker course and exam; or a sales associate license through 14 hours of CE within 6 months) are **unverified**.

## 9. Common misconceptions

- **"Broker associate is a third license level."** It is a broker license used under another broker.
- **"A sales associate can work for two unrelated brokers if both agree."** Never: one license, one employer.
- **"Any 24 months of experience counts."** It must fall within the **preceding 5 years**.
- **"I have 2 years to take the broker exam."** You must **pass** it within 2 years.
- **"Missing broker post-license makes the license inactive."** It makes it **null and void**.

## 10. Memory sheet

- **Sales associate** works through an employer. **Broker** works in their own name. **Broker associate** = broker license under another broker.
- Only brokers: **office, escrow, paid directly by the public**. A sales associate may never be an officer or director of a brokerage corporation.
- **Licensure** = qualified. **Registration** = your employer and address on record.
- **Group** = one owner-developer's connected entities. **Multiple** = brokers only.
- **24 in 5 → 72 hours (≤ 8 absent) → pass within 2 years → 60 hours before first renewal, or void.**
- Sales **45**, broker **60**.

## 2. Why it matters

A Florida license is not permanent. It must be renewed every 2 years, and the first renewal carries a one-time education deadline that can end the license outright. The exam's most-swapped pair lives here: **missed post-license → null and void**, but **missed CE or renewal → involuntary inactive**. Expect questions on the 45, 14 and 28 hours, the 12- and 24-month reactivation bands, and the status words: active, voluntary inactive, involuntary inactive, suspended, revoked, null and void, ineffective.

In practice, the post-license deadline is the one that can end a new career before it starts.

## 3. Simple explanation

### The renewal calendar

Every Florida real estate license expires at midnight Eastern time on **March 31** or **September 30**. A new license is fitted to one of those two dates, so the **first renewal period is 18–24 months**. Every later period is **24 months**.

### Stage 1: post-license education (before the first renewal)

- **Sales associates: 45 hours** (s. 475.17(3)(a)). The statute caps the requirement at 45 classroom hours (50-minute hours, exam included); it is met with one or more FREC-approved courses totaling at least 45 hours. Courses may be classroom or approved distance learning; a distance course requires passing a timed exam.
- If absences exceed **10%** of classroom hours, no completion notice is issued.
- **Missed?** The license "shall be considered **null and void**." To work again, the person must requalify: complete the **63-hour** pre-license course and pass the state exam.
- **Exceptions:** the FREC may allow **6 extra months** for an individual physical hardship (as defined by rule). Holders of a **4-year or higher degree in real estate** are exempt.
- **Attorneys are not exempt** from post-license (per DBPR), even though Florida Bar members are exempt from CE.

Why "void" and not "inactive"? Think of post-license as the last piece of qualifying. If qualification is never finished, there is no license left to put on hold.

### Stage 2: continuing education (every later renewal)

- **14 hours** (50-minute hours) in each 2-year renewal period, starting **after** the first renewal (s. 475.182(1); Rule 61J2-3.009).
- Required split: **3 hours Core Law + 3 hours Ethics and Business Practices + 8 hours specialty**. This is mandatory for licenses expiring on or after March 31, 2025, and Core Law must be taken at least once each renewal period.
- Applies to **active and inactive** licensees. Approved distance learning is allowed.
- Florida Bar members in good standing are exempt from CE.
- Once per renewal cycle, attending one FREC legal agenda session can substitute for 3 hours. This requires 7 days' notice and does not apply if you attend as a party to a disciplinary case.

### Missing a renewal: involuntary inactive (s. 475.183; verified, V-11)

If a license is not renewed on time, for example because CE was not finished, it becomes **involuntary inactive**. The license still exists, but a clock starts:

| Time involuntarily inactive | To reactivate |
|---|---|
| 12 months or less | **14 hours** of commission-prescribed CE |
| More than 12 but less than 24 months | **28-hour** commission-prescribed reactivation course |
| More than 24 months | License **expires** (null and void) |

### The status map

| Status | How it happens | May the person work? | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Active** | Registered with an employing broker (a broker: with an office) | Yes | Renew every 2 years |
| **Voluntary inactive** | The licensee chooses it | No | Renewable indefinitely with 14 hours of CE per cycle |
| **Involuntary inactive** | Failure to renew | No | 2-year countdown (table above) |
| **Suspended** | Discipline: up to **10 years** | No | The license exists but cannot be used |
| **Revoked** | Discipline | No | Permanent cancellation (narrow exceptions) |
| **Null and void** | Post-license missed; involuntary inactive over 24 months | No | The license no longer exists; requalify |
| **Ineffective** | License exists but cannot be used, e.g., associate with no employer | No | Register with an employer (L03-4) |

## 4. Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Renewal period | Time between license expirations: 18–24 months the first time, then 24 months |
| Post-license education | 45 hours (sales associate) due before the first renewal |
| Continuing education (CE) | 14 hours per renewal period after the first: 3 Core Law, 3 Ethics, 8 specialty |
| Core Law | The required 3-hour Core Law part of CE, taken at least once each renewal period |

## 5. Practical example

**Sofia** and **Lucas** are licensed as sales associates in Jacksonville in May 2024. Their first expiration is **March 31, 2026**: the only March 31 or September 30 date that falls 18–24 months later (about 22 months).

| Event | Rule | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Sofia finishes her 45 hours in January 2026 and renews | Post-license done before the 1st renewal | Renewed; next expiration March 31, 2028 |
| Lucas has not finished his 45 hours on March 31, 2026 (no degree, no hardship extension) | Post-license missed | **Null and void**: 63-hour course and state exam again |
| Sofia forgets her CE and does not renew by March 31, 2028 | Failure to renew | **Involuntary inactive** |
| …she reactivates in January 2029 (about 10 months) | 12 months or less | **14 hours** of CE |
| …or in October 2029 (about 18 months) | More than 12 but less than 24 months | **28-hour** reactivation course |
| …or does nothing until after March 31, 2030 | More than 24 months | **Null and void** |

Sofia's 14 hours for 2028 must include **3 Core Law + 3 Ethics and Business Practices + 8 specialty**.

## 6. Portugal comparison

**Familiar principle.** In Portugal, IMPIC's listed requirements for a mediation license are commercial good standing (*idoneidade comercial*), civil liability insurance (or an equivalent guarantee) and fees. No state exam or minimum course hours appear.

**Florida rule.** Florida adds an education pathway with hard deadlines: 63 hours to enter, 45 hours before the first renewal, and 14 hours every 2 years after that.

**Precise difference.** In Florida, missing an education deadline can **void** a license. Whether Portugal has any comparable renewal or continuing-education duty *requires verification* (our comparison data does not cover it).

**Exam implication.** Never assume a license simply continues. Know **63 / 45 / 14** and which miss voids (post-license) and which inactivates (CE).

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

**Memorize exactly**

- First renewal: **18–24 months**; later periods **24 months**; licenses expire **March 31 or September 30**.
- Post-license: **45 hours** before the first renewal → missed = **null and void** → 63-hour course + state exam (s. 475.17(3)).
- Absences over **10%** → no post-license completion notice. Individual physical hardship: the FREC may allow **6 extra months**. Exempt: **4-year real estate degree**. Attorneys **not** exempt.
- CE: **14 hours** per 2-year period after the first renewal: **3 Core Law + 3 Ethics + 8 specialty** (licenses expiring on or after March 31, 2025). Active **and** inactive licensees. Florida Bar members exempt (s. 475.182; Rule 61J2-3.009).
- Missed renewal → **involuntary inactive**. Reactivation: **≤ 12 months → 14 hours**; **more than 12 but less than 24 months → 28-hour course**; **more than 24 months → void** (s. 475.183; Rule 61J2-3.010).
- Suspension: up to **10 years** (s. 475.25(1)).
- Voluntary inactive renewable indefinitely (manual; not yet verified). Its CE duty is confirmed by Rule 61J2-3.009.
- Revocation is permanent with narrow exceptions (manual; not yet verified). "Ineffective" is a course and exam term (manual; not yet verified).

**Understand**

- Older materials describe CE only as "14 hours, including core law". The **3 + 3 + 8** split is the current requirement.
- The study manual mentions only "late CE" for reactivation. The **28-hour course** is a verified addition from s. 475.183.
- The 18–24-month first period comes from an official DBPR sheet (2018). Nothing current contradicts it, but no current-year restatement was found, and the rule citation the manual gives (61J2-1.008 or 61J2-3.020) is unverified.

## 9. Common misconceptions

- **Swapping the two deadlines.** Post-license missed → **void**. CE or renewal missed → **involuntary inactive**.
- **"Involuntary inactive can be fixed any time."** Only for 24 months; after that the license is void.
- **"Attorneys are exempt from post-license, like CE."** Florida Bar members are exempt from CE, but **not** from post-license.
- **"CE is due at the first renewal too."** The first renewal requires post-license; CE starts after it.
- **"Voluntary inactive licensees need no CE."** They complete 14 hours per cycle.
- **"Any 14 hours will do."** It must be 3 Core Law + 3 Ethics + 8 specialty.
- **"A suspended license no longer exists."** It exists but cannot be used. Revoked and null-and-void licenses are the ones that are gone.

## 10. Memory sheet

- **63 to get in · 45 to stay in · 14 to keep going.**

- First renewal **18–24 months**; then every **24**. Expiry **Mar 31 / Sep 30**.
- CE = **3 Core + 3 Ethics + 8 specialty**.
- Involuntary inactive: **≤ 1 year → 14 · 1–2 years → 28 · past 2 years → dead.**
- Florida Bar members: no CE, **but post-license yes**. 4-year real estate degree: no post-license.

## 2. Why it matters

The DBPR and the FREC can reach you only through the contact details on record, and they expect you to report some events yourself. This lesson gives you the clocks: **10 days** for a change of mailing or e-mail address (and for a change of business address or employing broker), **30 days** to report a criminal conviction, finding of guilt or plea, and **60 days** when a Florida resident becomes a nonresident.

Exam questions test 10 versus 30, the *nolo contendere* and "adjudication withheld" traps, and the classic scenario of an associate who keeps showing homes between brokers. In practice, changing brokers is common, and doing it wrong means working without an effective license.

## 3. Simple explanation

### 1. Keep your contact details current

- Under **s. 455.275(1)**, each licensee is **solely responsible** for notifying the DBPR in writing of their current **mailing address**, **e-mail address** and **place of practice**. Failing to report a change is a violation.
- **Rule 61J2-10.038** sets the deadline: notify the Department in writing of any change to your mailing or e-mail address **within 10 days after the change**. A **first** violation gets a **citation** (a lighter penalty notice instead of a full disciplinary case). **Repeat** violations lead to disciplinary proceedings.
- **s. 475.23** separately requires notice within **10 days** of a change of **business address or employing broker**, on a Commission form.
- **Moving out of Florida** does not end the license (manual; not yet verified). A licensee who changes from Florida resident to **nonresident** must notify the Commission within **60 days**, and the new mailing address is still due within 10 days.

Why so strict? The DBPR can contact you only through the details on record, and the law puts the burden of keeping them current on the licensee alone.

### 2. Report crimes yourself

- **s. 455.227(1)(t)**, which covers every DBPR licensee: report **in writing to the FREC within 30 days** after being **convicted or found guilty** of, or **pleading guilty or nolo contendere** to, **a crime in any jurisdiction**, **regardless of adjudication**.
- **s. 475.25(1)(p)**, the real estate law's own version, is narrower: it covers **felonies** only. Because licensees are bound by both, the rule to learn is the broader one: **any crime**.
- ***Nolo contendere*** means "no contest". The defendant does not admit guilt but accepts the penalty.
- **Adjudication withheld** means the court accepts the plea or finding of guilt but does not formally declare the person convicted. The report is still required.
- The 30 days run from the **plea, finding of guilt or conviction**, not from the arrest.

Why? The FREC judges honesty and trustworthiness throughout a career, not only on the application (L02-3).

### 3. One license, one employer

A sales associate may hold only **one** license and work for only **one** broker or owner-employer at a time. A group license lets a sales associate work for several connected entities of one owner-developer; it never allows working for an unrelated broker at the same time (L03-2).

### 4. Changing brokers

- When employment ends, the associate's license cannot be used until the **new employer's registration is filed**. In the gap, it is **ineffective**.
- Showing listings, listing or negotiating in the gap is **operating without an effective license**.
- The broker must notify the DBPR when an associate leaves.
- The 10-day notice in s. 475.23 is a **reporting deadline**, not a 10-day permission to work unregistered.

## 4. Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Mailing address | The address on DBPR records where official notices are sent |
| Place of practice | Where the licensee practices; it must be on record with the DBPR |
| Nolo contendere | "No contest" plea: no admission of guilt, but the penalty is accepted |
| Adjudication withheld | The court accepts the plea or finding but does not formally declare a conviction |
| Found guilty | A court or jury decides the person committed the crime |
| Employing broker | The broker a sales associate is registered under |
| Ineffective license | A license that exists but cannot be used, e.g., between employers |
| Citation | A lighter penalty notice; used for a first address-rule violation instead of disciplinary proceedings |
| Nonresident licensee | A Florida licensee who does not live in Florida |

## 5. Practical example

**Ana**, a sales associate from Porto, works for Brickell Bay Realty in Miami.

| Date | Event | Duty | Deadline |
|---|---|---|---|
| June 3 | Moves apartments and changes her e-mail | Written notice to the DBPR of the new mailing and e-mail address | **June 13** (10 days) |
| August 1 | Pleads *nolo contendere* to a misdemeanor in Georgia; adjudication withheld | Written report to the FREC (any crime, any jurisdiction, regardless of adjudication) | **August 31** (30 days) |
| September 15 | Leaves Brickell Bay for Coral Gate Realty | Change of employing broker on the Commission form | **September 25** (10 days), but **no work** until Coral Gate's registration is filed |
| The next year | Moves to Georgia and becomes a nonresident | Notify the Commission of nonresidency; update the mailing address | **60 days**; 10 days |

On September 16, before Coral Gate's registration is filed, Ana holds an open house for a former client. Her license still exists, but it is **ineffective**: she is operating without an effective license. The 10-day notice period does not protect her.

## 6. Portugal comparison

**Familiar principle.** In Portugal, IMPIC requires commercial good standing (*idoneidade comercial*) as a condition of the mediation business's license. Integrity matters in both systems.

**Florida rule.** Florida makes integrity and contact details a **personal, continuing** duty of every licensee: crimes reported within 30 days, address and e-mail within 10 days, change of employer on record before working.

**Precise difference.** Whether Portuguese law requires a consultant or agency to report address changes or criminal matters to IMPIC, and on what deadline, *requires verification*; our comparison data does not cover it. In Portugal, consultants usually work under the agency's license (*requires verification*), so moving agencies may feel like a private matter. In Florida, changing brokers is a **registration event with the state**.

**Exam implication.** Answer only with the Florida clocks: 10, 30 and 60 days.

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

**Memorize exactly**

- Mailing or e-mail address change: written notice to the DBPR within **10 days** (Rule 61J2-10.038; s. 455.275(1)). First violation → citation; repeat → discipline.
- Change of business address or employing broker: **10 days**, on a Commission form (s. 475.23).
- Florida resident becomes nonresident: notify the Commission within **60 days** (V-34; no statute section given in our sources).
- Conviction, finding of guilt, or guilty or *nolo contendere* plea to **any crime in any jurisdiction**: written report to the FREC within **30 days**, **regardless of adjudication** (s. 455.227(1)(t)). Chapter 475's own rule covers felonies (s. 475.25(1)(p)).
- One license, one employer at a time for sales associates (manual; not yet verified).
- No services between brokers until the new employer's registration is filed; the broker notifies the DBPR when an associate leaves (manual; not yet verified). Do not read the 10-day notice period as permission to work in the gap.
- The license survives a move out of state (manual; not yet verified).

**Understand**

- The 10-day address deadline is confirmed in the 2020 rule text. The rule was amended again effective 7/18/2024, and that text was not read directly, so the continuing 10-day period is inferred.
- According to the DBPR annual report, that 2024 amendment changed the definition of "current mailing address" by removing "residential", so the brokerage office's physical location can serve. The amended text itself was not read. Older materials that require a **residential** address are likely outdated.
- "Any crime, regardless of adjudication" comes from s. 455.227(1)(t). Chapter 475 alone would require reporting only felonies, but licensees are bound by both.

## 9. Common misconceptions

- **"Nolo contendere is not a conviction, so there is nothing to report."** It must be reported within 30 days.
- **"Adjudication was withheld, so it doesn't count."** The duty applies regardless of adjudication.
- **"Only felonies must be reported."** Under s. 455.227(1)(t), any crime must be reported.
- **"A crime in another state doesn't count."** Any jurisdiction counts.
- **"The 30 days start at the arrest."** They start at the plea, finding of guilt or conviction.
- **"My license is current, so I can keep working between brokers."** It is ineffective until the new registration is filed.
- **"The 10-day notice gives me 10 days to work for the new broker unregistered."** It is a reporting deadline only.
- **"Only my postal address matters."** E-mail address and place of practice must be on record too.

## 10. Memory sheet

- **Address or e-mail = 10. Employer or business address = 10. Become a nonresident = 60.**
- **Crime = 30:** conviction, finding of guilt, guilty or *nolo* plea, **any crime, anywhere, adjudication or not**, in writing to the FREC.
- *Nolo* = report. Withheld = report.
- **No employer = no work.** One license, one employer.
- First address violation = citation; repeat = discipline.
