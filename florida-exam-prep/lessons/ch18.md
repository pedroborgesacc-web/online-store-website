## 3. Simple explanation

Florida property taxes are **ad valorem** (by value) and fund local government; there is no state property tax. The county **property appraiser** assesses each parcel at **just value** as of **January 1**. In **August** the **TRIM** (Truth in Millage) notice shows proposed values and rates. An owner who disagrees can talk informally with the appraiser and petition the **Value Adjustment Board (VAB)**. Bills are mailed from **November 1** with early-payment discounts of **4% (Nov), 3% (Dec), 2% (Jan), 1% (Feb)**; taxes become **delinquent April 1** and are enforced through **tax certificates** and, eventually, a tax deed sale.

**Special assessments** are not ad valorem: charges on properties specially benefited by an improvement (sewer, paving), often per front foot, with lien priority like property taxes.

**Watch item (V-23):** a homestead property-tax amendment (CS/HJR 1-F) is on the November 2026 ballot. It is not law unless approved, and exam questions usually lag law changes.

## 10. Memory sheet

Jan 1 assess · Aug TRIM · Nov bills (4-3-2-1%) · Apr 1 delinquent. Special assessments ≠ ad valorem.

## 3. Simple explanation

**Taxable value = assessed value − exemptions.**

- **Homestead:** first **$25,000** of assessed value exempt from **all** taxes; an **additional exemption** of up to **$25,000** on assessed value above **$50,000**, for **non-school** taxes only. Since 2025 the additional exemption is **indexed to CPI** ($25,722 for 2025, V-09). Exam math usually uses $25,000.
- **$5,000** each for a widow/widower, blind person, or totally and permanently disabled person (V-10; older books say $500).
- Disabled veterans: larger or total exemptions (details not covered).
- **Green Belt:** agricultural land assessed on use value.

**Save Our Homes:** a homestead's assessed value can rise no more than **3% per year or the CPI change, whichever is less**; the accumulated benefit is **portable** to a new Florida homestead (limits apply). A sale resets assessed value to just value.

**Computing tax:** tax = taxable value × mills ÷ 1,000 (1 mill = $1 per $1,000 = 0.001). Run school and non-school taxable values separately.

Worked example (manual): assessed $310,000, homestead. School: 310,000 − 25,000 = 285,000 × 6 mills = **$1,710**. Non-school: 310,000 − 50,000 = 260,000 × 9 mills = **$2,340**. Total **$4,050**.

## 10. Memory sheet

25k all taxes + 25k (CPI-indexed) non-school above 50k. 5k widow/blind/disabled. SOH 3% or CPI. Mills ÷ 1,000.

## 3. Simple explanation

**Depreciation (cost recovery):** straight-line over **27.5 years** (residential rental) or **39 years** (nonresidential); buildings only, never land.

**Basis:** cost; **adjusted basis** = cost + improvements − depreciation. **Capital gain** = amount realized − adjusted basis. Depreciation lowers basis, so gain grows; depreciation taken is **recaptured** at sale.

**1031 like-kind exchange:** defers tax on investment or business real property exchanged for like-kind property. **Boot** (cash, debt relief, personal property) is taxable to the extent of gain, but does not disqualify the exchange.

**Principal residence exclusion:** **$250,000** single / **$500,000** married filing jointly, if owned and used as the main home **2 of the last 5 years**.

**Installment sale:** the seller recognizes gain as payments are received, spreading the tax.

## 10. Memory sheet

27.5 / 39 · land never. 1031 = investment, boot taxed. Home = 250/500, 2 of 5.
