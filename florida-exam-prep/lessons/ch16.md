## 3. Simple explanation

**Elements of value — DUST:** Demand, Utility, Scarcity, Transferability.

**Market value** is the most probable price in an arm's-length sale between informed parties under no unusual pressure. It differs from **price** (what was paid) and **cost** (what it took to build). Others: assessed value (taxes), insurable value.

**Principles:** **substitution** (no one pays more than an equal substitute costs — the foundation of appraisal); **highest and best use** (legally permissible, physically possible, financially feasible, maximally productive); **anticipation** (value of expected future benefits — basis of the income approach); **contribution** (a feature is worth what it adds, not what it cost); **conformity** with **progression** (modest house lifted by better neighbours) and **regression** (mansion dragged down by lesser ones); **plottage** (value gain from combining parcels; the act is **assemblage**); increasing and decreasing returns.

## 10. Memory sheet

DUST. Substitution underlies everything. Progression lifts, regression drags.

## 3. Simple explanation

The **sales comparison approach** is best for homes and vacant land. Choose recent comparable sales and **adjust the comparables, never the subject**: comparable better → **subtract**; comparable inferior → **add** (CBS / CIA). The comparable with the fewest and smallest adjustments carries the most weight.

Worked example (manual): comp sold $410,000; it has a pool worth $25,000 the subject lacks (−$25,000); the subject has a renovated kitchen worth $15,000 the comp lacks (+$15,000) → indicated value **$400,000**.

## 10. Memory sheet

Adjust the comp. Better → subtract. Inferior → add.

## 3. Simple explanation

The **cost approach** is best for new construction and special-purpose properties (schools, churches, fire stations): **Value = land (by sales comparison) + cost new − accrued depreciation**. **Reproduction** cost = exact replica; **replacement** cost = equal utility with current materials and standards. Land never depreciates.

**Depreciation:** **physical deterioration** (wear; curable or incurable), **functional obsolescence** (outdated design — one bathroom, no closets; curable or incurable), **external (economic) obsolescence** (outside the property line — an airport, the economy; **always incurable**).

## 10. Memory sheet

Land + cost new − depreciation. External = outside = incurable.

## 3. Simple explanation

The **income capitalization approach** is for income property: **IRV — Value = NOI ÷ cap rate**.

Build NOI: potential gross income − vacancy and collection loss = effective gross income; − operating expenses (fixed, variable, reserves) = **NOI**. **Debt service and depreciation are not operating expenses.**

Shortcuts: **GRM** = price ÷ **monthly** gross rent (houses, duplexes); **GIM** = price ÷ **annual** gross income.

**Reconciliation:** the appraiser weighs the approaches by their reliability for the property — never a simple average. **USPAP** governs appraisal practice.

**Who may appraise:** federally related transactions require a state-licensed or certified appraiser. A real estate licensee may prepare CMAs/BPOs and charge for them, but may never label them appraisals; per the manual, a licensee doing appraisal work outside federally related transactions must still follow USPAP.

## 10. Memory sheet

IRV. NOI excludes debt service and depreciation. GRM monthly, GIM annual. Reconcile, don't average.
