## 3. Simple explanation

Title transfers **voluntarily** by deed (sale or gift) or will, and **involuntarily** by descent (intestate succession), **escheat** (no will and no heirs → state), **eminent domain** (government taking with just compensation), foreclosure, and **adverse possession** — in Florida, **7 years** of open, notorious, hostile, continuous possession **plus** color of title or payment of taxes (manual; not verified).

Title passes by **delivery and acceptance of a deed during the grantor's lifetime** — not by signing, not by recording.

**Wills:** dying **testate** (real property passes by **devise** to devisees) or **intestate** (Florida's statute of descent distributes to heirs). Probate proves the will and clears title. Homestead devise restrictions override the will. A will takes effect only at death and can be revoked any time before.

## 10. Memory sheet

7 years + color of title or taxes. Devise = will; descent = no will; escheat = no heirs.

## 3. Simple explanation

**Deeds from most to least protection:**

- **General warranty** — warrants title against defects arising at any time. Covenants: seisin, quiet enjoyment, against encumbrances, further assurance, warranty forever.
- **Special warranty** — only against defects arising during the grantor's ownership.
- **Bargain and sale** — implies ownership; no warranties.
- **Quitclaim** — conveys whatever interest the grantor has; no warranties; cures a **cloud on title**.

**A valid Florida deed requires:** a written instrument; a **competent grantor** (grantee need only be identifiable and may be a minor); consideration (nominal is enough); words of conveyance (granting clause); a legal description; the **grantor's signature before two witnesses** (s. 689.01, V-05); **delivery and acceptance**. The grantee does not sign. A notary **acknowledgment** is required for **recording**, not validity.

## 10. Memory sheet

General > special > bargain & sale > quitclaim. Two witnesses. Delivery makes it valid; recording warns the world.

## 3. Simple explanation

**Constructive notice:** what the public records (and visible possession) impart to everyone. **Actual notice:** what a person actually knows. Florida protects a good-faith purchaser who records without notice of prior claims.

**Title evidence:** an **abstract of title with an attorney's opinion**, or **title insurance** (the modern standard). **Owner's policy:** issued for the purchase price, protects the owner while she owns, not transferable. **Lender's policy:** for the loan amount, declines with the balance, follows the loan. Title insurance covers defects existing **before** the policy date — never defects the owner creates later.

## 3. Simple explanation

**Liens** are money claims against property. *Specific* liens attach to one property: property tax and special assessment liens (priority over all others regardless of date), mortgages, vendor's liens, construction liens (relate back to the notice of commencement). *General* liens attach to all the debtor's property: judgment liens, IRS liens, estate liens. Otherwise priority follows recording date (first in time, first in right), unless a lienholder agrees to **subordination**.

**Easements** — rights to use another's land: *appurtenant* (benefits an adjoining parcel: dominant estate benefited, servient estate burdened; runs with the land); *in gross* (benefits a person or company, e.g., utilities); *by necessity* (landlocked parcel); *by prescription* (**20 years** of open, continuous, adverse use in Florida — manual). A **license** is revocable permission, not an interest in land. An **encroachment** is a physical intrusion (a fence over the line), revealed by a **survey**. A **deed restriction** (restrictive covenant) is a private limitation binding future owners.

## 10. Memory sheet

Taxes first, then recording order. Appurtenant = parcel to parcel; in gross = to a person/company. Prescription 20 years.

## 3. Simple explanation

**Fixtures:** personal property that becomes real property when attached. Tests: **intent** (dominant), method of annexation, adaptation to the realty, relationship of the parties. **Trade fixtures** installed by a business tenant remain the tenant's personal property and must be removed by lease end (if left, they become the landlord's by accession). **Emblements** (annual crops) are personal property; a tenant farmer may harvest crops planted before the tenancy ended.

**Water rights:** **riparian** (rivers and streams); **littoral** (oceans, seas, large lakes). Florida owns the land seaward of the mean high-water line. **Accretion** (gradual deposit, owner gains), **erosion** (gradual loss), **avulsion** (sudden change — boundaries do not move).

## 10. Memory sheet

Intent wins. Trade fixtures stay with the tenant. Gradual moves boundaries; sudden does not.
