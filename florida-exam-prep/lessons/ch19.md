## 3. Simple explanation

Land-use control is **police power** — regulation for health, safety and welfare, with **no compensation** owed. **Eminent domain** is the government **taking** property for public use through condemnation, requiring **just compensation**. When regulation destroys all value, the owner may sue for **inverse condemnation**.

Every Florida local government has a **comprehensive plan**; **zoning** implements it (residential, commercial, industrial, agricultural, special districts; **buffer zones** ease transitions).

**Relief and special cases:**

- **Variance** — permission to deviate from a dimensional rule (setback, height) because of hardship unique to the parcel.
- **Special exception / conditional use** — a use the code permits with case-by-case approval (school, church, daycare).
- **Nonconforming use** — lawful before the code changed ("grandfathered"); may continue but usually not expand; if destroyed or abandoned, may not resume.
- **Rezoning** — changing the district itself.
- **PUD** (planned unit development) — mixed uses with clustered density traded for open space.

**Construction control:** building permits, inspections, and a **certificate of occupancy** before use. Florida contractors are licensed by the CILB (manual).

## 10. Memory sheet

Regulate (police power) = no pay. Take (eminent domain) = just compensation. Hardship + dimensions = variance; permitted-with-approval use = special exception.

## 3. Simple explanation

- **Lead-based paint** (federal): housing built **before 1978** — sellers and landlords disclose known hazards, give the EPA pamphlet, and buyers get a **10-day** inspection opportunity (may be waived). Licensees share responsibility for compliance.
- **Radon** (Florida): a statutory radon notice appears in every **sale and lease** contract.
- **Asbestos** (insulation, pre-1980s): dangerous when **friable**; encapsulation is often safer than removal.
- **CERCLA/Superfund:** strict, joint and several liability for cleanup — an owner can be liable without causing the contamination; the innocent-landowner defense requires due diligence (Phase I environmental audit).
- Other terms: **brownfields** (redeveloped contaminated sites), **wetlands** permitting, **EIS** (environmental impact statement for major federal actions), **underground storage tanks** (e.g., near gas stations).

## 10. Memory sheet

1978 = lead paint (not 1988, the fair housing year). Radon notice in every sale and lease. CERCLA = owner liable without fault.
