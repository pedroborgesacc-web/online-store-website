## 3. Simple explanation

**Civil Rights Act of 1866:** prohibits **racial** discrimination in the sale or rental of **all** property, real or personal, with **no exceptions**; enforced by suit in federal court.

**Fair Housing Act (1968, Title VIII):** race, color, religion, national origin. **1974** added **sex**. **1988** added **familial status** (children under 18, pregnant women, people obtaining custody) and **handicap** (disability). Bona fide **housing for older persons** may exclude children: 55+ communities with at least 80% of units occupied by at least one person 55+, or communities where all occupants are 62+.

**Prohibited practices:** refusing to sell or rent; different terms; discriminatory advertising; false statements about availability; **blockbusting** (panic peddling: inducing owners to sell by suggesting a protected class is moving in); **steering** (directing buyers toward or away from areas by protected class); **redlining** (lenders refusing or varying loans by area).

**Disability:** landlords must allow **reasonable modifications** (physical changes, generally at the tenant's expense) and make **reasonable accommodations** in rules (e.g., assistance animals despite a no-pets rule).

**Exemptions (never for race):** an owner of no more than 3 single-family homes selling or renting without a broker and without discriminatory ads; the **"Mrs. Murphy"** owner-occupied building of no more than 4 units; religious organizations and private clubs limiting non-commercial housing to members. Using a **broker** or a **discriminatory ad** ends the exemption.

**Enforcement:** complaints to **HUD within 1 year** (manual) or a court action. Brokerage offices display the equal housing opportunity poster. Florida also has its own Fair Housing Act (Ch. 760, details not verified this session).

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

1866 race (no exceptions) · 1968 · 1974 sex · 1988 familial status + handicap · 55+/80% or 62+ · ≤3 homes · ≤4 units owner-occupied.

## 9. Common misconceptions

- Mixing 1978 (lead paint) with 1988 (fair housing amendments).
- Believing an exemption survives if a broker is used.

## 10. Memory sheet

Race = no exemptions. Steer buyers · block(bust) sellers · red(line) loans. Modification = physical; accommodation = rules.

## 3. Simple explanation

- **ADA** (Americans with Disabilities Act): accessibility of **public accommodations** and commercial facilities (including brokerage offices); barriers removed where readily achievable. Governs business premises, not private homes.
- **ECOA** (Equal Credit Opportunity Act): no credit discrimination by race, color, religion, national origin, sex, **marital status, age, or receipt of public assistance income**.
- **HMDA** (Home Mortgage Disclosure Act): lenders report lending data, which exposes redlining.
- **CFPB** administers the TRID disclosure rules.

**Marketing rules:**

- **Do Not Call:** numbers on the national (and Florida) registry may not be cold-called, unless there is **written permission** or an **established business relationship** — up to **18 months** after a purchase/transaction, up to **3 months** after an inquiry. A request to stop calling overrides these.
- **CAN-SPAM** (commercial email): truthful headers and subject lines, identification as an ad where required, a working opt-out honored within **10 business days**, a physical postal address.
- **Junk Fax Prevention Act:** no unsolicited fax ads without an established business relationship.

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

DNC 18 months / 3 months · CAN-SPAM 10 business days · ECOA classes.

## 9. Common misconceptions

- "A wheelchair ramp at the office is a fair housing issue." It is ADA.
- "Another brokerage's expired listing is fair game for cold calls." No relationship exists.

## 10. Memory sheet

Housing = FHA · credit = ECOA · public accommodation = ADA · data = HMDA. DNC: 18 after a deal, 3 after a question.

## 3. Simple explanation

**RESPA** applies to **federally related loans on 1–4 unit residential property**. It prohibits **kickbacks and unearned fees** for referrals of settlement services (title, inspection, lending), and prohibits sellers from requiring a particular title company. **TRID** (TILA-RESPA Integrated Disclosures): the **Loan Estimate** within **3 business days** of application; the **Closing Disclosure** at least **3 business days before closing**.

**TILA / Regulation Z** requires disclosure of the cost of credit, headlined by the **APR** (rate plus certain costs — compare loans by APR). **Trigger terms** in ads (a down-payment amount, a payment amount, the number of payments, the finance charge) require full disclosure of credit terms; "low monthly payments" alone is not a trigger term. **Right of rescission:** **3 business days** for refinances and home-equity loans on a **principal residence** — not purchase-money loans, not investment property.

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

1–4 units · LE 3 business days after application · CD 3 business days before closing · rescission 3 business days (refi/home equity, primary home).

## 9. Common misconceptions

- "A buyer can rescind her purchase mortgage within 3 days." Not for purchase loans.
- "Disclosure makes a settlement-service kickback legal under RESPA." RESPA prohibits it.

## 10. Memory sheet

3 after applying · 3 before closing · 3 to rescind a refi. Specific numbers in ads trigger disclosure.

## 3. Simple explanation

**Florida Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (Chapter 83).**

**Security deposits:** held in a separate Florida account (interest-bearing or not) or secured by a surety bond; the tenant is told in writing how the deposit is held within **30 days** of receipt.

**After move-out:** return within **15 days** if no claim; otherwise send a **certified-mail notice of intent to impose a claim within 30 days**; the tenant has **15 days to object**. A landlord who misses the 30-day notice **forfeits** the right to impose a claim. In a dispute, the prevailing party recovers attorney's fees.

**Ending tenancies without a specific term (s. 83.57, verified V-04):**

| Tenancy | Written notice before end of period |
|---|---|
| Year to year | at least 60 days |
| Quarter to quarter | at least 30 days |
| Month to month | at least 30 days |
| Week to week | at least 7 days |

**Correction:** the study manual lists 15 days (month) and 45 days (quarter). The current statute says 30 and 30. Older books show 15 days for month-to-month because the law changed; no version with 45 days was found.

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

30-day notice of how held · 15 / 30 / 15 · 60 / 30 / 30 / 7.

## 9. Common misconceptions

- Using the outdated 15-day month-to-month notice.
- Thinking a late notice of claim still lets the landlord deduct.

## 10. Memory sheet

Deposit: 30 to say how held; 15 return · 30 claim · 15 object. Notices: 60 / 30 / 30 / 7.
