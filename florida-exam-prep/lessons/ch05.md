## 3. Simple explanation

**Escrow (trust) funds** are money held for others: deposits, rents, advance fees. A broker may hold them in a bank, credit union or savings association doing business in Florida, or place them with a Florida title company or attorney. A broker is not required to maintain an escrow account, but must handle funds properly either way. An **interest-bearing** account requires the **written consent of all parties**, including who receives the interest.

**The deposit clock (manual; not verified this session):**

- The **sales associate** delivers the deposit to the broker by the **end of the next business day**.
- The **broker** deposits it by the **end of the third business day** after the brokerage receives it. The day of receipt is not counted; weekends and legal holidays never count. "Immediately" in FREC rules means this window.

Worked example: received Thursday → associate delivers by end of Friday → broker deposits by end of Tuesday (Fri 1, Mon 2, Tue 3).

**Broker's own money:** up to **$1,000** in a sales escrow account and **$5,000** in a property-management escrow account, to cover bank charges. More is **commingling**. Using escrow money for personal or business purposes is **conversion** (more serious).

## 6. Portugal comparison

See PT-07: Florida deposits sit in escrow under strict business-day clocks; the Portuguese sinal "return double" rule does not apply.

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

Next business day · 3rd business day · $1,000 / $5,000 · interest-bearing needs all parties' written consent.

## 9. Common misconceptions

- Counting calendar days or the day of receipt.
- Thinking a short "borrowing" of escrow funds is acceptable if repaid.

## 10. Memory sheet

Associate 1 business day, broker 3. Sales 1K, rentals 5K. Mixing = commingling; spending = conversion.

## 3. Simple explanation

When a broker holding escrow funds receives **conflicting demands** (or has a good-faith doubt about who is entitled), Rule 61J2-10.032 (verified V-15) requires:

1. Written notice to the FREC within **15 business days** of the last demand.
2. Instituting a settlement procedure within **30 business days** of the last demand.

**Settlement procedures (M.A.L.E.):**

- **Mediation** — all parties consent in writing; must be completed within **90 days** after the last demand, otherwise the broker promptly uses another procedure.
- **Arbitration** — all parties consent; binding.
- **Litigation** — interpleader: the broker deposits the funds with the court.
- **Escrow Disbursement Order (EDO)** — the FREC decides; available only when the broker holds the funds.

If the matter settles or goes to court before an EDO issues, notify the FREC within **10 business days**. A broker who follows the procedure and the resulting order cannot be disciplined for failure to account. (Manual: if a broker obeys an EDO and is sued, the Recovery Fund may pay the broker's costs.)

**No FREC notice needed (s. 475.25(1)(d)1, verified V-16):** a residential **condominium buyer cancels** under s. 718.503, or a **buyer in good faith fails to satisfy the financing clause** — the broker may return the deposit to the buyer. When a **title company or attorney** holds the deposit, the broker is not the escrow holder and the FREC procedure does not apply. The manual's "HUD sale" exception was not found in the statute and is not taught.

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

15 business days notify · 30 business days institute · mediation 90 days · 10 business days if settled.

## 9. Common misconceptions

- Treating 15/30 as calendar days.
- Requesting an EDO when a title company holds the money.

## 10. Memory sheet

15 to tell, 30 to choose. M.A.L.E. EDO only for broker-held funds. Condo cancellation and good-faith loan denial = return to buyer, no notice.

## 3. Simple explanation

Every advertisement by a licensee must include the **registered name of the brokerage firm**. An ad without it is a **blind ad**, in any medium: yard sign, newspaper, website, Instagram, WhatsApp status. If a sales associate's name appears, it must be the name registered with DBPR. Prices advertised must be those agreed with the seller. On the internet, the firm name must appear on the ad itself, not only on a page one click away. Team names may not suggest the team is an independent brokerage (manual; team-advertising rule details not verified).

An **owner advertising his own property** without a brokerage has no brokerage name to include.

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

Registered brokerage name in every licensee ad. No blind ads.

## 9. Common misconceptions

- "My personal account is exempt." It is not.

## 10. Memory sheet

No firm name = blind ad. Owners selling their own property are not licensee ads.

## 3. Simple explanation

**Offices and signs.** An active broker must maintain a registered office (a stationary location where records are kept) with an entrance sign showing the broker's name, any trade name, and **"Licensed Real Estate Broker"** (or "Lic. Real Estate Broker"). Additional business locations are **branch offices**, each registered.

**Who may be paid.** A sales associate may be paid **only by the employing broker** — never directly by a seller, buyer, another associate or another brokerage. Paying **unlicensed persons** for real estate services is prohibited (a per-closing bonus to an unlicensed assistant is unlawful; a salary for clerical work is fine). A broker may **rebate** part of the commission to a party to the transaction with full disclosure. Commissions are **negotiable**; agreements among competitors on rates are **price fixing** (antitrust).

**Kickbacks.** Between licensees, sharing compensation through brokers is lawful. A kickback from a settlement-service provider is permitted under Chapter 475 only with full disclosure to all parties and if no other law prohibits it — and RESPA usually prohibits it in residential financed transactions.

**Rental lists (s. 475.453, verified V-21).** A licensee who sells rental information for a fee must give a contract or receipt: **75% refund** if the tenant does not obtain a rental (demand within **30 days** of the contract), **full refund** if the information was not current or accurate.

**Associate compensation disputes** are contract matters between associate and broker (not FREC matters, not Recovery Fund claims).

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

"Licensed Real Estate Broker" on the sign · paid only by employing broker · rebates to parties with disclosure · rental list 75% / 100% / 30 days.

## 9. Common misconceptions

- "A gift from a happy seller isn't compensation."
- "A standard commission rate exists."

## 10. Memory sheet

Money flows through the broker. Unlicensed: salary for clerical OK, per-deal pay never. Rental lists: keep 25%.
