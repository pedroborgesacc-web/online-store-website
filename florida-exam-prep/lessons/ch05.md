## 2. Why it matters

An earnest money check handed to you belongs neither to you nor to your broker. Florida treats it as **trust money**. It must reach the right place within a fixed number of **business days**. Apart from a small, capped amount of the broker's own money allowed in the account for bank charges, it must not be mixed with the brokerage's money, and it must never be used as the brokerage's own money. Mixing in more than the allowed amount is a serious violation, and spending escrow money is more serious still.

On the exam, escrow is one of the most predictable topics in Brokerage Activities and Procedures, which carries 12% of the exam (tied for the largest section). Expect a weekday-to-deadline question, and a question on how much of the broker's own money may sit in escrow.

## 3. Simple explanation

### What escrow money is

**Escrow (trust) funds** are money a broker holds **for other people**: a buyer's **earnest money (binder) deposit**, rents collected for a landlord, advance fees. The broker looks after this money but does not own it.

### Who holds it, and where

- A **sales associate does not hold escrow**. Her job is to pass the deposit to her broker (or employer) quickly.
- The **broker** may keep escrow funds in an escrow account at a **bank, credit union or savings association doing business in Florida**, or place them with a **Florida title company or attorney** (manual; not yet verified).
- When a title company or attorney holds the deposit, the contract names it, and the broker still has duties (see below; V-39).
- A broker is **not required to maintain an escrow account** at all, but must handle funds properly and keep records either way (manual; not yet verified).
- An **interest-bearing** escrow account is allowed only with the **written consent of all parties**, which also states who receives the interest (manual; not yet verified).

### The deposit clock

A **business day** is any day except **Saturday, Sunday and legal holidays**. The day the deposit arrives is **day 0**, and counting starts on the next business day.

| Who | Deadline | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Sales associate → broker (or employer) | End of the **next business day** after receipt | Rule 61J2-14.009, F.A.C. (verified, V-36) |
| Broker → escrow account | End of the **third business day** after receipt. FREC rules call this "**immediately**" | Rule 61J2-14.008, F.A.C. (verified, V-37) |

**Both clocks start on the same day.** The course counts the broker's three days from the day the deposit first came into the brokerage, which is the day the associate received it (the manual's worked example). The hand-off to the broker does not restart the clock. If the associate uses her full day, the broker has two business days left.

**Why so short?** A check in a handbag or a car can be lost or misused, and it is the broker who must account for escrow money. The rules move it quickly to the broker, then into a protected account.

### When a title company or attorney holds the deposit

The broker is **not** the escrow holder, but still has duties under Rule 61J2-14.008(2)(b), F.A.C. (corrected, V-39):

1. The contract names the holder: its **name, address and telephone number**.
2. Within **10 business days after each deposit is due**, the broker sends the holder a **written request** for written verification that the deposit was received. *This step is not required if the seller or the seller's agent nominated the title company or attorney in writing.*
3. Within **10 business days of that request**, the broker gives the **seller's broker** (or the seller, if unrepresented) a copy of the verification, or a notice that none was received.

### The broker's own money: commingling and conversion

A broker may keep a small amount of **personal or brokerage funds** in escrow. The manual explains that this covers bank charges. The limits are in Rule 61J2-14.010(2), F.A.C. and s. 475.25(1)(k), F.S. (verified, V-38):

- up to **$1,000** per **sales** escrow account;
- up to **$5,000** per **property management** escrow account.

Keeping more than that is **commingling**: mixing personal money with trust money. **Conversion** is worse. It means using escrow money for personal or business purposes, even "just until next week".

### Records

Whether or not the broker keeps an escrow account, the broker must keep records of the funds handled (manual; not yet verified). Brokerage records are kept for at least **5 years** (s. 475.5015, F.S.; V-40; see L06-2).

## 5. Practical example

**Sofia** is a sales associate at a Coral Gables brokerage. At a showing on **Thursday** afternoon, a buyer gives her a **$15,000** earnest money check. There are no legal holidays this week.

| Step | Rule | Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| Sofia delivers the check to her broker | Next business day | **End of Friday** |
| The broker deposits it in the sales escrow account | 3rd business day after receipt: Fri (1), Mon (2), Tue (3) | **End of Tuesday** |

Variations:

- **Monday is a legal holiday.** Sofia's deadline is still Friday. The broker counts Fri (1), Tue (2), Wed (3), so the deadline is the **end of Wednesday**.
- **The check is handed over at a Saturday open house.** Saturday is day 0 and Sunday does not count. Sofia's deadline is the **end of Monday**. The broker counts Mon (1), Tue (2), Wed (3), so the deadline is the **end of Wednesday**.
- **The buyer chose a title company to hold the deposit**, due Friday. Within 10 business days after Friday, the broker asks the title company in writing to verify receipt. Within 10 business days of that request, the broker sends the seller's broker the verification, or a notice that none arrived. If the seller had nominated the title company in writing, the request would not be required.
- **Bank fees.** The broker keeps $800 of brokerage money in the sales escrow account: allowed. Keeping $1,800 is commingling. Paying the office rent from escrow and "putting it back next week" is conversion.

## 6. Portugal comparison

**Familiar principle.** In Portugal, a deposit paid on a contrato-promessa is presumed to be **sinal**. Under Código Civil art. 442, if the payer defaults, the other party keeps it; if the recipient defaults, the payer can demand double. Who holds the money in practice *requires verification*.

**Florida rule.** Earnest money is normally held in **escrow** by the broker, a title company or an attorney, under strict business-day clocks. Contracts often treat the deposit as liquidated damages if the buyer defaults, and disputes go through the conflicting-demand procedures (L05-2).

**The difference.** Florida exam content has no automatic "return double" rule. The skills tested are about handling the money: who holds it, how fast it moves, and what must never be mixed with it.

**Exam implication.** Count **business** days, and never use the sinal rules to answer a Florida question.

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

**Memorize exactly**

- Sales associate → broker (or employer): by the **end of the next business day** after receipt (Rule 61J2-14.009; V-36).
- Broker → escrow account ("immediately"): by the **end of the third business day** after receipt (Rule 61J2-14.008; V-37).
- Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays are not business days. The day of receipt is not counted.
- Broker's own funds: up to **$1,000 per sales escrow account** and up to **$5,000 per property management escrow account** (Rule 61J2-14.010(2); s. 475.25(1)(k); V-38).
- Title company or attorney holds the deposit (Rule 61J2-14.008(2)(b); V-39): holder's name, address and phone on the contract; written verification request within **10 business days after each deposit is due** (not required if the seller or seller's agent nominated the holder in writing); copy or "none received" notice to the **seller's broker** (or an unrepresented seller) within **10 business days of the request**.
- Interest-bearing escrow account: **written consent of all parties**, including who receives the interest (manual; not yet verified).
- Places to hold escrow: a bank, credit union or savings association doing business in Florida, or a Florida title company or attorney. A broker need not maintain an escrow account (manual; not yet verified).

**Understand**

- **Corrected manual statement.** The manual's single step ("give the holder's statement to the seller within 10 business days") was corrected (V-39). There are two 10-business-day windows, and the copy goes to the seller's broker (see Common misconceptions).
- One scraped copy of the rule had a general "$5,000 per account" sentence. The account-specific **$1,000** sales limit is the rule and matches the statute.
- The statute gives a broker a reasonable time to correct **escrow errors** when there is **no shortage** and no significant threat of economic harm to the public. "Borrowing" escrow money creates a shortage: it is conversion, not an error.
- The Legislature intends that paying out escrowed funds should not be delayed by a dispute over the broker's own funds in the account.

## 9. Common misconceptions

- **"Three days includes the weekend."** Count business days only, and never the day of receipt.
- **"The broker's three days start when the associate hands over the check."** The course counts from the first receipt. The hand-off does not restart the clock.
- **"A short loan from escrow is fine if it is repaid."** Using escrow money for any personal or business purpose is conversion, and there is no repayment grace period.
- **"Every escrow account may hold $5,000 of the broker's money."** $5,000 is the limit for property management accounts. For sales escrow accounts the limit is $1,000.
- **"None of the broker's own money may ever be in escrow."** Up to $1,000 (sales) or $5,000 (property management) is allowed for bank charges. Only more than that is commingling.
- **"The broker may keep the interest."** Only if all parties consent in writing, and the consent says who gets it.
- **"If a title company holds the deposit, the broker has nothing to do."** The broker must request written verification (unless the seller or seller's agent nominated the holder in writing) and pass it on.
- **"The holder's statement goes to the seller within 10 business days"** (the manual's version). There are two windows: the request is due within 10 business days after the deposit is due, and the copy goes to the **seller's broker** (or an unrepresented seller) within 10 business days of that request.

## 10. Memory sheet

- **Associate 1, broker 3**, in business days. Day of receipt = day 0. Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays don't count.
- Both clocks start at the **first receipt**.
- **Sales 1K, rentals 5K.** More = **commingling**. Spending it = **conversion**.
- Interest-bearing account = **all parties consent in writing** (including who gets the interest).
- Title company or attorney holds the deposit: **holder named on the contract → written request within 10 business days after the deposit is due → copy to the seller's broker within 10 business days of the request**.
- Keep brokerage records at least **5 years**.

## 2. Why it matters

Deals fail. When the buyer and the seller both claim the deposit, the broker in the middle is holding someone else's money and cannot simply pick a winner. Florida gives the broker a fixed procedure with business-day deadlines. A broker who follows it and then obeys the resulting order is protected from discipline for failure to account. A broker who ignores it can be disciplined.

On the exam this is pure recall: **15 business days to notify, 30 to choose a procedure**, the four procedures (M.A.L.E.), when an escrow disbursement order is available, and the two situations in the statute where the broker may simply return the deposit to the buyer.

## 3. Simple explanation

### What starts the procedure

Rule 61J2-10.032, F.A.C. (effective 5/21/2024; verified, V-15) applies when a broker **holds the funds in its own escrow account** and one of two things happens:

- **Conflicting demands.** Two or more parties claim the same deposit. For example, the buyer wants it back and the seller wants to keep it.
- **Good-faith doubt.** The broker honestly does not know who is entitled, even if not everyone has made a demand.

### The two deadlines

| Step | Deadline | Counted from |
|---|---|---|
| Written notice to the FREC | **15 business days** | The **last** demand (or when the broker came to have good-faith doubt) |
| Start a settlement procedure | **30 business days** | The **last** demand |

**Why the *last* demand?** There is no conflict until the second claim arrives. Suppose the buyer demands the deposit on Monday and the seller counter-demands nine days later. The seller's demand is the last one, so you count from it.

### The four procedures: M.A.L.E.

| Procedure | Who decides | How it starts | Key point |
|---|---|---|---|
| **M**ediation | The parties themselves, helped by a neutral mediator | All parties consent **in writing** | Must be completed within **90 days** after the last demand; otherwise the broker promptly uses another procedure. Non-binding (manual; not yet verified) |
| **A**rbitration | A neutral arbitrator | All parties consent | Binding (manual; not yet verified) |
| **L**itigation (interpleader) | A court | The broker files it | The broker deposits the funds with the court and asks it to decide |
| **E**scrow Disbursement Order (EDO) | The FREC | The broker requests it | Available **only when the broker holds the funds** |

Note that the sources state the mediation limit as **90 days**, not business days.

### After a procedure starts

- If the matter **settles**, or **goes to court**, before an EDO is issued, the broker notifies the FREC within **10 business days**.
- A broker who follows the procedure and the resulting order cannot be disciplined for failure to account.
- The manual adds that if a broker obeys an EDO and is later sued over the payout, the Recovery Fund may pay the broker's court costs and any judgment (manual; not yet verified).

### When the procedure does not apply

1. **Two exceptions in the statute** (s. 475.25(1)(d)1, F.S.; corrected, V-16). In these cases the licensee **may return the deposit to the buyer without notifying the FREC or starting a procedure**:
   - a **residential condominium buyer** delivers written notice of intent to cancel under **s. 718.503**. The cancellation periods are taught in L08-5: 15 days for a developer sale, and 7 days for a resale, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays, since July 1, 2025 (V-08). Older books say 3 business days for resales, which is outdated.
   - the buyer, **in good faith**, fails to satisfy the terms of the **financing clause**. For example, the buyer applied for the loan honestly and was turned down.
2. **A title company or attorney holds the deposit.** The broker is not the escrow holder. There is no FREC notice and **no EDO**. The broker's duty is the written verification procedure from L05-1 (V-39).
3. **"HUD sale."** The manual lists this as an exception. It was not found in the statute and is not taught.

## 4. Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Conflicting demands | Two or more parties claim the same escrowed deposit |
| Good-faith doubt | The broker honestly does not know who is entitled to escrowed funds |
| Settlement procedure | One of the four ways to resolve the dispute (M.A.L.E.) |
| Mediation | A neutral person helps the parties reach their own agreement; requires all parties' written consent |
| Arbitration | A neutral arbitrator decides the dispute; requires all parties' consent |
| Interpleader | A lawsuit in which the broker deposits the disputed funds with the court and asks it to decide |
| Escrow Disbursement Order (EDO) | FREC order deciding who receives disputed funds held by a broker |
| Financing clause | Contract term making the purchase depend on the buyer getting a loan |
| Failure to account | Not accounting for, or not delivering, money held for others; a ground for discipline |

## 5. Practical example

A buyer and seller sign a contract for a house in **Orlando**. The listing broker holds the buyer's **$20,000** deposit in its sales escrow account. The buyer cancels, citing the inspection, and demands the deposit back. The seller says the buyer missed the inspection deadline, and on **Wednesday, March 3, 2027** she demands the deposit for herself. Assume no legal holidays fall between March 3 and April 14.

| Date | Event | Broker's duty |
|---|---|---|
| Wed, Mar 3, 2027 | Seller's demand = **last demand** (day 0) | Start counting |
| Wed, Mar 24, 2027 | 15th business day | Written notice to the FREC is due |
| Wed, Apr 14, 2027 | 30th business day | A settlement procedure must have been started |
| Tue, Jun 1, 2027 | 90 days after the last demand (calendar days) | If mediation was chosen, it must be finished; otherwise the broker promptly uses another procedure |

Variations:

- **The broker requests an EDO.** Before the FREC issues it, the buyer and seller sign a settlement. The broker notifies the FREC within **10 business days**.
- **Financing failure.** The buyer applied for the loan in good faith and was denied, and the contract had a financing clause. The broker may return the deposit to the buyer with no FREC notice.
- **Condo resale in Miami.** The buyer delivers written notice of cancellation within the s. 718.503 period. The broker may return the deposit with no FREC notice.
- **A title company holds the deposit.** The broker is not the escrow holder, so there is no FREC procedure and no EDO.

## 6. Portugal comparison

**Familiar principle.** A Portuguese deposit on a contrato-promessa is presumed to be **sinal** (Código Civil art. 442). If the payer defaults, the other party keeps it. If the recipient defaults, the payer can demand double.

**Florida rule.** What happens to the deposit depends on the **contract**. Often the deposit is liquidated damages if the buyer defaults. The broker releases escrowed funds only as the contract provides, on a written agreement of the parties, or through a settlement procedure.

**The difference.** Florida has no automatic double-return rule, and when demands conflict the broker does not decide who wins: the procedure does.

**Exam implication.** Suppose a question asks whether the seller receives double the deposit when a buyer defaults. The answer is no. If it asks what the broker does with competing claims, the answer is notice within 15 business days and a procedure within 30.

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

**Memorize exactly**

- Rule 61J2-10.032 (V-15): written notice to the FREC within **15 business days** of the last demand (or of coming to have good-faith doubt); a settlement procedure started within **30 business days** after the last demand.
- M.A.L.E.: **m**ediation (all parties consent **in writing**; completed within **90 days** after the last demand, or the broker promptly uses another procedure), **a**rbitration (all parties consent), **l**itigation (interpleader), **e**scrow disbursement order (only for funds the **broker** holds).
- Settled or in court before an EDO is issued: notify the FREC within **10 business days**.
- s. 475.25(1)(d)1 (V-16): return to the buyer with no FREC notice or procedure when a residential condo buyer cancels in writing under s. 718.503, or the buyer fails the financing clause in good faith.
- Mediation is non-binding; arbitration is binding (manual; not yet verified).

**Understand**

- Following the procedure and the resulting order protects the broker from discipline for failure to account.
- The Recovery Fund paying a broker's costs after the broker obeyed an EDO (manual; not yet verified).
- The manual's "HUD sale" exception is not in the statute. Do not use it.
- The manual describes the 10-business-day notice more broadly ("the parties settle before the procedure concludes"). The verified wording ties it to a settlement or lawsuit before an EDO is issued.
- Condo resale cancellation is now 7 days (since July 1, 2025). Older materials say 3 business days, which is outdated.

## 9. Common misconceptions

- **"15 and 30 are calendar days."** Both are **business** days.
- **"The clock starts at the first demand."** It starts at the **last** demand.
- **"The broker can request an EDO when a title company holds the money."** An EDO is only for funds the broker holds.
- **"The broker can give the deposit to whoever seems to be right."** Once demands conflict, the broker follows the procedure and the resulting order. Only the two exceptions in the statute let the broker return the deposit to the buyer without it.
- **"Any loan denial lets the broker return the deposit."** Only if the buyer acted **in good faith** under the financing clause.
- **"Mediation means someone decides for the parties."** Mediation needs everyone's written consent and is non-binding. Arbitration is the binding one (binding/non-binding: manual; not yet verified).
- **"If the seller backs out, the buyer automatically gets double the deposit."** That is the Portuguese sinal rule (art. 442), not Florida law. In Florida the contract and, if demands conflict, the settlement procedure decide who gets the deposit.

## 10. Memory sheet

- **15 to tell, 30 to choose**, both in business days and both from the **last demand**.
- **M.A.L.E.**: Mediation (written consent, 90 days), Arbitration (consent), Litigation (interpleader), EDO (broker-held funds only).
- Settled or in court before the EDO is issued → tell the FREC within **10 business days**.
- **Condo cancellation and good-faith loan denial** = return the deposit to the buyer, no notice.
- Title company or attorney holds it → no FREC procedure, no EDO.
- No "HUD sale" exception. No sinal.

## 2. Why it matters

As a sales associate you will advertise all the time: yard signs, portal listings, Instagram reels, WhatsApp statuses, flyers. In Florida each of these is a licensee advertisement, and each one must name your **brokerage**. An ad without the brokerage name is a **blind ad**, a violation in any medium. The exam tests it often, usually disguised as "it was her personal account" or "the name is in her profile".

## 3. Simple explanation

### The purpose

The idea behind the rules: the public should be able to see which licensed **brokerage** stands behind an ad. The brokerage is the licensed firm responsible for its associates, so its name is the one element that can never be left out.

### The core rules (Rules 61J2-10.025 and 61J2-10.026, F.A.C.; verified, V-44)

1. **The brokerage name goes in every ad.** Every real estate advertisement by a licensee must include the **licensed (registered) name of the brokerage firm**. An ad without it is a **blind ad**, whatever the medium: yard sign, newspaper, website, Instagram, WhatsApp status.
2. **Your own name.** If a licensee's personal name appears, **at least the last name** must be used **as registered** (with the DBPR). A registered "Robert Silva" may advertise as "Bob Silva"; "Bob S." does not use the registered last name.
3. **Internet ads.** The brokerage name must be placed **next to, above or below the contact information** in the ad (the phone number, email address and so on). Putting the brokerage name on a profile page one click away does not meet the rule.
4. **Price.** The manual adds that an advertised price must be the price agreed with the seller (manual; not yet verified).

### Teams and groups

A team is a group of licensees within one brokerage who market together under a team name. The team is not a brokerage, and its advertising must not make it look like one.

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Allowed words | The team name may include **"team"** or **"group"** |
| Banned words | **"Brokerage", "Realty", "Real Estate", "Properties", "Company"** or similar words suggesting a separate brokerage |
| Size | In ads, the team name may **not appear larger** than the brokerage name |
| Overall impression | Team advertising may not create the impression that the team is an independent brokerage (manual; not yet verified. The verified word and size rules above serve the same purpose) |

### Who is not covered

An **owner who is not a licensee**, advertising her own property, is not placing a licensee ad. She has no brokerage to name.

### What is not a confirmed rule

The manual says an associate's own name "cannot dominate" over the brokerage identity (manual; not yet verified). The confirmed size limit (V-44) applies to **team names**: a team name may not appear larger than the brokerage name.

Ads are also covered by rules taught elsewhere: fair housing (L07-1), Do Not Call and CAN-SPAM (L07-2), and credit-advertising trigger terms (L07-3).

## 4. Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Advertisement | Any marketing message by a licensee about property or real estate services, in any medium |
| Blind ad | A licensee's ad that does not include the licensed brokerage name |
| Licensed brokerage name | The firm's name as registered with the DBPR |
| Contact information | The phone number, email address or other way to reach the brokerage or licensee shown in an ad |
| Team or group | Licensees within one brokerage who market together under a team name |

## 5. Practical example

**Joana Ferreira** is a sales associate with **Atlantic Palm Realty, LLC** in Miami, and a member of a team. Does each ad comply?

| Ad | Complies? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Yard sign: "FOR SALE — Call Joana, 305-555-0142" | **No** | Blind ad: no brokerage name |
| Instagram reel with her phone number; the brokerage name is only in her bio | **No** | The brokerage name must be next to, above or below the contact details in the ad itself |
| Website: "Jo Ferreira · Atlantic Palm Realty, LLC · 305-555-0142" | **Yes** | Registered last name, and the brokerage name sits beside the contact details |
| Flyer: "Joana F., your Brickell expert · Atlantic Palm Realty, LLC" | **No** | Her registered last name does not appear |
| Team ad: "THE FERREIRA GROUP" in large letters, brokerage name in small print at the bottom | **No** | The team name may not be larger than the brokerage name |
| The team renames itself "Ferreira Realty Team" | **No** | "Realty" suggests a separate brokerage |
| Her neighbour, not a licensee, posts "House for sale by owner, call 786-555-0199" | **Not a licensee ad** | There is no brokerage to name |
| A price $25,000 below the seller's agreed price, "to get calls" | **No** | The price must be the one agreed with the seller (manual; not yet verified) |

## 6. Portugal comparison

**Familiar principle.** In Portugal, mediation advertising is regulated, and you may be used to seeing an agency's license identification in its ads. Both points, and whether and how Portuguese law requires that identification, *require verification*.

**Florida rule.** The required element is the **licensed brokerage name**. It must appear in every licensee ad, including an associate's personal social media.

**The difference.** The Florida rule is about the brokerage's **name**. A license number in an ad does not replace it.

**Exam implication.** Answer only with the Florida rule. An associate's personal post still needs the brokerage name. An owner advertising her own property needs none.

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

**Memorize exactly**

- Every real estate ad by a licensee must include the **licensed (registered) name of the brokerage firm**. Without it, the ad is a **blind ad** (V-44).
- Licensee's personal name: at least the **last name as registered** (V-44).
- Internet ads: the brokerage name goes **next to, above or below the contact information** (V-44).
- Team names (V-44):
  - "team" or "group" allowed;
  - "Brokerage", "Realty", "Real Estate", "Properties", "Company" or similar words banned;
  - team name **not larger** than the brokerage name.
- Advertised price = the price agreed with the seller (manual; not yet verified).

**Understand**

- The verified rules are in Rules 61J2-10.025 and 61J2-10.026, F.A.C. (V-44).
- The manual's "an associate's name cannot dominate the brokerage identity" is not yet verified. The size limit for team names is confirmed.
- The rule is about licensee advertising. An owner who is not a licensee needs no brokerage name.

## 9. Common misconceptions

- **"My personal account is exempt."** It is not. A licensee's ad on any account or medium needs the brokerage name.
- **"The brokerage name in my bio or profile is enough."** In an internet ad it must sit next to, above or below the contact information.
- **"My license number replaces the brokerage name."** The rule requires the brokerage **name**.
- **"Team names are not allowed."** They are allowed. They may use "team" or "group" but not "Realty", "Real Estate", "Properties", "Company" or "Brokerage".
- **"A nickname is illegal."** A nickname is allowed if the registered **last name** appears.
- **"An owner selling by herself must name a brokerage."** An owner who is not a licensee has none to name.
- **"An individual licensee's name may never be bigger than the brokerage name."** That comes from the manual's "cannot dominate" and is not yet verified. The confirmed size rule applies to team names.

## 10. Memory sheet

- **No firm name = blind ad**, in any medium, including personal social media.
- Personal name: at least the **registered last name**.
- Internet: firm name **next to, above or below** the contact info.
- Teams: "team" or "group" only. No Realty, Real Estate, Properties, Company or Brokerage. Never larger than the brokerage name.
- Owners who are not licensees are not placing licensee ads.

## 2. Why it matters

This lesson covers the office and its sign, and how money moves through a brokerage: who may pay a sales associate, payments to unlicensed people, rebates, kickbacks, commission rates and rental lists. Most violations here start with good intentions, such as a thank-you gift from a seller, a "bonus" for a helpful assistant or a referral fee from a lender. The exam describes exactly these situations and asks whether the payment is lawful.

## 3. Simple explanation

### The office and its entrance sign

An active broker must maintain a registered **office**, a stationary location where records are kept. Every additional location where the broker does business is a **branch office**, and each one is registered with a fee (manual; not yet verified).

The **entrance sign** comes from statute: s. 475.22(1), F.S. (verified, V-45). A sign is required **at the entrance of the principal (main) office and of each branch office**. It must show:

| Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Name | The broker's name, together with the trade name, if any |
| A firm (brokerage company) | The firm's name **plus** the name of at least one broker |
| Words | **"Licensed Real Estate Broker"** or **"Lic. Real Estate Broker"** |

### Money flows through the broker

A sales associate may be paid **only by her employing broker** (or by an owner-developer employer; see L03-2). She is never paid directly by a seller, a buyer, another associate or another brokerage (manual; not yet verified). The path is fixed: **customer → brokerage → associate**.

- Pay may be a salary, a commission or both, as agreed with the broker (manual; not yet verified).
- A pay dispute is a **contract matter between the associate and the broker**. The associate sues her broker, not the customer. It is not a FREC matter and not a Recovery Fund claim (manual; not yet verified).

### Paying unlicensed people

Paying an **unlicensed person** for real estate services is prohibited (manual; not yet verified). The test is **what the payment is for**:

- A **salary** for clerical work, such as scheduling, filing or answering phones, is fine.
- A **per-closing or per-deal** bonus is pay for transactions, and it is unlawful.
- Paying a past customer a fee for each client referred is unlawful.

The manual treats a small thank-you gift to a past customer for a referral as a gray zone, decided by asking whether the payment was *for a real estate service* (manual; not yet verified). The statute's exact wording on referral payments to unlicensed persons was not checked in this course (not yet verified).

### Lawful payments

- **Rebates.** A broker may rebate part of the commission to a **party to the transaction** (the buyer or seller), with full disclosure to all interested parties (manual; not yet verified).
- **Referrals between licensees.** Sharing compensation is lawful **broker to broker**. An associate may not pay or receive a referral fee directly (manual; not yet verified).

### Kickbacks and RESPA

A **kickback** is a payment for referring business. Under Chapter 475, a kickback from a settlement-service provider (such as an inspector, title company or lender) is allowed only if **all parties are fully informed** and **no other law prohibits it** (manual; not yet verified).

Another law usually does prohibit it. **RESPA Section 8** (verified, V-53) bans kickbacks and unearned fees for referrals. RESPA covers **federally related mortgage loans on 1–4 family residential property**, which describes most financed home sales. Because Chapter 475 allows a kickback only when no other law forbids it, full disclosure does not save a payment that RESPA prohibits (see L07-3).

### Commission rates are negotiable

There is no "standard", "board-approved" or "customary" rate. Each broker agrees its fee with its own client. Competing brokers who agree on rates are engaged in **price fixing**, which antitrust law prohibits (manual; not yet verified). The reason: antitrust law protects competition. If competitors agree on a rate, consumers lose the chance to shop around and bargain, so each broker must set its fee independently, client by client.

### Rental information lists (s. 475.453, F.S.; verified, V-21)

Some licensees sell lists of available rentals to people looking for a home. A broker or sales associate who sells a rental information list, for a fee paid by the prospective tenant, must give the tenant a **contract or receipt**. It must provide for:

- repayment of any amount **over 25%** of the fee if the tenant **does not obtain a rental**, so the tenant gets **75%** back;
- repayment of the **full fee** on demand if the information was **not current or accurate** in any material respect.

A violation is a **first-degree misdemeanor**, and the licensee's license is also subject to **suspension or revocation**. Older editions of the statute (2002/2005) required the tenant to demand the 75% refund **within 30 days** of the contract. The 2025 excerpts checked did not show that window, so it is **not re-confirmed as current**. Exam questions written from older materials may still use it.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Principal office | The broker's registered main office |
| Branch office | Any additional registered location where the broker does business |
| Trade name | A business name the brokerage operates under |
| Employing broker | The broker a sales associate is registered under, and her only source of pay |
| Rebate | Part of the commission returned to the buyer or seller, with full disclosure |

## 5. Practical example

**Marco** is a sales associate at **Gulf Harbor Realty, Inc.** in Tampa. His broker is **Elena Ruiz**. Which of these is lawful?

| Situation | Lawful? | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Branch entrance sign: "Gulf Harbor Realty, Inc. · Elena Ruiz · Lic. Real Estate Broker" | **Yes** | Firm name, a broker's name and the required words |
| Sign: "Gulf Harbor Realty · REALTOR®" | **No** | No broker's name and no required words. "REALTOR®" does not replace them |
| A seller hands Marco a $500 gift card at closing "for all your work" | **No** | An associate is paid only by the employing broker |
| The team's unlicensed assistant gets $100 per closed deal for scheduling showings | **No** | Per-deal pay to an unlicensed person |
| The same assistant gets a monthly salary for scheduling | **Yes** | Salary for clerical work |
| Elena rebates $2,000 of the commission to the buyer, disclosed to all parties | **Yes** | Rebate to a party, with full disclosure |
| Marco pays a $1,000 referral fee directly to an agent at another brokerage | **No** | Referral fees go broker to broker |
| A lender offers Elena $250 for each buyer she refers for a federally related home loan, fully disclosed | **No** | RESPA Section 8. Disclosure does not cure it |
| Elena and a rival broker agree neither will take listings below 6% | **No** | Price fixing |

**Rental list calculation.** A tenant pays **$200** for a list.

| Outcome | Refund | Licensee may keep |
|---|---|---|
| Accurate list, no rental obtained | 75% × $200 = **$150** | 25% × $200 = $50 |
| Half the listings had already been rented | Full fee: **$200** | $0 |
| Accurate list, tenant rents a unit from it | No refund required | $200 |

## 6. Portugal comparison

**Familiar principle.** Under Lei 15/2013, art. 19, remuneration is due when the deal the mediation aimed at is concluded and perfected. Under exclusivity it remains due if the deal fails for reasons attributable to the client. Courts require a causal link between the mediator's work and the deal.

**Florida rule.** Commission terms come from the listing contract. Under an exclusive right of sale the brokerage earns the fee whoever sells; under an open listing only the procuring cause earns it. By law, the money flows **customer → brokerage → associate**. A sales associate may be paid only by her employing broker.

**The difference.** In Portugal a statute (art. 19) says when the remuneration is due; in Florida the listing contract sets when the commission is earned. More important for the exam, Florida law fixes **who may pay whom**: an associate never takes money directly from a customer or another brokerage.

**Exam implication.** "The associate accepted payment from the seller (or another broker)" → violation. A "standard rate" → price fixing. There is no Portuguese comparison for office signs, rental lists or RESPA in this course (*requires verification*).

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

**Memorize exactly**

- Entrance sign (s. 475.22(1); V-45): principal office **and each branch**; broker's name and any trade name (a firm: its name plus at least one broker); the words **"Licensed Real Estate Broker"** or **"Lic. Real Estate Broker"**.
- Rental list (s. 475.453; V-21): contract or receipt; no rental obtained → refund any amount **over 25%** (75% back); information not current or accurate → **full** refund on demand; violation = **first-degree misdemeanor** plus license suspension or revocation.
- RESPA Section 8 (V-53): no kickbacks or unearned fees for referrals on federally related mortgage loans (1–4 family residential).
- Associate paid only by the employing broker; no pay to unlicensed persons for real estate services; rebates to parties with full disclosure; Chapter 475 kickbacks only with full disclosure and no other law forbidding them (manual; not yet verified).
- Registered office; each branch office registered (manual; not yet verified).
- Rental-list demand window: **30 days** of the contract in older editions; not shown in the 2025 excerpts, so not re-confirmed as current.

**Understand**

- Commission rates are negotiable; competitors agreeing on rates is price fixing (manual; not yet verified).
- A Chapter 475 kickback needs full disclosure **and** no other law against it. On most financed home sales RESPA is that other law.

## 9. Common misconceptions

- **"A gift from a happy seller isn't compensation."** Anything of value for the associate's work is compensation. It must come through the broker.
- **"A standard commission rate exists."** Rates are negotiated with each client. Competitors agreeing on rates is price fixing.
- **"'REALTOR®' on the main office sign is enough."** The sign needs "Licensed Real Estate Broker" or "Lic. Real Estate Broker", and **each branch** needs one too.
- **"A small per-closing bonus for an unlicensed assistant is fine."** Per-deal pay to an unlicensed person is unlawful at any amount. A salary for clerical work is fine.
- **"Disclosure makes any kickback legal."** RESPA Section 8 prohibits referral kickbacks on federally related loans even when they are disclosed.
- **"On a rental list, the licensee keeps 75%."** It is the reverse: the licensee keeps at most 25%, and the full fee is refunded if the list was inaccurate.
- **"Rental-list violations are second-degree misdemeanors, like most s. 475.42(1) violations."** They are **first-degree**. (Unlicensed practice is a third-degree felony; V-12.)

## 10. Memory sheet

- Sign at **every** office entrance: broker's name + trade name + "Licensed (Lic.) Real Estate Broker". For a firm, at least one broker's name.
- **Money flows through the broker.** Associates are paid only by their employing broker.
- Unlicensed people: **salary for clerical work OK, per-deal pay never.**
- Rebate to a **party**, with full disclosure: OK. Referral fees: **broker to broker**.
- RESPA §8: no referral kickbacks on federally related residential loans. Disclosure doesn't cure it.
- Rates are negotiable. Competitors agreeing on rates = **price fixing**.
- Rental lists: **keep 25%, return 75%**; inaccurate list = **100%** back. First-degree misdemeanor plus license discipline. The 30-day demand window is from older text and not re-confirmed.
