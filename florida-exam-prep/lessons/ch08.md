## 3. Simple explanation

**Bundle of rights:** possession, control, enjoyment, exclusion, disposition (manual mnemonic D.E.E.P. C).

**Freehold estates** (ownership of indefinite duration):

- **Fee simple absolute** — the maximum bundle; inheritable; no conditions.
- **Fee simple defeasible** — ownership that can be lost if a condition is broken ("so long as the land is used as a school").
- **Life estate** — ownership measured by a life (the life tenant's, or another person's: *pur autre vie*). The **life tenant** has full use, collects rents, pays taxes and upkeep, may sell or lease her interest (which ends at the measuring death), and must not commit **waste**. The future interest is a **remainder** if it passes to a third party (remainderman) or a **reversion** if it returns to the grantor or heirs.

## 6. Portugal comparison

See PT-15: a life estate resembles *usufruto* in purpose but uses US vocabulary (remainder, reversion, waste).

## 9. Common misconceptions

- "A life tenant's lease survives her death." It ends with the life estate.
- Swapping remainder and reversion.

## 10. Memory sheet

Fee simple = everything. Life estate: remainder goes forward to a third person; reversion comes back.

## 3. Simple explanation

**Leasehold (nonfreehold) estates** give possession without ownership:

- **Estate for years** — any fixed term, even one week; ends automatically without notice.
- **Periodic tenancy** — renews week to week, month to month, etc., until notice (Florida notice periods: 60/30/30/7, Lesson L07-4).
- **Tenancy at will** — indefinite; ends with proper notice.
- **Tenancy at sufferance** — a holdover tenant without the landlord's consent.

**A lease** is both a contract and a conveyance of a leasehold. Elements: parties, description, term, rent, signatures. A lease longer than one year must be in writing (statute of frauds). **Correction (V-05):** the manual says Florida requires two witnesses on leases over one year; s. 689.01 now states that **no subscribing witnesses are required for leases**.

**Lease types:** **gross** (fixed rent; landlord pays expenses), **net** (tenant also pays some or all taxes, insurance, maintenance; triple net = all three), **percentage** (base rent plus a percentage of gross sales over a breakpoint — retail), **ground** (long-term land lease; tenant builds), **graduated/step-up** (scheduled increases), **index** (tied to CPI).

**Assignment vs sublease:** assignment transfers the **entire remaining term** (assignee pays the landlord; the original tenant stays secondarily liable unless released). A sublease transfers part of the term or space (subtenant pays the tenant, who stays primarily liable). A lease survives the sale of the property: the buyer takes subject to existing leases.

## 9. Common misconceptions

- "An estate for years must last a year."
- "Transferring the last 14 months of a 3-year lease is a sublease." It is an assignment — measure against what remains.

## 10. Memory sheet

Fixed term = estate for years (no notice). All remaining term = assignment. Leases: writing if >1 year, no witnesses now.

## 3. Simple explanation

- **Tenancy in common** — Florida's default when a deed to two or more unmarried people is silent. Shares may be unequal; each owner may sell or mortgage his share; at death the share passes to heirs or devisees.
- **Joint tenancy with right of survivorship** — requires the four unities (**PITT**: Possession, Interest, Time, Title) and, in Florida, **express survivorship language**; at death the share passes to the surviving joint tenants outside probate.
- **Tenancy by the entireties** — married couples only (unities plus marriage); automatic survivorship; neither spouse can convey alone; protected from creditors of only one spouse. A deed to a married couple is presumed to create an entireties estate.

Florida is **not** a community-property state. Any co-tenant may sue for **partition**.

## 6. Portugal comparison

See PT-13: Florida's outcome depends on the deed form and marital status, not on a marital property regime.

## 10. Memory sheet

Married + silent = entireties. Unmarried + silent = in common. Survivorship needs words (or marriage).

## 3. Simple explanation

Florida "homestead" has three separate meanings. This lesson covers the first two; the tax exemption is in Chapter 18.

**1. Protection from forced sale (Art. X):** the owner's primary residence, up to **1/2 acre of contiguous land within a municipality** or **160 contiguous acres outside**, is protected from forced sale by most creditors. It does **not** stop: property taxes and assessments; the mortgage on the home; liens for labor or materials used to improve the home (construction liens). Federal (IRS) liens can also reach it.

**2. Family protection:** if the owner has a **spouse or minor child**, the homestead cannot be freely devised away from them, and the **spouse must join** in any deed or mortgage of the homestead, even if title is in one name.

## 6. Portugal comparison

See PT-16.

## 9. Common misconceptions

- Mixing creditor protection facts with the $25,000 tax exemption.
- "Homestead blocks the lender who financed the home." It does not.

## 10. Memory sheet

Half acre in town · 160 acres outside. Taxes, the home's mortgage, and repair liens get through. Spouse joins.

## 3. Simple explanation

**Condominium:** created by **recording a declaration of condominium**. The buyer owns the **unit in fee simple** plus an **undivided share of the common elements**, which cannot be separated from the unit. Buyers receive the declaration, bylaws, rules, FAQ and financial information.

**Cancellation rights (s. 718.503):** purchase of a **new unit from the developer** — **15 days**. **Resale** — the manual says **3 business days** after receiving the documents; fragmentary 2025 statute excerpts show **7 days** (excluding weekends and legal holidays) — **unresolved (V-08)**; check the current statute before relying on either.

**Cooperative:** a corporation owns the whole building; the buyer receives **shares** plus a **proprietary lease** — **personal property**; the board usually approves buyers.

**Timeshare:** the right to use a unit for periods; **10-day** cancellation right that cannot be waived.

**HOA:** mandatory-membership community with recorded covenants; a **disclosure summary** must be given before the contract (if not, the buyer can void the contract, per manual).

**CDD (Community Development District):** a special-purpose local government that finances community infrastructure and charges assessments (named in the FREC section title; not covered in the manual).

## 9. Common misconceptions

- "Co-op buyers own real estate."
- Mixing 15 (developer condo) / resale (flagged) / 10 (timeshare).

## 10. Memory sheet

Condo = deed + common elements. Co-op = shares + lease. 15 developer · resale flagged · 10 timeshare.
