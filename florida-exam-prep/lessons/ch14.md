## 3. Simple explanation

**Step 1 — Concept.** Most exam math is one idea: a **part** is a **rate** (percentage) of a **total**.

**Step 2 — Units.** Percent means "per hundred": 6% = 6/100 = 0.06. To turn a percent into a decimal, move the decimal point two places left; to turn a decimal into a percent, move it two places right. Fraction → decimal: divide top by bottom (3/8 = 0.375 = 37.5%).

**Step 3 — Formula (the percent triangle).** **Part = Total × Rate.** Rearranged: **Total = Part ÷ Rate**; **Rate = Part ÷ Total**.

**Step 4 — Conditions.** Identify which number is the *total the percent is taken of*. If the percent is applied to the unknown, divide.

**Step 5 — Worked example.** A 6% commission was $9,450. Price = $9,450 ÷ 0.06 = $157,500.

**Step 6 — Reasonableness check.** A commission is a small fraction of the price: if your "price" is smaller than the commission, you multiplied instead of divided.

**Step 7 — Common errors.** Decimal placement (0.6 vs 0.06); using the wrong base; rounding too early.

**Percentage change** = (new − old) ÷ **old**. $80,000 → $92,000 is +15%; $92,000 → $80,000 is about −13%. The base changes, so the percentages differ.

## 10. Memory sheet

Part = Total × Rate. Percent of the unknown → divide. Change ÷ original.

## 3. Simple explanation

**Units:** 1 ft = 12 in · 1 yd = 3 ft · 1 sq yd = **9 sq ft** · 1 acre = **43,560 sq ft** · 1 mile = **5,280 ft** · 1 sq mile = 640 acres. (Metric intuition: 1 sq ft ≈ 0.0929 m²; 1 acre ≈ 4,047 m².)

**Formulas:** rectangle = length × width; triangle = ½ × base × height; composite shapes = split into rectangles/triangles and add. Volume (if asked) = length × width × height (cubic yards = cubic feet ÷ 27).

**Front feet:** the frontage dimension along the street or water — usually the first number given (75' × 150' lot has 75 front feet). Price per front foot = price ÷ front feet.

**Common errors:** dividing square feet by 3 (instead of 9) to get square yards; mixing feet and yards; forgetting the ½ for triangles.

## 10. Memory sheet

sq yd = sq ft ÷ 9 · acres = sq ft ÷ 43,560 · front foot = first dimension.

## 3. Simple explanation

**Commission chain:** total commission = price × rate → split between brokerages → split between brokerage and associate. Multiply through each step. Working backwards, divide by each split.

**Net to seller:** the seller wants a fixed amount after the commission. Because the commission is a percentage **of the price**, **Price = (Net + fixed costs) ÷ (1 − rate)**.

Worked example: net $188,000, 6% commission → $188,000 ÷ 0.94 = $200,000. Check: $200,000 × 6% = $12,000; $200,000 − $12,000 = $188,000 ✓.

**Common error:** $188,000 × 1.06 = $199,280 — that takes 6% of the net, not of the price, and leaves the seller short.

## 10. Memory sheet

Net ÷ (1 − rate). Always check by taking the commission from your answer.

## 3. Simple explanation

**Profit or loss percentage** is measured **on cost** (what was paid): (sale − cost) ÷ cost.

**Recovering the cost:** Sale = Cost × (1 + profit%), so **Cost = Sale ÷ (1 + profit%)**. For a loss, Cost = Sale ÷ (1 − loss%).

Worked example: sold for $276,000 at a 15% profit → cost = $276,000 ÷ 1.15 = $240,000. **Never** $276,000 × 0.85 = $234,600 (that takes 15% of the sale price).

## 10. Memory sheet

When a percent is "of" the unknown, divide. Profit is on cost.

## 3. Simple explanation

**Simple interest:** I = P × R × T, with T in **years** (18 months = 1.5).

**Amortization, first payment:** month's interest = balance × annual rate ÷ 12; principal = payment − interest; new balance = old balance − principal.

**LTV:** loan ÷ **lesser of price or appraised value**. Down payment = price − loan.

**Points:** cost = points% × **loan amount** (not the price). Course rule: each point ≈ +1/8% yield.

## 10. Memory sheet

T in years · interest ÷ 12 for a month · LTV uses the lower value · points on the loan.

## 3. Simple explanation

**Florida's three transfer taxes (verified V-18, V-19):**

| Tax | Rate | Base | Rounding | Customarily paid by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Doc stamps on the **deed** | $0.70 per $100 (Miami-Dade single-family $0.60; other Miami-Dade property $0.60 + $0.45 surtax per manual) | Full purchase price, including any mortgage assumed | Round **up** to next $100 | Seller |
| Doc stamps on the **note** | $0.35 per $100 | Amount financed — new **and assumed** (course convention, V-25) | Round **up** to next $100 | Buyer |
| **Intangible tax** | 2 mills (0.002) | **New** mortgage only | None | Buyer |

Worked example (manual): $412,480 sale in Broward with a new $330,000 loan. Deed: round to $412,500 → 4,125 × $0.70 = **$2,887.50**. Note: 3,300 × $0.35 = **$1,155**. Intangible: 330,000 × 0.002 = **$660**. If the buyer *assumed* $330,000 instead: note stamps yes, intangible no.

## 3. Simple explanation

**Prorating** divides an expense or income between seller and buyer as of closing. Florida course conventions: the **365-day method** (actual days in each month) and **the buyer owns the day of closing** — unless the question says otherwise.

**Paid in arrears** (property taxes, mortgage interest): the bill comes later, so the seller owes the buyer for the seller's days (Jan 1 through the day before closing): **debit seller, credit buyer**.

**Paid in advance** (rent the seller collected, prepaid insurance or association dues): the buyer gets credit for unused rent (credit buyer, debit seller); the seller gets credit for prepaid expenses beyond closing (debit buyer, credit seller).

Worked example (manual): closing May 20; annual taxes $5,475 unpaid; daily $15. Seller's days: Jan 31 + Feb 28 + Mar 31 + Apr 30 + May 19 = 139 → **$2,085**, debit seller / credit buyer.

## 10. Memory sheet

365 days · buyer owns closing day · arrears → debit seller · advance rent → credit buyer.

## 3. Simple explanation

A **debit** is what a party owes; a **credit** is what a party receives.

| Item | Buyer | Seller |
|---|---|---|
| Purchase price | Debit | Credit |
| Binder deposit | Credit (single entry) | — |
| New mortgage | Credit | — |
| **Assumed mortgage** | **Credit** | **Debit** |
| Existing mortgage payoff | — | Debit |
| Deed doc stamps | — | Debit |
| Note stamps + intangible | Debit | — |
| Unpaid taxes (proration) | Credit | Debit |
| Commission | — | Debit (customarily) |

**Correction (V-24):** the study manual's table shows the assumed mortgage as a buyer credit only. An assumed mortgage is a **double entry**: the buyer is credited because it pays part of the price, and the seller is debited because the seller hands over the debt instead of receiving that cash.

**Cash due:** buyer brings total debits − total credits; seller receives total credits − total debits.

## 10. Memory sheet

Deposit: buyer credit only. New loan: buyer credit only. Assumed loan: debit seller + credit buyer. Prorations between parties: double entries.
