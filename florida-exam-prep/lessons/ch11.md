## 3. Simple explanation

**Valid contract essentials:** competent parties; offer and acceptance (mutual assent); legal purpose; consideration (the mutual promises suffice — earnest money is customary but not legally required). Manual mnemonic: C.O.L.C.

**Classifications:** bilateral (promise for a promise — a sale contract) vs unilateral (promise for an act — an option, a reward); executory (obligations remain) vs executed (fully performed); express vs implied.

**Statute of frauds:** contracts for the **sale of real estate**, and contracts that cannot be performed within **one year**, must be in writing and signed by the party to be charged. A lease of one year or less may be oral.

**Statute of limitations (Florida):** written contract **5 years**; oral contract **4 years**.

**Defects:** **void** (no contract: illegal purpose, no genuine assent); **voidable** (one party may cancel: minor, fraud, duress, misrepresentation); **unenforceable** (valid but courts won't enforce: missing writing, time-barred).

## 10. Memory sheet

C.O.L.C. Sale of land = writing. 5 written / 4 oral. Void ≠ voidable ≠ unenforceable.

## 3. Simple explanation

An **offer** may be revoked any time before acceptance is communicated, even if the offeror promised to keep it open (unless an option was paid for). Acceptance must mirror the offer; any change is a **counteroffer**, which **terminates the original offer**. Offers also end by rejection, lapse of time, death or insanity of a party before acceptance, or destruction of the property.

**Option contract:** for consideration, the **optionor** (owner) is bound to sell on fixed terms during the option period; the **optionee** may exercise or walk away, losing only the option money. On exercise it becomes a bilateral sale contract.

**Equitable title:** the buyer's interest under a signed sale contract until legal title passes by deed at closing.

**Remedies:** specific performance (land is unique); liquidated damages (often the deposit); rescission; compensatory damages. Contracts are generally assignable unless prohibited; personal-service contracts (including listings) are not.

## 10. Memory sheet

Counteroffer kills the offer. Optionor bound, optionee chooses. Contract → equitable title.

## 3. Simple explanation

A listing is an employment (personal-service) contract between the seller and the **brokerage**.

- **Open listing** — any number of brokers; only the **procuring cause** earns the commission; the seller may sell personally free of commission.
- **Exclusive agency** — one brokerage, but the seller keeps the right to sell personally without commission.
- **Exclusive right of sale** — one brokerage earns the commission **no matter who sells**, including the seller.
- **Net listing** — the broker keeps the excess over a set net price; the manual says it is legal in Florida but conflict-laden (not verified).

**Florida rules for written listings:** a **definite expiration date**; **no automatic renewal**; a **copy to the person signing within 24 hours**. The manual states listings need not be written unless the term exceeds one year.

**Termination:** performance, expiration, mutual agreement, abandonment, destruction of the property, death or incapacity of either party. Not assignable to another brokerage without consent.

## 10. Memory sheet

RIGHT of sale = always paid. AGENCY = seller can sell free. Written listing: end date, no auto-renew, copy in 24 hours.

## 3. Simple explanation

**FR/BAR contract** (Florida Realtors / Florida Bar): standard residential form, with an **AS IS** version — the buyer takes the property in its current condition but keeps a due-diligence right to inspect and cancel; the seller's duty to disclose known latent defects survives (*Johnson v. Davis*). The **effective date** is when the last party signs or initials the final offer/counteroffer (and it is delivered); every deadline counts from it. **"Time is of the essence"** makes deadlines strict. Common contingencies: financing, appraisal, inspection, sale of buyer's home.

**Required disclosures around the contract:**

- **Radon** notice — every sale and rental of a building.
- **Lead-based paint** — housing built before 1978 (Chapter 19).
- **Property tax disclosure** — buyer should not rely on the seller's current taxes.
- **Energy-efficiency information brochure.**
- **Flood disclosure (s. 689.302, V-06)** — the seller of residential property gives the statutory flood disclosure form at or before contract execution (flood insurance, past claims, federal assistance, known flooding). Added by a 2024 law; the manual only mentions "flood-zone awareness".
- **Condominium / HOA documents and disclosure summaries.**
- **Seller's property disclosure** — customary; honesty is required regardless.

## 10. Memory sheet

AS IS shifts repairs, not disclosure. Effective date starts the clocks. Radon · lead (pre-1978) · tax · energy · flood · condo/HOA.
