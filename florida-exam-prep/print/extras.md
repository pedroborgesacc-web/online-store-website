# Math formulas and calculation patterns {#formulas}

The study manual estimates about ten pure-arithmetic questions across the whole exam (manual; not yet verified), spread over Sections 12, 14, 16, 17 and 18. They are the most reliable points on the test because the method never changes. Write every step, round **only at the end**, and check by working backwards.

## The one rule behind most problems

**Part = Total × Rate** · **Total = Part ÷ Rate** · **Rate = Part ÷ Total**

- If the percent is "of" the number you are looking for → **divide**. If the percent is "of" a number you have → **multiply**.
- **% change = change ÷ original.** $80,000 → $92,000 = +15%. Going back down from $92,000 to $80,000 is about −13%, not −15%.

## Area and units

| Convert | Rule |
|---|---|
| Square feet → square yards | ÷ **9** |
| Cubic feet → cubic yards | ÷ **27** |
| Square feet → acres | ÷ **43,560** |
| Mile | **5,280** feet |
| Square mile (one section) | **640** acres (manual; V-26) |

- Rectangle = length × width · triangle = **½** × base × height · odd shapes: split, then add.
- **Front feet = the first dimension** given (street or water side). Price per front foot = price ÷ front feet.
- Convert units **before** pricing. Example: 330 ft × 132 ft = 43,560 sq ft = 1 acre.

## Commissions and net to seller

- **Chain:** price × rate → × brokerage share → × associate share. Each split is "of" the level above. $300,000 × 6% = $18,000 → listing side 50% = $9,000 → associate 60% = **$5,400**.
- **Net to seller: Price = (Net + fixed costs) ÷ (1 − commission rate).**
- Seller wants $185,000 net, pays $3,000 in fixed costs, 6% commission: ($185,000 + $3,000) ÷ 0.94 = **$200,000**. Never net × 1.06 (that gives $199,280 and leaves the seller short).
- Costs stated as a % of price join the rate; costs stated in dollars join the net.

## Profit, loss and appreciation

- **Profit or loss is always measured on cost:** (sale − cost) ÷ cost.
- **Cost known → multiply:** sale = cost × (1 ± rate). **Cost unknown → divide:** cost = sale ÷ (1 + profit %) or ÷ (1 − loss %).
- Sold for $276,000 at a 15% profit → cost = $276,000 ÷ 1.15 = **$240,000** (never × 0.85).
- Yearly appreciation compounds unless the question says "of the original value".

## Interest, loans, LTV and points

- **Simple interest: I = P × R × T** (T in years; months ÷ 12).
- **One month's interest = balance × annual rate ÷ 12.** $200,000 at 6% → $1,000 interest. Payment $1,199.10 → principal paid = **$199.10**; new balance $199,800.90.
- **LTV = loan ÷ the lower of price and appraisal.** Price $250,000, appraisal $240,000, 80% LTV → loan $192,000; **down payment = price − loan** = $58,000.
- **Points are a % of the loan**, not the price: 2 points on $192,000 = $3,840. 1 point ≈ 1/8% more yield (manual; not yet verified).
- **Qualifying (course conventions, manual):** housing ratio = PITI ÷ **gross** monthly income (28% conventional / 31% FHA); total ratio = (PITI + recurring debts) ÷ gross (36% / 43%). Maximum PITI = the **lower** of the two results. Gross $8,000: 28% = $2,240; 36% = $2,880 − $800 debts = $2,080 → maximum PITI **$2,080**.

## Florida documentary stamps and intangible tax

| Tax | Rate | Base | Who usually pays (custom, manual) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deed stamps | **$0.70 per $100** (or part of $100) | Full price, including any assumed mortgage | Seller |
| Note stamps | **$0.35 per $100** (or part of $100) | New **and** assumed notes | Buyer |
| Intangible tax | **0.002** (2 mills) | **New** mortgages only, never assumed ones | Buyer |

- Stamps: **round the base up** to the next $100, then multiply. Intangible: never round.
- Price $250,150 → 2,502 × $0.70 = **$1,751.40**. New note $200,000 → $700 note stamps + $400 intangible.
- **Miami-Dade** deed stamps: **$0.60** per $100 for a single-family residence; **$1.05** ($0.60 + $0.45 surtax) for any other property (V-18).
- Divisibility check: a deed-stamp answer ÷ 0.70 (or 0.60 / 1.05) must be a whole number; a note-stamp answer ÷ 0.35 too. Also confirm you rounded **up**.

## Prorations

- Use **365 days** and the **buyer owns the day of closing** (course convention, V-79; follow the question if it says otherwise).
- Seller's days = January 1 to the day before closing. Daily amount = annual bill ÷ 365.
- **Taxes paid in arrears (unpaid) → debit seller, credit buyer.** Prepaid rent the seller collected → debit seller, credit buyer. Prepaid items the seller paid (dues, insurance) → debit buyer, credit seller.
- Example: tax bill $3,650 = $10/day; closing April 10 → seller owns Jan 1–Apr 9 = 99 days → **$990** debit seller, credit buyer.
- Check: seller days + buyer days = 365.

## Closing statement entries

- Debit = that party owes it. Credit = that party receives it.
- Earnest-money deposit and new loan → **buyer credit only**. Seller's loan payoff → **seller debit only**. Purchase price → debit buyer, credit seller.
- **Assumed loan → debit seller + credit buyer** (V-24).
- Cash the buyer brings = buyer debits − buyer credits. Cash to the seller = seller credits − seller debits.

## Property tax

**Tax = taxable value × mills ÷ 1,000**

- Homestead: $25,000 off everything + up to $25,000 more off **non-school** taxes on value above $50,000 (exam figure; indexed in law).
- Assessed $200,000 homestead: school taxable $175,000 × 7 mills = $1,225; non-school taxable $150,000 × 12 mills = $1,800 → **$3,025**.
- Check: school taxable = non-school taxable + $25,000 (when assessed is $75,000 or more).

## Appraisal and investment

- **Cost approach:** value = land + (cost new − accrued depreciation). Example: 2,000 sq ft × $150 = $300,000 − 10% depreciation ($30,000) + land $80,000 = **$350,000**.
- **Income approach (IRV):** Value = NOI ÷ cap rate · Rate = NOI ÷ value · NOI = value × rate. NOI $45,000 ÷ 9% = **$500,000**. Higher rate → lower value.
- **NOI** = potential gross − vacancy/collection loss = effective gross − operating expenses. **Never subtract mortgage payments or depreciation.**
- **GRM** = price ÷ **monthly** rent (houses, duplexes): $180,000 ÷ $1,500 = 120. **GIM** = price ÷ **annual** gross income.
- Comparables: **adjust the comp, not the subject.** Comp better → subtract; comp worse → add.
- Tax depreciation (a different concept): 27.5 years residential rental, 39 years nonresidential, straight-line, land never (V-77).

# Portugal vs Florida: the traps your experience sets {#portugal}

Your Portuguese practice helps you understand deals, and it is a risk on this exam. When an answer "feels normal", check it against the Florida rule below. **On the exam, only Florida and US federal law count.** The middle column is the instinct to watch for, not a statement of Portuguese law.

| Topic | The Portuguese instinct | The Florida exam answer |
|---|---|---|
| Who holds the license | The agency holds the IMPIC license; consultants work under it | **Every person** who earns pay for a real estate service needs their own license, active only while registered with a broker. Unlicensed practice = 3rd-degree felony |
| The regulator | One body (IMPIC) licenses and supervises | **DBPR investigates, FREC decides (rules, discipline), courts jail** |
| Getting licensed | Good standing + insurance | **63** hours pre-license → exam → **45** post-license before the first renewal (or the license becomes void) → **14** CE every 2 years |
| Working for both sides | Agencies often deal with buyer and seller in one sale | **No dual agency.** Default = **transaction broker** unless otherwise established in writing. "Dual agent" in an answer = wrong |
| Listing contract | Mediation contract on an approved model | Definite expiration date, **no automatic renewal**, copy to the seller within 24 hours. Exclusive right of sale → paid even if the owner sells; exclusive agency → not |
| Who pays the associate | Remuneration rules between agency and client | Money flows **only through the employing broker**. Associate paid directly by a customer or another broker = violation |
| Deposits | *Sinal*: defaulting buyer loses it, defaulting seller returns **double** | **No double-deposit rule.** Escrow clocks: associate → broker by end of next business day; broker deposits by end of the 3rd business day (count **business** days) |
| Two contracts | CPCV, then *escritura* and registration | **One** contract for sale and purchase → buyer has **equitable title**; legal title passes when the deed is **delivered and accepted** |
| Transfer taxes | Buyer pays IMT and stamp duty, progressive brackets | Flat per $100, **rounded up**. Deed stamps $0.70 = **seller** (custom); note stamps $0.35 + intangible 2 mills = **buyer**, new money only |
| Annual property tax | IMI on the registered value | **Mills × taxable value ÷ 1,000**, after exemptions; school and non-school computed separately |
| Proving ownership | Land registry | Recording gives **constructive notice** but is not needed for a valid deed. Title insurance covers **past** defects |
| Deeds | Public deed, no warranty grades | **General > special > bargain and sale > quitclaim.** Two witnesses needed; the grantee does not sign; notary is for recording |
| Married co-owners | Marital property regime decides | Florida is **not** community property. Married + silent deed → **tenancy by the entireties**; unmarried + silent → **tenancy in common** |
| Apartments | *Propriedade horizontal* | Condo = real property (deed). **Co-op = shares + proprietary lease = personal property** |
| Lifetime rights | *Usufruto* | Life estate: to a third party after death = **remainder**; back to the grantor = **reversion** |
| Family home | Spouse consent for the family home | Homestead = **three separate rules**: creditor shield, limits on transfer by will, tax exemption. None stops property taxes or the home's own mortgage |
| Units | m² and hectares | Feet and acres only. **1 acre = 43,560 sq ft** ≈ 4,047 m²; 1 mile = 5,280 ft; 1,000 sq ft ≈ 93 m² |
| Loan affordability | Bank effort rate on **net** income | Ratios use **gross** monthly income; PITI includes taxes and insurance |
| Foreclosure | Court enforcement | Judicial. Borrower may redeem **until the sale**; no redemption after it |
| Discrimination | General equality rules | Exact US protected classes; **no exemption ever for race** (1866 Act). Know steering, blockbusting, redlining |
| Advertising | License identification | **Brokerage name** in every ad, including personal social media. Missing = blind ad |
| Lease notices | Portuguese notice periods | Ending a tenancy without a term: **60 / 30 / 30 / 7** days (year / quarter / month / week, s. 83.57) |
| Energy | Energy certificate to sell or rent | Florida: the buyer gets **information** about an optional energy rating, not a certificate |

**Rule for every doubtful question:** if your answer depends on how it works in Portugal, stop and pick the answer that matches the Florida statute or FREC rule.

# Exam-day tactics {#tactics}

## The format (V-01, V-80)

- **100 multiple-choice questions · 3 h 30 · 75 correct to pass.** That is about **2 minutes per question**: plenty of time if you do not get stuck.
- The exam may include **pilot questions** that do not count toward your score; you get extra time for them. You cannot tell which ones they are, so answer every question as if it counts.
- A blank answer can never be right: **never leave a question blank.**
- Cancelling: contact Pearson VUE at least **2 days** before the appointment, or you may lose the fee.

## How to read each question

1. **Read to the last word** before looking at the choices. Note the actor (sales associate, broker, FREC, DBPR) and the moment (before, at, after closing).
2. Watch **NOT, EXCEPT, LEAST, FIRST, MOST**. Rephrase: "Which one is false?"
3. Watch legal qualifiers: **business days vs calendar days**, "in writing", "unless", "may" vs "must".
4. Answer with **current Florida law**, never Portuguese practice, never "what usually happens".
5. Eliminate first. An answer with *always* or *never* is right only when the rule really is absolute (race discrimination, dual agency).
6. Math: write the formula, plug in, compute, then **check the answer backwards**. If your answer is not among the choices, re-read the question; do not pick the closest number.
7. Stuck after about 2 minutes → choose your best answer, **flag it**, move on. Return at the end with fresh eyes.
8. Change an answer only if you find a concrete reason (a word you misread, a rule you remembered). Do not change it on a feeling.

## Inside the exam room

- First pass: answer everything you know quickly and flag the rest.
- Second pass: flagged questions and calculations.
- Last 10 minutes: make sure **every** question has an answer.

## The day before and the morning of the exam

- Confirm in the Candidate Information Booklet what to bring (ID and the course-completion documents it lists), the address and the arrival time.
- Light review only: the memory sheets you marked, the numbers table and the formula pages. Sleep early.
- Exam morning: a normal breakfast, arrive early, no new material.
