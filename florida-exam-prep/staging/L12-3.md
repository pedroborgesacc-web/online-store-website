## 2. Why it matters

Before a buyer falls in love with a house, someone must answer "can they borrow enough?" Lenders answer with two ratios. If you can work them out quickly, you can steer buyers to a realistic price range and understand a lender's "no". Knowing Florida's interest ceiling and the names of the predatory practices also lets you warn homeowners about abusive loan offers.

On the exam, ratio questions are common calculation items (you are given income and payments and asked whether the borrower qualifies), and the usury figures (18%, 25%, $500,000) are exact recall. Residential Mortgages is about 9% of the exam (V-02).

## 3. Simple explanation

> **Source status.** The qualifying ratios and their benchmarks (28/36 and 31/43) are **course conventions** from the study manual (manual; not yet verified, V-26). They are teaching benchmarks, not statutes, and real loan-program rules change over time. Florida's usury limits are verified against the statutes (V-72). The names of the predatory practices and the SAFE Act requirement come from the manual (manual; not yet verified).

### Step 1: What it represents

A lender asks two questions:

1. **Housing expense ratio:** what share of **gross** monthly income goes to the house payment (**PITI**)?
2. **Total obligations ratio:** what share goes to the house payment **plus** all other recurring debts?

**PITI** means Principal + Interest + (property) Taxes + (homeowner's) Insurance. **Gross** income is income before taxes and deductions, not take-home pay.

**Usury** is a separate test: is the loan's annual simple interest rate above Florida's legal ceiling?

### Step 2: Units

| Quantity | Unit | Conversion |
|---|---|---|
| Income | US$ per **month**, **gross** | Annual ÷ 12 |
| Property taxes, insurance | $ per month | Often quoted yearly, so divide by 12 |
| Principal and interest, recurring debts | $ per month | Usually given monthly |
| Ratios | Percent | Decimal × 100. Round to one decimal place only at the end |
| Usury | Percent **per year, simple interest**; loan size = principal in $ | n/a |

Aside: ratios have no units. Just keep every amount monthly and in dollars. There is no metric conversion here.

### Step 3: Formula

- **Housing expense ratio** = PITI ÷ gross monthly income
- **Total obligations ratio** = (PITI + recurring monthly debts) ÷ gross monthly income

For "maximum" questions:

- From the housing ratio: maximum PITI = income × housing benchmark.
- From the total ratio: maximum PITI = (income × total benchmark) − recurring debts.
- **The maximum PITI allowed is the lower of the two.**

**Usury check:** annual simple rate = annual interest ÷ principal. Compare it with the ceiling for that loan size.

### Step 4: When it applies

**Benchmarks (course conventions; manual; not yet verified, V-26)**

| Loan type | Housing expense ratio | Total obligations ratio |
|---|---|---|
| Conventional | 28% | 36% |
| FHA | 31% | 43% |

The borrower must meet **both** ratios. A ratio exactly at the benchmark passes, because the limit is "should not exceed". The manual's example below qualifies at exactly 36%.

**Florida usury (verified, V-72)**

- **Loans of $500,000 or less:** interest above the equivalent of **18% per year simple interest** is usurious and unlawful (ss. 687.02(1), 687.03(1), Fla. Stat.).
- **Loans over $500,000** (a loan, advance, line of credit or forbearance): interest is not usurious unless it exceeds the s. 687.071 rate, which in effect makes the ceiling **25%**.
  - A loan counts as over $500,000 if the principal **initially exceeds** that amount **or may reasonably be expected to exceed it** during the term (s. 687.03(3)).
- **Criminal usury** (s. 687.071), unless otherwise allowed by law. Willfully and knowingly charging:
  - **more than 25% but not more than 45%** a year is a **second-degree misdemeanor**;
  - **more than 45%** is a **third-degree felony**.
- Knowingly making an **extortionate extension of credit** is a **second-degree felony**.
- Credit extended in violation of s. 687.071 is **not an enforceable debt** in Florida.

**Predatory lending (manual; not yet verified).** The exam names four practices:

- **equity stripping**: lending that drains the owner's equity;
- **loan flipping**: repeated refinancing to collect fees;
- **packing**: hidden add-on products;
- **balloon abuse**: misuse of a balloon, which is a lump final payment.

The federal **SAFE Act** requires mortgage loan originators to be licensed and registered through **NMLS**.

### Step 5: Worked example

**Example A: buyers in Tampa.** Their gross income is $102,000 a year. Principal and interest are $1,780 a month, taxes $4,800 a year and insurance $3,240 a year. They pay $520 a month on a car and $90 a month in credit-card minimums.

1. Gross monthly income: 102,000 ÷ 12 = **$8,500**
2. Taxes: 4,800 ÷ 12 = $400. Insurance: 3,240 ÷ 12 = $270
3. PITI: 1,780 + 400 + 270 = **$2,450**
4. Housing ratio: 2,450 ÷ 8,500 = 0.2882 → **28.8%**
5. Total: 2,450 + 520 + 90 = 3,060. Then 3,060 ÷ 8,500 = 0.36 → **36.0%**
6. **Conventional:** 28.8 > 28 ✗ and 36.0 ≤ 36 ✓, so they **do not qualify**. **FHA:** 28.8 ≤ 31 ✓ and 36.0 ≤ 43 ✓, so they **qualify**.
7. Maximum conventional PITI: 28% × 8,500 = $2,380. The total ratio would allow 36% × 8,500 − 610 = $2,450. The lower is **$2,380**, so the PITI must fall by $70.

**Example B (study manual).** Gross monthly income $7,500; PITI $2,025; car and cards $675.

- Housing: 2,025 ÷ 7,500 = **27%** ✓ (≤ 28).
- Total: 2,700 ÷ 7,500 = **36%** ✓ (≤ 36). The borrower only just qualifies.
- With $750 of car and card payments instead: 2,775 ÷ 7,500 = **37%** ✗.

### Step 6: Reasonableness check

- A result over 100% means you divided upside down. A result of 2% or 3% usually means you used **annual** income.
- The total ratio is **always at least** the housing ratio.
- Multiply back: 8,500 × 0.288 ≈ 2,448, close to the $2,450 PITI ✓.
- Boundaries matter. In Example B, $75 more of debt turns a pass into a fail.

### Step 7: Common errors

- Dividing by **net** or **annual** income instead of gross monthly income.
- Leaving **taxes and insurance** out of PITI, or the recurring debts out of the total ratio.
- Rounding 36.4% down to "36%" so the borrower appears to qualify.
- Mixing up the conventional and FHA benchmarks, or checking only one ratio.
- Applying 18% to a loan over $500,000, or forgetting that a loan **expected** to exceed $500,000 counts as over.

**G2.** The same borrower pays $380 for a car and $120 in credit-card minimums. Find the total ratio. Does the borrower qualify conventionally? *Hint: PITI plus every recurring debt.*

<details><summary>Check your answer</summary>

1,890 + 380 + 120 = $2,390. Then 2,390 ÷ 6,800 = 0.3515 → **35.1%**. Both ratios are within 28/36, so **yes**.

</details>

### Step 9: Independent practice

**I1.** Gross monthly income $9,200; PITI $2,530; car $610; card minimums $140. Does the borrower qualify conventionally?

<details><summary>Check your answer</summary>

Housing: 2,530 ÷ 9,200 = **27.5%** ✓. Total: 3,280 ÷ 9,200 = 0.3565 → **35.7%** ✓. **Qualifies.**

</details>

**I2.** Gross income $87,000 a year; P&I $1,690; taxes $3,960 a year; insurance $2,280 a year; other debts $820 a month. Conventional, FHA, both or neither?

<details><summary>Check your answer</summary>

Monthly income $7,250. PITI = 1,690 + 330 + 190 = **$2,210**. Housing: 2,210 ÷ 7,250 = 0.3048 → **30.5%**. Total: 3,030 ÷ 7,250 = 0.4179 → **41.8%**. Conventional fails both ratios; FHA passes both (≤ 31, ≤ 43). Answer: **FHA only**.

</details>

**I3.** Gross monthly income $10,000; PITI $2,600. Under the conventional benchmarks, what is the most the borrower may pay on other monthly debts?

<details><summary>Check your answer</summary>

Housing: 26% ✓. Total limit: 36% × 10,000 = $3,600, minus 2,600 = **$1,000**.

</details>

**I4.** A private Florida lender makes a $350,000 loan at 21% per year simple interest, and no other law allows that rate. Is this usury? Is it criminal usury?

<details><summary>Check your answer</summary>

The loan is $500,000 or less, so the ceiling is 18%. At 21% it is **usurious and unlawful**: $73,500 a year of interest (350,000 × 0.21) against a $63,000 maximum (350,000 × 0.18). It is **not criminal usury**, because the rate is not above 25%.

</details>

**I5.** A line of credit starts at $460,000 but is reasonably expected to reach $540,000 during its term. Its rate is 24% simple. Is it usurious? And at 30%, charged willfully and knowingly?

<details><summary>Check your answer</summary>

It counts as **over $500,000** (s. 687.03(3)), so the ceiling is effectively 25%, and at 24% it is **not usurious**. At 30% (more than 25%, not more than 45%) it is criminal usury, a **second-degree misdemeanor**, unless otherwise allowed by law, and the credit is not an enforceable debt.

</details>

### Step 10: Exam application

**Ratios:**

1. Convert the income to **gross monthly**.
2. Build the full **PITI**.
3. Compute **both** ratios.
4. Compare them with the right set: conventional 28/36 or FHA 31/43. "At or below" qualifies.

For "maximum PITI" questions, keep the lower of the two limits. If a question states different benchmarks, use its numbers.

**Usury:**

1. Find the loan size, including any **expected** growth above $500,000.
2. Pick the ceiling: 18% or 25%.
3. If the rate is above 25%, place it in the criminal tiers.

## 4. Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| PITI | Principal, interest, taxes, insurance: the monthly housing payment |
| Gross monthly income | Income before taxes and deductions, per month |
| Housing expense ratio | PITI ÷ gross monthly income |
| Total obligations ratio | (PITI + recurring debts) ÷ gross monthly income |
| Recurring debts | Monthly debt payments, e.g. a car loan and credit-card minimums |
| Usury | Charging interest above the legal maximum |
| Criminal usury | Willfully and knowingly charging more than 25% a year, unless otherwise allowed by law (s. 687.071) |
| Loan flipping / packing | Repeated refinancing to collect fees / hidden add-on products |
| SAFE Act | Requires mortgage loan originators to be licensed and registered (NMLS) |

## 5. Practical example

**Marta, a nurse in Orlando**, earns **$90,000 a year gross**, and her take-home pay is about $5,700 a month. The home she wants has a PITI of **$1,980**, and she pays **$640** a month on a car and cards.

- **Correct (gross):** $90,000 ÷ 12 = $7,500. Housing: 1,980 ÷ 7,500 = **26.4%** ✓. Total: 2,620 ÷ 7,500 = 0.3493 → **34.9%** ✓. She qualifies under 28/36.
- **Wrong (take-home):** 1,980 ÷ 5,700 = 34.7% and 2,620 ÷ 5,700 = 46.0%. These figures would wrongly make her look unqualified, even under FHA.

**Her neighbor's phone call.** A caller offers her neighbor his **third refinance in two years**, each with large fees (**loan flipping**). The fees drain his equity (**equity stripping**), the paperwork hides an add-on product (**packing**), and a low monthly payment ends in a huge final payment he could not make (**balloon abuse**).

The caller also offers a $60,000 second mortgage at **22%** simple interest. The loan is $500,000 or less, so the ceiling is 18%. At 22% it is **usurious** unless otherwise allowed by law: $13,200 a year of interest against a maximum of $10,800. It is not criminal usury, because the rate is not above 25%.

Marta suggests he check the originator in **NMLS** (SAFE Act) before signing anything.

## 6. Portugal comparison

**Familiar principle.** The course's Portuguese entry says Portuguese banks apply a debt-service-to-income limit (*taxa de esforço*) on **net** income under Banco de Portugal macroprudential measures. Your own Drive guide says the limit was lowered from 50% to 45% in May 2026. All of this **requires verification** (PT-18).

**Florida rule.** The exam convention divides PITI (housing, 28%) and PITI plus recurring debt (total, 36%) by **gross** monthly income. FHA uses 31/43.

**Precise difference.** Gross vs net income is the trap, and PITI includes taxes and insurance. For example, take gross income of $8,000 a month, PITI of $2,200 and a $500 car payment. The housing ratio is 2,200 ÷ 8,000 = **27.5%** and the total ratio is 2,700 ÷ 8,000 = **33.75%**. That borrower qualifies conventionally.

**Exam implication.** Use gross income, and include taxes and insurance in PITI. Never use a Portuguese percentage or net income in a Florida answer. "Florida divides by GROSS."

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

**Memorize exactly**

- Housing expense ratio = **PITI ÷ gross monthly income**. Total obligations ratio = **(PITI + recurring debts) ÷ gross monthly income**.
- **Conventional 28% / 36%** and **FHA 31% / 43%**: course conventions, not statutes (manual; not yet verified, V-26).
- PITI = principal, interest, **taxes, insurance**.
- Usury: **18% per year simple interest** on loans of **$500,000 or less**. In effect **25%** on loans over $500,000, including loans whose principal may reasonably be expected to exceed it during the term (ss. 687.02(1), 687.03(1) and (3); verified, V-72).
- Criminal usury, unless otherwise allowed by law (s. 687.071(2)-(4); verified, V-72):
  - willfully and knowingly charging **more than 25% up to 45%** is a **second-degree misdemeanor**;
  - **more than 45%** is a **third-degree felony**;
  - an extortionate extension of credit is a **second-degree felony**;
  - such credit is **not an enforceable debt**.
- Predatory practices: equity stripping, loan flipping, packing, balloon abuse. The **SAFE Act** requires mortgage loan originators to be licensed and registered (NMLS) (manual; not yet verified).

**Understand**

- Pass **both** ratios. Exactly at the benchmark passes.
- The maximum PITI is the **lower** of the two limits.
- Above 18% on a small loan is unlawful (civil usury). The **criminal** tiers begin only above 25%.
- The benchmarks are teaching conventions. If a question gives other figures, use them.

| Annual simple rate | Loan of $500,000 or less | Loan over $500,000 (or expected to exceed it) |

## 9. Common misconceptions

- **"Use take-home pay."** No. Divide by **gross** monthly income.
- **"The house payment is P&I."** No. PITI adds **taxes and insurance**.
- **"28/36 is Florida law."** No. These are course conventions, not statutes.
- **"FHA uses 28/36 too."** No. The FHA benchmarks are **31/43**.
- **"18% caps every Florida loan."** No. Loans over $500,000, including those expected to exceed it, have an effective ceiling of **25%**.
- **"Anything above 18% is a crime."** No. The criminal tiers start **above 25%**.
- **"The Portuguese 45% limit applies here."** No. That figure requires verification, uses net income and has no place in a Florida answer.

## 10. Memory sheet

- **Housing** = PITI ÷ **gross** monthly income. **Total** = (PITI + recurring debts) ÷ gross.
- **28/36 conventional · 31/43 FHA**: course conventions (manual; not yet verified). Pass **both**; meeting the benchmark exactly is OK.
- **PITI includes taxes and insurance.** Annual figures ÷ 12.
- Maximum PITI = the **lower** of (income × housing %) and (income × total % − debts).
- **Usury: 18%** simple for loans of $500,000 or less · **25%** above (including loans expected to exceed it).
- **Criminal** (willful and knowing, unless otherwise allowed by law): above 25% up to 45% = 2nd-degree misdemeanor · above 45% = 3rd-degree felony · extortionate credit = 2nd-degree felony · not enforceable.
- **Predatory:** equity stripping, loan flipping, packing, balloon abuse. **SAFE Act**: originators licensed and registered (NMLS).
