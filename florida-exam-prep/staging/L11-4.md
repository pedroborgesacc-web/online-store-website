## 2. Why it matters

The standard Florida residential sale contract is the FR/BAR form, which has an AS IS version. The **effective date** starts every deadline, and "time is of the essence" makes those deadlines strict. Around the contract sits a set of required disclosures, several recent or renamed, so older textbooks are out of date. And a seller who thinks "AS IS" allows silence about a known hidden defect risks liability.

On the exam (Section 11, 12% of the questions, V-02), expect:

- What is the effective date? (Not the first offer, not closing.)
- Which buildings need the radon notice? (Any building, sale or rental.)
- Lead-based paint: housing built before which year? (1978.) Flood disclosure: when? (At or before contract.)
- An AS IS seller hid a known latent defect. Liable? (He may be: *Johnson v. Davis*.)

## 3. Simple explanation

**Source status.** The descriptions of the FR/BAR forms, the effective date, "time is of the essence" and contingencies come from the study manual *(manual; not yet verified)*. The disclosures in Step 4 are verified against statutes and federal rules (V-ids given), except the customary seller's disclosure.

### Step 1: The FR/BAR contract and its AS IS version

**FR/BAR** stands for Florida Realtors / Florida Bar. It is the standard residential sale contract, and it has an **AS IS** version *(manual; not yet verified)*:

- The buyer takes the property in its **current condition**; the seller makes no repairs.
- The buyer keeps a **due-diligence right to inspect and cancel** within the inspection period.
- The seller's duty to disclose **known latent defects** (hidden defects) **survives** AS IS (*Johnson v. Davis*, Step 5).

Why both? AS IS gives the seller certainty about repairs; the inspection right keeps the buyer from buying blind.

### Step 2: The effective date and "time is of the essence"

The **effective date** is the date the **last party signs or initials the final offer or counteroffer** and it is delivered (communicated) to the other side. **Every contract deadline counts from it**: inspection, financing and the rest *(manual; not yet verified)*. Before that moment there is no contract, so no clock can start.

**"Time is of the essence"** is a clause that makes every deadline strictly enforceable: missing a deadline by a single day is a breach *(manual; not yet verified)*. Calendar every deadline on the effective date.

### Step 3: Contingencies

A **contingency** is a condition that must be met for the contract to go forward. Standard ones: **financing, appraisal, inspection, sale of the buyer's home**. If a contingency is not met, the protected party may cancel and the deposit is returned *(manual; not yet verified)*. For escrow: a buyer's good-faith failure to obtain financing is one of the statutory cases in which the broker may return the deposit without notifying FREC (V-16; see L05-2).

### Step 4: Required disclosures around the contract

| Disclosure | Applies to | When | Content | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Radon** | Sale or rental of **any building** | On at least one document signed at or before the sale contract or rental agreement | Statutory radon notice | s. 404.056(5); V-73 |
| **Lead-based paint** | Housing built **before 1978**, sales and leases | If disclosure comes after an offer, before the seller or lessor accepts it | EPA pamphlet; known lead paint and hazards; available records and reports; Lead Warning Statement and signed acknowledgments in the contract or lease | 40 CFR 745 Subpart F; V-60 |
| **Property tax disclosure summary** | Buyer of **residential** property | At or before contract | Do not rely on the seller's current taxes; a sale or improvements can raise the assessment | s. 689.261; V-75 |
| **Energy-efficiency rating information** | Buyer of a building | At or before signing the contract | Information on the **option** of an energy-efficiency rating (older books: "brochure") | s. 553.996; V-74 |
| **Flood disclosure** | Seller of **residential** real property | **At or before** contract execution | Prescribed form: flood insurance (homeowners' policies do not cover flood); past flood insurance claims; federal flood assistance received; known flood damage | s. 689.302; V-06 |
| **Condominium documents** | Condo buyers | Buyer's cancellation window | Developer sale: **15 days**. Resale: **7 days**, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays, after signing and receiving the documents (since July 1, 2025). Both rights end at closing | s. 718.503; V-08 |
| **HOA disclosure summary** | Buyers in an HOA community | **Before** the contract is signed | If late, buyer may void within **3 days** after receiving it or before closing, whichever is first; not waivable; ends at closing | s. 720.401; V-65 |
| **Seller's property disclosure** | Customary | — | Honesty required regardless; *Johnson v. Davis* applies with or without a form | manual; not yet verified |

**Notes on the table**

- **Flood (V-06, gap-filled).** Added by CS/CS/HB 1049 (2024). Its effective date was not confirmed in the verification pass. The manual only mentions "flood-zone awareness"; older books miss it. The verified rule is not limited to mapped flood zones. Separately, a residential **lease of 1 year or longer** needs the landlord's flood disclosure at or before signing (since October 1, 2025); shorter leases are not covered (V-07).
- **Condo resale: outdated figure.** Older books, including the manual's appendix, say **3 business days**. Current law is 7 days as above (V-08).
- **Property tax: why it matters.** A long-time homestead owner's assessed value may have been held down by the Save Our Homes cap (increase of no more than 3% or the CPI change, whichever is lower; V-67). The buyer's tax bill can be much higher than the seller's.
- **Lead-based paint details (V-60).** Excluded from "target housing": housing for the elderly or persons with disabilities, and 0-bedroom dwellings, unless a child under 6 lives or is expected to live there. Exempt: foreclosure sales; leases of 100 days or less with no renewal; leases of housing certified lead-free; renewals where disclosure was already made. **Buyers** get a **10-day** opportunity for a risk assessment or inspection before being bound, unless the parties agree in writing on a different period; the buyer may waive it in writing. **Tenants** get the disclosure but **not** the 10-day period. The seller also discloses to each agent, and agents must ensure compliance. HUD's identical rule is 24 CFR Part 35, Subpart A. More in L19-2.

### Step 5: *Johnson v. Davis*: AS IS shifts repairs, not disclosure

Under *Johnson v. Davis*, a seller must disclose **known** facts that **materially affect the value** of the property and are **not readily observable**. The duty applies with or without a disclosure form, and **AS IS does not excuse it**. AS IS limits the seller's **repair** obligations; it never permits concealing a known latent defect.

The duty has limits. A fact the seller does not know, or one that is readily observable, is outside this rule (though the seller must still be honest). By statute, the fact that a homicide, suicide or death occurred on the property, or that an occupant had HIV/AIDS, is **not** a material fact that must be disclosed (s. 689.25; V-47; see L04-4).

## 4. Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| FR/BAR contract | Standard Florida residential sale contract (Florida Realtors / Florida Bar) |
| AS IS contract | Seller makes no repairs; buyer may inspect and cancel within the inspection period |
| Effective date | Date the last party signs or initials the final offer or counteroffer and it is delivered; deadlines run from it |
| Time is of the essence | Clause making contract deadlines strict |
| Latent defect | A hidden defect, not readily observable |
| Material fact | A fact that significantly affects the property's value |
| Target housing | Housing built before 1978, for the lead-based paint rule |

## 5. Practical example

**An AS IS sale in Jacksonville.** Mr. Hale sells his 1972 house on the AS IS FR/BAR contract. He knows the septic drain field backs up after heavy rain; it is now the dry season and nothing is visible.

| Step | What happens | Analysis |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Priya offers on Monday. Mr. Hale counters on Tuesday. Priya initials the counteroffer on Thursday evening, and it is delivered to Mr. Hale's agent the same evening. | **Effective date: Thursday.** All deadlines count from it *(manual; not yet verified)*. |
| 2 | At or before contract: radon notice, flood disclosure form, property tax summary, energy-rating information. | Required (V-73, V-06, V-75, V-74). |
| 3 | Built in 1972: target housing. | Lead pamphlet, disclosures and Lead Warning Statement. Priya gets a 10-day inspection opportunity unless a different period is agreed in writing or she waives it in writing (V-60). |
| 4 | Priya's inspector finds nothing in the dry weather. Variation: she sends a cancellation notice one day after the inspection period ends. | Too late: time is of the essence, so the deadline is strict *(manual; not yet verified)*. |
| 5 | After closing, the rains come and sewage backs up. Mr. Hale knew. | He **may be liable**: a known, material, not readily observable defect (*Johnson v. Davis*). "AS IS" is no defense to concealment. |
| 6 | A previous owner died of natural causes in the house. | Not a material fact that must be disclosed (s. 689.25, V-47). |

## 6. Portugal comparison

**The familiar principle.** In Portugal a CPCV is typically signed first, and the sale is later formalized by escritura pública or documento particular autenticado and registered (PT-08; details require verification). An energy certificate (certificado energético) is required to sell or rent (PT-23; requires verification).

**The Florida rule.** One contract, usually the FR/BAR, is the binding purchase agreement. Once the last party initials the final counteroffer, the effective date starts the inspection and financing deadlines, and "time is of the essence" makes each strict (PT-08). For energy, the buyer must receive **information about the option** of an energy-efficiency rating at or before contract (s. 553.996; V-74; PT-23).

**The precise difference.** Florida has no separate promise contract: deadlines run from the effective date of the one contract. Florida's energy rule is information, not a certificate. The course sources have no Portuguese comparison for the radon, lead, flood or property-tax disclosures; any Portuguese equivalent requires verification.

**Exam implication.** A Florida seller does **not** have to obtain an energy rating before listing (PT-23). Memory aid: *Florida: information, not certificate.*

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

**Memorize exactly**

- Effective date = last party signs or initials the final offer or counteroffer, and it is delivered; all deadlines run from it. Time is of the essence = deadlines strict *(manual; not yet verified)*.
- Radon: any building, sale or rental; at or before contract (V-73).
- Lead-based paint: built **before 1978**; buyer's **10-day** inspection opportunity, unless a different period is agreed in writing or the buyer waives it in writing; tenants get no 10-day period (V-60).
- Property tax summary (residential) and energy-rating information: at or before contract (V-75, V-74).
- Flood disclosure: residential seller, **at or before** contract execution, s. 689.302 (V-06). Leases of 1 year or longer: since October 1, 2025 (V-07).
- Condo cancellation: developer **15 days**; resale **7 days** excluding weekends and legal holidays (older books: 3 business days, outdated). HOA summary late → **3 days** or closing, whichever first (V-08, V-65).
- *Johnson v. Davis*: known, material, not readily observable → disclose, even AS IS.

**Understand**

- AS IS moves repair costs to the buyer, not the duty of honesty.
- There is no contract until the last signature is delivered, so no deadline can start before the effective date.
- Stigmas (death, homicide, suicide, HIV/AIDS) are not material facts that must be disclosed (V-47).

## 9. Common misconceptions

- **"AS IS means the seller can stay silent."** No. AS IS shifts repairs, never the duty to disclose known hidden defects (*Johnson v. Davis*).
- **"The effective date is the date of the first offer, or of closing."** No. It is when the last party signs or initials the final offer or counteroffer and it is delivered.
- **"Radon notices are needed only for older homes or when radon is found."** No. Every sale or rental of any building (V-73).
- **"Tenants also get the 10-day lead inspection period."** No. Buyers only; tenants get the disclosure (V-60).
- **"The flood disclosure is only for flood zones, or on request."** No. Residential sellers give it at or before contract (V-06).
- **"Florida sellers must obtain an energy rating."** No. The buyer receives information about the option of a rating (V-74).
- **"Condo resale buyers have 3 business days."** Outdated. Now 7 days, excluding weekends and legal holidays (V-08).

- **AS IS shifts repairs, not disclosure.** Known + material + hidden → disclose (*Johnson v. Davis*).
- **Effective date starts the clocks**; time is of the essence makes them strict *(manual; not yet verified)*.
