## 2. Why it matters

When a case reaches the FREC's final order (L06-1), the FREC chooses from a fixed menu of penalties with exact limits. The exam tests those limits (**$5,000 per count**, **10 years** of suspension), the line between **administrative** penalties (FREC) and **crimes** (courts), and the labels for misconduct: fraud, misrepresentation, concealment, false promise, culpable negligence, breach of trust.

This is part of Section 6 of the exam outline (3%, shared with L06-1 and L06-3). In practice, one bad habit can become several counts, each with its own fine.

## 3. Simple explanation

### Three separate tracks

| Track | Who decides | Possible result |
|---|---|---|
| Administrative (the license) | FREC, after the L06-1 process | Fine, probation, suspension, revocation and others; fines go to the state |
| Criminal | Criminal court | Jail and criminal fines |
| Civil | Civil court | Damages paid to the harmed person (and possibly the Recovery Fund, L06-3) |

The FREC never imprisons anyone and never awards damages to consumers. The tracks can run together: breaking the rules on selling rental information to prospective tenants is a crime **and** also exposes the license to discipline (s. 475.453; V-21).

### The administrative penalty menu

Under **s. 475.25(1), F.S.** (verified, V-41) the FREC may deny an application or renewal, issue a reprimand, fine up to **$5,000 for each count or separate offense**, place a licensee on probation, suspend a license for up to **10 years**, or revoke it.

| Penalty | Meaning | Limit or note |
|---|---|---|
| Notice of noncompliance | Written notice for a first minor violation, letting the licensee correct it | Manual; not yet verified |
| Citation | A set fine for a listed minor violation, instead of formal proceedings | Manual; not yet verified |
| Reprimand | Formal written disapproval | — |
| Administrative fine | A money penalty; the manual says it is paid to the state | Up to **$5,000 per count** |
| Probation | Continued work under FREC conditions | — |
| Required education | Courses the FREC orders the licensee to complete | Manual; not yet verified |
| Suspension | The license exists but cannot be used | Up to **10 years** |
| Revocation | The license is cancelled | Permanent, with narrow exceptions (manual; not yet verified) |
| Denial | Application or renewal refused | — |

A **count** is one separate violation charged. Three counts mean fines of up to 3 × $5,000 = **$15,000**.

### Criminal penalties (courts only)

| Conduct | Classification | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Operating as a broker or sales associate without a **valid, current, active** license | **Third-degree felony**: up to 5 years and $5,000 under the general sentencing statutes | s. 475.42(1)(a) (V-12) |
| Any other violation of s. 475.42(1) | **Second-degree misdemeanor**, unless Chapter 475 sets a different punishment | s. 475.42(2) (V-12) |
| Violating the rules on selling rental information lists to prospective tenants (for example, not making the required refunds) | **First-degree misdemeanor**, plus license discipline | s. 475.453 (V-21) |

A **felony** is the more serious class of crime; a **misdemeanor** the less serious. Note "valid, current, **active**": the felony is not limited to people who never held a license.

### The violation vocabulary

The FREC may discipline a licensee who commits these **in any business transaction**, not only in real estate (s. 475.25; manual; not yet verified). A licensee who cheats partners in a restaurant venture answers to the FREC too.

| Term | Meaning | Florida example |
|---|---|---|
| Fraud | Intentional deception | Inventing a "second offer" to push a Tampa buyer to pay more |
| Misrepresentation | False statement of a material fact | "The roof was replaced last year," when it is 15 years old |
| Concealment | Hiding a material fact | A rug placed over a rotten floor section before showings |
| False promise | A promise to induce action that the licensee does not intend to keep or cannot keep | "Sign today and the seller will pay all your closing costs," when the seller already refused |
| False pretenses | Pretending a false situation exists to obtain something | Charging an owner a "marketing fee" by pretending a buyer is lined up |
| Dishonest dealing by trick or scheme | A planned arrangement to cheat | Two contracts for one sale, one with a false price for the lender |
| Culpable negligence | Reckless indifference to others' rights: more than carelessness; no intent to deceive is needed | Again and again leaving deposit checks in the car for days because "nothing has gone wrong yet" |
| Breach of trust | Betraying the confidence a principal placed in the licensee | Telling a buyer the seller's lowest price while acting as the seller's single agent |

**Puffing** ("the best sunset in Naples") is opinion, not misrepresentation. A one-time careless slip is **simple** negligence, not culpable negligence.

### Other named grounds

- **Commingling** (mixing personal or business funds with escrow funds). A broker may lawfully keep up to **$1,000** of personal or brokerage funds in a sales escrow account and **$5,000** in a property management escrow account (V-38).
- **Conversion** (using escrow funds for personal or unauthorized purposes).
- **Failure to account and deliver** money or property to the person entitled to it.
- Operating as a **broker** while licensed as a **sales associate**.
- Conviction of a crime involving **moral turpitude** (serious dishonesty or immorality).
- **Adjudication of mental incompetence** (manual wording: confinement in a mental institution).
- **Records** and **crime reporting**, below.

**Records (s. 475.5015; verified, V-40).** A broker keeps at least one legible copy of all books, accounts and records for **at least 5 years**, counted from receipt of entrusted funds or, if none, from the date any party signs the listing, offer, rental management agreement, lease or other agreement engaging the broker. If the records are involved in litigation: at least **2 years after** the case and any appeal end, and **never less than 5 years** in total.

**Reporting a crime (s. 455.227(1)(t); verified, V-35).** Within **30 days**, in writing, to the FREC: any conviction or finding of guilt, or plea of guilty or **nolo contendere** ("no contest"), to **any crime** in any jurisdiction, **regardless of adjudication** (even if the court "withholds adjudication", that is, does not formally enter a conviction). Chapter 475's own rule (s. 475.25(1)(p)) covers only felonies.

## 4. Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Count | One separate violation charged; fines apply per count |
| Reprimand | Formal written disapproval on the licensee's record |
| Felony / misdemeanor | More serious / less serious class of crime |
| Material fact | A fact that would affect a reasonable party's decision or the property's value |
| Puffing | Exaggerated sales opinion; not a statement of fact |
| Moral turpitude | Serious dishonesty or immorality |
| Nolo contendere | "No contest" plea: no admission of guilt, but the penalty is accepted |
| Withheld adjudication | The court does not formally enter a conviction; the crime must still be reported |

## 5. Practical example

**Bruno**, a sales associate in Fort Myers, faces a formal complaint with three counts:

1. On a Friday he received a $10,000 deposit check and kept it in his car until the next Thursday. He had to deliver it to his broker by the end of the next business day: Monday, if not a legal holiday (Rule 61J2-14.009; V-36).
2. He told the buyer, "The seller replaced the roof last year," knowing it is 15 years old: misrepresentation.
3. Two months ago he pleaded nolo contendere to a misdemeanor, adjudication withheld, and never told the FREC. Chapter 475's felony-only rule would not reach it; s. 455.227(1)(t) does.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Maximum total fine? | 3 counts × $5,000 = **$15,000** |
| Other FREC options? | Reprimand, probation, suspension up to 10 years, revocation |
| Can the FREC order him to pay the buyer for the roof? | No: the buyer must sue in civil court |
| Can the FREC jail him? | No: only a criminal court |

Bruno's unlicensed cousin shows homes for $150 per showing: operating without a license, a **third-degree felony** for a criminal court.

## 6. Portugal comparison

**Familiar principle.** A regulator supervises the sector. In Portugal this role is usually associated with IMPIC, I.P., which licenses real estate mediation businesses (*requires verification*: this course has no checked Portugal comparison for this topic).

**Florida rule.** Each licensee holds a personal license, and the FREC disciplines that personal license. Crimes go to the courts; unlicensed practice is a third-degree felony.

**Precise difference.** Whether the Portuguese license is held by the mediation business rather than by each consultant, how consultants inside an agency operate, and the types, amounts and procedure of Portuguese sanctions all *require verification*. In Florida the sales associate personally can be fined, and his or her own license suspended or revoked, for conduct in any business transaction.

**Exam implication.** Use only Florida's limits: $5,000 per count, 10 years, revocation by the FREC; jail only by a court. Never let a Portuguese rule answer a Florida question.

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

**Memorize exactly**

- FREC (s. 475.25(1); V-41): denial, reprimand, fine up to **$5,000 per count or separate offense**, probation, suspension up to **10 years**, revocation.
- No valid, current, active license: **third-degree felony**, up to 5 years and $5,000 (s. 475.42(1)(a); V-12).
- Other s. 475.42(1) violations: **second-degree misdemeanor**, unless the chapter sets a different punishment (s. 475.42(2); V-12).
- Rental information violations: **first-degree misdemeanor** plus license discipline (s. 475.453; V-21).
- Records: **5 years**; if litigated, **2 years** after the case and appeals end, never less than 5 in total (s. 475.5015; V-40).
- Crimes: written report within **30 days**; any crime; guilty or nolo contendere pleas included; regardless of adjudication (s. 455.227(1)(t); V-35).

**Understand**

- **Outdated figure:** the fine cap was **$1,000 per count** in the 2002 statute; it was $5,000 by 2010 and is $5,000 in the 2024 and 2025 editions. The 2026 edition's penalty text was not retrieved, but the excerpts checked showed no change (V-41).
- Manual-only (not yet verified): notice of noncompliance and how citations work; ordered education; exceptions to permanent revocation; that fines go to the state; first-degree misdemeanor penalties (up to $1,000 and 1 year); DBPR citations of up to $5,000 and injunctions against unlicensed persons; the violation vocabulary and the "any business transaction" reach; the commingling, conversion, failure to account and deliver, broker-while-associate, moral turpitude and mental incompetence grounds.
- Rule 61J2-14.012 also requires the broker to keep deposit slips, depository statements and agreements, and to reconcile escrow accounts monthly (V-40).

## 9. Common misconceptions

- **"The FREC can order restitution or jail."** Damages need a civil judgment; jail needs a criminal court.
- **"The maximum fine is $5,000 per case."** It is $5,000 **per count**.
- **"The fine cap is $1,000."** That was the 2002 cap. ($1,000 is also the personal-funds limit in a sales escrow account, a different rule.)
- **"Suspension and revocation are the same."** Suspension is temporary (up to 10 years); revocation cancels the license.
- **"Culpable negligence is any mistake."** It is reckless indifference, more than carelessness.
- **"Only real estate deals count."** The vocabulary applies in any business transaction.
- **"Only felony convictions must be reported."** Any crime, guilty and nolo contendere pleas included, regardless of adjudication.
- **"Records are kept 3 years."** At least 5; if litigation is involved, also at least 2 years after it and any appeal end.
- **"Unlicensed practice is a misdemeanor."** It is a third-degree felony.

## 10. Memory sheet

- **Fine 5K per count · suspend 10 · revoke = cancel · records 5 · report crimes in 30.**
- FREC: deny, reprimand, fine, probation, suspend, revoke. Never jail, never damages.
- No active license = **3rd-degree felony**. Other s. 475.42(1) = 2nd-degree misdemeanor. Rental lists = 1st-degree misdemeanor + license action.
- Fraud = intent · misrepresentation = false fact · concealment = hidden fact · false promise = promise to induce · culpable negligence = reckless · breach of trust = betrayed confidence.
- Any business transaction. Old $1,000 cap = outdated.
