## 2. Why it matters

**In practice.** Buyers ask "What will my taxes be?", and in Florida the honest answer is often "more than the seller pays": Save Our Homes may have held a long-time owner's assessed value far below market value. You must explain how a bill is built (value, exemptions, millage), make a rough estimate, and warn buyers not to rely on the seller's current taxes, as the property tax disclosure summary for residential buyers does (V-75).

**On the exam.** Section 18 is 3% of the exam (V-02), about 3 of 100 questions (V-01). This lesson supplies its math question and its traps: which taxes the second homestead exemption reduces, the current widow/blind/disabled amount, "3% or CPI, whichever is **less**", and a tax in mills with separate school and non-school values.

## 3. Simple explanation

*Labels: V-nn = checked against official sources on 2026-10-19/20. "(manual; not yet verified)" = from the study manual only. Values, millage rates and CPI changes in the examples are invented for practice.*

### Step 1: What it represents

A Florida property tax bill is built in layers:

| Layer | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Just value** | Market value, set by the county property appraiser as of January 1 (V-76; L18-1) |
| **Assessed value** | Where the tax starts. A homestead's can sit below just value, because Save Our Homes limits its growth (V-67) |
| **Exemptions** | Dollar amounts subtracted from assessed value, for owners who qualify |
| **Taxable value** | Assessed value − exemptions. A homestead has **two**: school and non-school |
| **Millage** | Each taxing authority's rate in mills (school board, county, city and others) |

**Homestead exemptions (s. 196.031; V-09).** The owner must own the property and occupy it as a permanent residence on **January 1** (N-140).

- The first **$25,000** of assessed value is exempt from **all** taxes, school taxes included.
- An **additional exemption of up to $25,000** applies to assessed value **above $50,000**, for **non-school taxes only**. Since **January 1, 2025** (Amendment 5, approved November 5, 2024) it is **indexed to CPI-U** (the US Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers, an inflation measure; see Step 2) when the change is positive: **$25,722 for 2025** (Florida DOR, 2.9% CPI). **Exam math usually uses the round $25,000.** Older books say "value between $50,000 and $75,000"; with indexing the top of that band moves ($75,722 for 2025).

**Why two taxable values?** The additional exemption does not apply to school levies, so school taxes are computed on a base that is normally $25,000 larger.

**Other exemptions**

- **$5,000** each for a widow or widower, a blind person, or a totally and permanently disabled person who is a Florida resident (s. 196.202; V-10). Raised from $500 by the 2022 Legislature, first for the 2023 tax roll: **$500 is outdated**, though older printings still show it.
- Disabled veterans (combat-disabled, or service-connected and totally disabled): larger or total exemptions; details not covered (manual; not yet verified).
- **Green Belt:** agricultural land is assessed on **use value** (its value as farmland), not market value (manual; not yet verified).

**Save Our Homes (SOH)** (Art. VII, s. 4(d), Fla. Const.; s. 193.155; V-67). After the January 1 reassessment, a homestead's assessed value may rise by no more than **3% of the prior year's assessed value or the percentage change in the CPI for the preceding calendar year, whichever is lower**. Why: an owner who stays should not be taxed out of the home by a hot market. Just value − assessed value = the **accrued SOH benefit**.

- **Reset:** on a change of ownership, assessed value resets to just value (N-142).
- **Portability:** an owner who sets up a new Florida homestead may transfer the benefit, **up to $500,000**, if the new homestead is established **within 3 years after January 1 of the year the old one was abandoned** and the owner had a homestead exemption on January 1 of any of the 3 preceding years; claimed on **Form DR-501T** with the homestead application (V-67). The 3-year window replaced 2 years from 2021: "2 years" is **outdated**.
- **Pending, not law (V-23; V-67):** CS/HJR 1-F (2026 Special Session F) is on the **November 3, 2026** ballot. If at least 60% of voters approve, from January 1, 2027 it would, among other things, let the **full** accrued benefit transfer for non-school levies (no $500,000 cap for those levies). Until then the 3%/CPI cap and the $500,000 cap remain current. A 2026 House analysis of HB 7031 mentions possible changes to which prior homestead can be ported from; enactment status not verified. Exam questions usually lag law changes.

### Step 2: Units

- **Dollars**, in US notation: $1,710.50 (comma for thousands, point for decimals).
- **Mill** = one-thousandth. **1 mill = $1 of tax per $1,000 of taxable value = 0.001** (manual; not yet verified; N-144). It works like per mille (‰), not like percent.

| Millage | Tax per $1,000 | Decimal | Percent |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 mill | $1.00 | 0.001 | 0.1% |
| 6 mills | $6.00 | 0.006 | 0.6% |
| 18.4 mills | $18.40 | 0.0184 | 1.84% |

- **Percent** for SOH (3% = 0.03). The CPI is the Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers, U.S. City Average (V-67).

### Step 3: Formula

1. **SOH maximum** = last year's assessed value × (1 + the **lower** of 3% or the CPI change).
2. **Additional exemption** = the smaller of $25,000 (or the indexed figure) and (assessed value − $50,000); zero at $50,000 or less.
3. **School taxable value** = assessed value − $25,000 (− any other exemption the question applies).
4. **Non-school taxable value** = assessed value − $25,000 − additional exemption (− any other exemption the question applies).
5. **Tax** = taxable value × mills ÷ 1,000, for each part; then add.

**Shortcut:** taxable value in thousands × mills = tax (285 × 6 = $1,710). With round figures and an assessed value of $75,000 or more: school taxable = assessed − $25,000; non-school taxable = assessed − $50,000.

### Step 4: When it applies

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Homestead | Two taxable values, as above |
| Not a homestead (rental, second home, vacant lot, store) | No homestead exemption, no SOH: assessed value × total millage (unless the question gives another exemption) |
| Widow/widower, blind, totally and permanently disabled | Also subtract $5,000. V-10 confirms the amount, not how each levy treats it: apply it as the question states |
| Last year's assessed value + market and CPI changes given | SOH first, then exemptions |
| Property sold | Reset to just value (N-142) at the next January 1 assessment (V-76); the buyer must qualify for homestead on their own (N-140) |
| Question gives $25,722 | Use it; otherwise $25,000 |

### Step 5: Worked example

**A. The manual's example.** A homestead is assessed at **$310,000**. School millage 6 mills; county plus city 9 mills.

| | School | Non-school |
|---|---|---|
| Assessed value | $310,000 | $310,000 |
| − first exemption | − $25,000 | − $25,000 |
| − additional exemption | does not apply | − $25,000 |
| **Taxable value** | **$285,000** | **$260,000** |
| × mills ÷ 1,000 | × 6 | × 9 |
| **Tax** | **$1,710** | **$2,340** |

Total **$4,050**. Second check with decimals: $285,000 × 0.006 = $1,710; $260,000 × 0.009 = $2,340 ✓.

With the indexed 2025 figure, non-school taxable is $310,000 − $25,000 − $25,722 = $259,278 → $2,333.50; total $4,043.50. Only $6.50 apart, so questions use $25,000 unless they give the indexed figure.

**B. The next year, with Save Our Homes.** Market value rose 8%; the CPI changed 2.4%. Cap = lower of 3% and 2.4% = **2.4%** (ignore the 8%). Maximum assessed value = $310,000 × 1.024 = **$317,440** (check: $310,000 × 0.024 = $7,440 ✓). Same millage: school $292,440 × 6 ÷ 1,000 = $1,754.64; non-school $267,440 × 9 ÷ 1,000 = $2,406.96; total **$4,161.60**.

### Step 6: Reasonableness check

- **School taxable ≥ non-school taxable**; at assessed values of $75,000 or more the gap is exactly $25,000 (round figures).
- **About $1 per $1,000 per mill:** $300,000 at 15 mills ≈ $4,500. Getting $45,000 means you divided by 100.
- **SOH adds at most $3 per $100** of last year's assessed value; more means you used the market rise or the higher rate.
- **An exemption saves exemption × mills ÷ 1,000:** $5,000 at 15 mills = $75.

### Step 7: Common errors

| Error | Correction |
|---|---|
| Taking $50,000 off school taxes | School gets only the first $25,000 |
| Full second exemption on a cheap home (assessed $62,000 → $25,000 off) | Only value above $50,000 counts: $12,000 |
| Dividing mills by 100 | Mills are per **1,000** |
| "Whichever is greater" (CPI 4% → 4%) | The **lower** one: 3% |
| Applying the cap to market value or to this year's value | Apply it to **last year's assessed value** |
| Subtracting $500 for a widow | $5,000 (V-10) |
| Estimating the buyer's tax on the seller's capped value | Resets to just value after a sale (N-142) |

### Step 10: Exam application

1. Homestead or not? SOH involved? Which exemption figures are given?
2. SOH first: the lower of 3% or CPI, on last year's assessed value.
3. Exemptions: $25,000 from both values; up to $25,000 more from non-school only; $5,000 if the question includes it.
4. Each taxable value × its mills ÷ 1,000; add. Round only at the end, to cents.

Wrong choices are built from the Step 7 errors. The annual tax is also what gets prorated at closing (L14-7): use the question's method, otherwise the course convention of a 365-day year with the buyer owning the closing day (V-79: unresolved; a course convention, not law).

## 4. Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Just value | Florida's term for market value, set as of January 1 |
| Assessed value | Value the tax starts from; for a homestead, capped by Save Our Homes |
| Taxable value | Assessed value − exemptions; school and non-school can differ |
| Homestead exemption | $25,000 off all taxes + up to $25,000 (CPI-indexed) off non-school taxes on value above $50,000 |
| Non-school levies | County, city and other local taxes (everything except the school board) |
| Mill / millage | 1 mill = $1 per $1,000 = 0.001 / a tax rate in mills |
| Save Our Homes (SOH) | Cap on a homestead's yearly assessed-value increase: 3% or CPI, whichever is less |
| Accrued SOH benefit | Just value − assessed value |
| Portability | Moving up to $500,000 of the benefit to a new Florida homestead (3-year window; Form DR-501T) |
| Green Belt | Agricultural land assessed on use value (manual; not yet verified) |

## 5. Practical example

**The Moreira house in Sarasota.** Homestead since 2010. On January 1, 2026: just value **$540,000**, assessed value **$275,000**. Millage: school 5.9, non-school 9.6. In June 2026 Daniel buys it and moves in. Assume a January 1, 2027 just value of $530,000, unchanged millage, and his own homestead exemption. Use the round $25,000 exemption figures.

| | Moreira 2026 bill | Daniel's 2027 estimate |
|---|---|---|
| Assessed value | $275,000 | $530,000 (reset to just value) |
| School | $250,000 × 5.9 ÷ 1,000 = $1,475.00 | $505,000 × 5.9 ÷ 1,000 = $2,979.50 |
| Non-school | $225,000 × 9.6 ÷ 1,000 = $2,160.00 | $480,000 × 9.6 ÷ 1,000 = $4,608.00 |
| **Total** | **$3,635.00** ($3,489.60 if paid in November with the 4% discount) | **$7,587.50** |

- The 2026 bill reflects the Moreiras' January 1, 2026 status and value (V-76) and is prorated at closing (L14-7).
- From 2027 the assessed value resets (N-142): Daniel's bill could be **more than twice** theirs even with his own homestead exemption, which is what his property tax disclosure summary warns (V-75). As a rental, with no homestead exemption: 530 × 15.5 = **$8,215**.
- The Moreiras move to a smaller home in Venice. Their benefit, $540,000 − $275,000 = **$265,000**, is under the $500,000 cap. Abandoning the old homestead in 2026, they must establish the new one within 3 years after January 1, 2026 and file Form DR-501T (V-67).

## 6. Portugal comparison

**Familiar principle.** Portugal also taxes property every year: **IMI** is an annual municipal tax on the registered taxable value (VPT) (PT-10; the course still marks the Portuguese side of this comparison "requires verification"). IMI rates and exemptions require verification.

**Florida rule.** The property appraiser sets just value as of January 1; taxing authorities set millage. Homesteads get the exemptions ($25,000 + up to $25,000 non-school) and the Save Our Homes cap (3% or CPI).

**Precise difference.** Florida computes the tax **in mills on taxable value after exemptions**, and school and non-school values can differ: a homestead assessed at $300,000 has school taxable $275,000 and non-school taxable $250,000 (round figures), because the additional exemption applies only to non-school levies (PT-10).

**Exam implication.** 1 mill = $1 per $1,000; run school and non-school separately when exemptions differ. Never answer a Florida question from IMI habits.

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

**Memorize exactly**

- Taxable value = assessed − exemptions. Tax = taxable value × mills ÷ 1,000. 1 mill = $1 per $1,000 = 0.001 (manual; not yet verified; N-144).
- Homestead: **$25,000** off **all** taxes + up to **$25,000** off **non-school** taxes on assessed value **above $50,000** (s. 196.031; V-09); CPI-indexed since 2025 (**$25,722** for 2025); exam math $25,000. Own and occupy as a permanent residence on January 1 (N-140).
- **$5,000** widow/widower, blind, totally and permanently disabled; Florida resident (s. 196.202; V-10). **$500 = outdated** (before the 2023 roll).
- Disabled veterans: larger or total exemptions. Green Belt: use value (manual; not yet verified).
- SOH: increase ≤ **3%** of prior assessed value or the **CPI** change, **whichever is lower** (Art. VII, s. 4(d); s. 193.155; V-67). Resets to just value on a change of ownership (N-142).
- Portability: up to **$500,000**; within **3 years** after January 1 of the year the old homestead was abandoned (**2 years = outdated**, before 2021); exemption held on January 1 of any of the 3 preceding years; Form **DR-501T** (V-67).
- CS/HJR 1-F (November 3, 2026 ballot; 60%; would apply from January 1, 2027): **not law** (V-23). HB 7031: status not verified.

**Understand**

- Exemptions reduce the value, never the tax directly.
- School taxable value is the larger one: the second exemption skips school levies.
- SOH caps assessed-value growth, not millage: a higher millage can still raise the bill.
- SOH protects the owner who stays; a sale resets it; portability lets the owner take part of it along.

## 9. Common misconceptions

- **"Homestead exempts the first $50,000 from all taxes."** No. $25,000 from all taxes; the second exemption covers value above $50,000, non-school only. Value from $25,000 to $50,000 gets no homestead exemption.
- **"The widow's exemption is $500."** Outdated; it is $5,000 (V-10).
- **"SOH is 3% or CPI, whichever is greater."** Whichever is **less**.
- **"SOH caps the tax bill."** It caps assessed-value growth; millage can still rise.
- **"The buyer keeps the seller's low assessed value."** It resets to just value (N-142).
- **"Portability has no limit."** Up to $500,000, within 3 years. The November 2026 measure would change this for non-school levies only if approved (V-23).
- **"A rental gets Save Our Homes."** No. SOH and the homestead exemption are for homesteads.
