## 2. Why it matters

**In practice.** Buyers always ask "What will my property taxes be?", and in Florida the answer is often "more than the seller pays": Save Our Homes may have held a long-time owner's assessed value down for years, and the homestead exemption depends on who owns and lives in the home on January 1. You must explain how a bill is built (value, exemptions, millage), make a quick estimate, and warn the buyer not to rely on the seller's current taxes, as the property tax disclosure summary given to buyers of residential property does (V-75).

**On the exam.** Section 18 (Taxes Affecting Real Estate) carries 3% of the exam (V-02), about 3 of the 100 questions (V-01). Expect: which taxes the second homestead exemption reduces, the current widow/blind/disabled amount, the Save Our Homes cap, and a tax computed in mills with separate school and non-school values.

## 3. Simple explanation

*Labels: V-nn = checked against official sources on 2026-10-19/20. "(manual; not yet verified)" = taken only from the study manual. Millage rates, CPI changes and property values in the examples are made up for practice.*

### Step 1: What it represents

A Florida property tax bill is built in layers.

| Layer | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Just value** | Florida's term for market value, set by the county property appraiser as of January 1 (V-76) |
| **Assessed value** | The value the tax starts from. For a homestead it can sit below just value, because Save Our Homes caps its yearly growth (V-67) |
| **Exemptions** | Fixed dollar amounts subtracted from assessed value; the owner must qualify |
| **Taxable value** | Assessed value − exemptions. A homestead has **two**: one for school taxes, one for non-school taxes |
| **Millage** | The tax rate in mills, set by each taxing authority (county, city, school board and others; L18-1) |

**The exemptions**

- **Homestead** (s. 196.031; V-09). The first **$25,000** of assessed value is exempt from **all** property taxes, school taxes included. An **additional exemption of up to $25,000** applies to assessed value **above $50,000**, for **non-school taxes only**. Since January 1, 2025 (Amendment 5, approved November 5, 2024) the additional exemption is **indexed to inflation** (CPI-U) when the change is positive: **$25,722 for 2025** (Florida Department of Revenue; a 2.9% CPI change). **Exam math usually uses the round $25,000.** Older books say the second exemption covers value "between $50,000 and $75,000"; with indexing, the top of that band moves ($75,722 for 2025). The owner must own the property and occupy it as a permanent residence on **January 1** (numbers database N-140).
- **$5,000** each for a widow or widower, a blind person, or a totally and permanently disabled person who is a Florida resident (s. 196.202; V-10). The 2022 Legislature raised it from $500, first applying to the 2023 tax roll. **$500 is outdated**, but older printings and question banks still show it.
- **Disabled veterans** (combat-disabled, service-connected totally disabled) receive larger or total exemptions; details are not covered (manual; not yet verified).
- **Green Belt:** agricultural land is assessed on its **use value** (its value as farmland), not market value (manual; not yet verified).

**Why two taxable values?** The law grants the additional homestead exemption against non-school levies only, so school taxes are computed on a larger base, normally $25,000 larger.

**Save Our Homes (SOH)** (Art. VII, s. 4(d), Fla. Const.; s. 193.155; V-67). After the January 1 reassessment, a homestead's assessed value may rise by no more than **3% of the prior year's assessed value or the percentage change in the CPI for the preceding calendar year, whichever is lower**. The cap shields an owner who stays in the home from sharp market-driven increases. When the market climbs faster than the cap, a gap opens between just value and assessed value: the **accrued SOH benefit**. SOH is a homestead rule; limits on other property are outside this course.

- **Reset.** On a change of ownership the assessed value resets to just value (N-142). The buyer starts over, so the buyer's bill can be much higher than the seller's (V-75).
- **Portability.** An owner who sets up a new Florida homestead can transfer the accrued benefit (just value − assessed value), **up to $500,000**. The new homestead must be established **within 3 years after January 1 of the year the old homestead was abandoned**, and the owner must have had a homestead exemption as of January 1 of any of the 3 preceding years. It is claimed on **Form DR-501T** with the homestead application (V-67). The window was 2 years before 2021: materials that say 2 years are **outdated**. How the benefit applies to the new home's value is the appraiser's calculation and beyond this course.
- **Pending, not law (V-23, V-67).** CS/HJR 1-F (2026 Special Session F) is on the **November 3, 2026** ballot. If at least 60% of voters approve, it takes effect January 1, 2027 and would, among other things, let the **full** accrued benefit transfer for non-school levies (no $500,000 cap for those levies). Until then, the 3%/CPI cap and the $500,000 cap are the law. A 2026 House analysis of HB 7031 mentions possible changes to which prior homestead can be ported from; its enactment status was not verified. Exam questions usually lag law changes.

### Step 2: Units

- **Dollars** for values, exemptions and tax, in US notation ($1,710.50).
- **Mill:** one-thousandth. **1 mill = $1 of tax per $1,000 of taxable value = 0.001** (a standard definition; manual; not yet verified, N-144). Same idea as "per mille" (‰).

| Millage | Tax per $1,000 | Decimal | Percent |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 mill | $1.00 | 0.001 | 0.1% |
| 9.5 mills | $9.50 | 0.0095 | 0.95% |
| 18.25 mills | $18.25 | 0.01825 | 1.825% |

- **Percent** for SOH (3% = 0.03). The CPI used is the Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers, U.S. City Average (V-67).

### Step 3: Formula

1. **SOH ceiling** = last year's assessed value × (1 + the **lower** of 3% or the CPI change).
2. **Additional exemption** = the smaller of $25,000 (or the indexed figure) and (assessed value − $50,000); zero at $50,000 or less.
3. **School taxable value** = assessed value − $25,000 (− any other exemption that applies).
4. **Non-school taxable value** = assessed value − $25,000 − additional exemption (− any other exemption).
5. **Tax** = taxable value × mills ÷ 1,000, for each; then add.

**Shortcut:** taxable value in thousands × mills = tax ($285,000 at 6 mills → 285 × 6 = $1,710). For any home assessed at $75,000 or more: school = assessed − $25,000; non-school = assessed − $50,000 (round figures).

### Step 4: When it applies

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Homestead (owned and occupied as a permanent residence on January 1) | Two taxable values, as above |
| No homestead (rental, second home, vacant lot, shop) | No homestead exemption, no SOH: assessed value × total millage |
| Widow/widower, blind, or totally and permanently disabled Florida resident | Subtract $5,000 too. V-10 confirms the amount, not how it is applied to each levy: follow the question's wording |
| Last year's assessed value plus market and CPI changes given | SOH first, then exemptions |
| Property just sold | Reset to just value (N-142) as of the next January 1 (V-76); the buyer must qualify for homestead on their own |
| Question gives $25,722 | Use it (2025); otherwise $25,000 |

### Step 5: Worked example

**A. The manual's example.** A homestead is assessed at **$310,000**. School millage 6 mills; county plus city 9 mills.

| | School | Non-school |
|---|---|---|
| Assessed value | $310,000 | $310,000 |
| − first exemption | − $25,000 | − $25,000 |
| − additional exemption | does not apply | − $25,000 |
| **Taxable value** | **$285,000** | **$260,000** |
| × mills ÷ 1,000 | × 6 | × 9 |
| **Tax** | **$1,710** | **$2,340** |

Total **$4,050**. Second check: $285,000 × 0.006 = $1,710; $260,000 × 0.009 = $2,340 ✓.

With the indexed 2025 figure: non-school taxable $310,000 − $25,000 − $25,722 = $259,278 × 9 ÷ 1,000 = $2,333.50; total $4,043.50. Only $6.50 apart, which is why questions use $25,000 unless they give the indexed figure.

**B. Next year, Save Our Homes.** The home's market value rose 8%; the CPI change was 2.4%. Cap = lower of 3% and 2.4% = **2.4%** (the 8% is irrelevant). New assessed value = $310,000 × 1.024 = **$317,440** (check: $310,000 × 0.024 = $7,440 ✓). Same millage: school 292,440 × 6 ÷ 1,000 = $1,754.64; non-school 267,440 × 9 ÷ 1,000 = $2,406.96; total **$4,161.60**.

### Step 6: Reasonableness check

- **School taxable ≥ non-school taxable**; above $75,000 of assessed value the gap is exactly $25,000 (round figures).
- **About $1 per $1,000 per mill.** $300,000 taxable at 15 mills ≈ $4,500. If you get $45,000, you divided by 100 instead of 1,000.
- **SOH moves at most $3 per $100.** A bigger jump means you used the market rise or the higher rate.
- **An exemption saves exemption × mills ÷ 1,000:** $5,000 at 15 mills saves $75.

### Step 7: Common errors

| Error | Correction |
|---|---|
| Taking $50,000 off school taxes | School gets only the first $25,000 |
| Full second exemption on a cheap home ($62,000 → $25,000 off) | Only value above $50,000 counts: $12,000 |
| Dividing mills by 100 | Mills are per **1,000** |
| "Whichever is greater" (CPI 4.1% → 4.1%) | The **lower**: 3% |
| Applying the cap to market value | Apply it to **last year's assessed value** |
| Subtracting $500 for a widow | $5,000 (V-10) |
| Buyer's estimate on the seller's capped value | Resets to just value on a sale (N-142) |

### Step 10: Exam application

1. Homestead or not? Is SOH needed? Which exemption figures does the question give?
2. SOH first: the lower of 3% or CPI, on last year's assessed value.
3. Exemptions: $25,000 from both values; up to $25,000 more from non-school only; $5,000 if the question includes it.
4. Each taxable value × its mills ÷ 1,000; add. Round only at the end (cents).

Wrong choices are built from the Step 7 errors. The annual tax you compute is the figure prorated at closing (L14-7; course convention, not law: 365-day year, buyer owns the day of closing, V-79).

## 4. Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Just value | Florida's term for market value, set as of January 1 |
| Assessed value | Value the tax starts from; for a homestead, capped by Save Our Homes |
| Taxable value | Assessed value minus exemptions; school and non-school can differ |
| Homestead exemption | $25,000 off all taxes + up to $25,000 (CPI-indexed) off non-school taxes on value above $50,000 |
| Mill / millage | 1 mill = $1 per $1,000 = 0.001 / the tax rate in mills |
| School vs non-school levies | School board taxes vs county, city and other local taxes |
| Save Our Homes (SOH) | Cap on a homestead's yearly assessed-value increase: 3% or CPI, whichever is less |
| CPI (CPI-U) | Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers, U.S. City Average |
| Accrued SOH benefit | Just value minus assessed value |
| Portability | Transfer of up to $500,000 of the benefit to a new Florida homestead established within 3 years after January 1 of the year the old one was abandoned |
| Form DR-501T | Form for claiming the portability transfer |
| Green Belt | Agricultural land assessed on use value (manual; not yet verified) |

## 5. Practical example

**The Lopes house in Sarasota.** The Lopes family has had a homestead exemption since 2009. On January 1, 2026: just value **$520,000**, assessed value **$260,000**. Millage: school 5.9, non-school 9.6. Daniel buys the house in June 2026 for $515,000 and moves in. Suppose its just value is $510,000 on January 1, 2027.

| | Lopes 2026 bill | Daniel's 2027 estimate (homestead) |
|---|---|---|
| Assessed value | $260,000 | $510,000 (reset to just value) |
| School | $235,000 × 5.9 ÷ 1,000 = $1,386.50 | $485,000 × 5.9 ÷ 1,000 = $2,861.50 |
| Non-school | $210,000 × 9.6 ÷ 1,000 = $2,016.00 | $460,000 × 9.6 ÷ 1,000 = $4,416.00 |
| **Total** | **$3,402.50** ($3,266.40 paid in November) | **$7,277.50** |

What you tell the parties:

- The 2026 bill reflects the Lopes family's January 1, 2026 status and value (V-76) and is prorated at closing (L14-7).
- From 2027 the assessed value resets (N-142), so Daniel's bill could be more than **twice** theirs even with his own homestead exemption. That is the warning in his property tax disclosure summary (V-75). As a rental it would be 510 × 15.5 mills = **$7,905**, with no exemption.
- The Lopes family is moving to a smaller home in Venice. Their benefit, $520,000 − $260,000 = **$260,000**, is under the $500,000 cap. Abandoning the old homestead in 2026, they must establish the new one within 3 years after January 1, 2026 and file Form DR-501T with the homestead application (V-67).

## 6. Portugal comparison

**Familiar principle.** Portugal also taxes property every year: **IMI** is an annual municipal tax on the registered taxable value (VPT) (PT-10). IMI rates and exemptions require verification.

**Florida rule.** The county property appraiser sets just value as of January 1 and the taxing authorities set millage. Homestead owners get the exemptions ($25,000 + up to $25,000 non-school) and the Save Our Homes cap (3% or CPI).

**Precise difference.** Florida computes the tax **in mills on taxable value after exemptions**, and the school and non-school values can differ. A homestead assessed at $300,000 has a school taxable value of $275,000 and a non-school taxable value of $250,000 (round figures). The school value is higher because the additional exemption applies only to non-school levies.

**Exam implication.** 1 mill = $1 per $1,000; run school and non-school separately whenever exemptions differ. Never answer a Florida question with an IMI habit.

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

**Memorize exactly**

- Taxable value = assessed value − exemptions. Tax = taxable value × mills ÷ 1,000. 1 mill = $1 per $1,000 = 0.001 (manual; not yet verified, N-144).
- Homestead: **$25,000** off **all** taxes + up to **$25,000** off **non-school** taxes on assessed value **above $50,000** (s. 196.031; V-09); CPI-indexed since 2025 (**$25,722** for 2025); exam math $25,000. Own and occupy as a permanent residence on January 1 (N-140).
- **$5,000** widow/widower, blind, totally and permanently disabled; Florida resident (s. 196.202; V-10). $500 = outdated.
- Disabled veterans: larger or total exemptions. Green Belt: use value (manual; not yet verified).
- SOH: increase ≤ **3%** or the **CPI** change, **whichever is lower** (Art. VII, s. 4(d); s. 193.155; V-67). Resets to just value on a change of ownership (N-142).
- Portability: up to **$500,000**; within **3 years** after January 1 of the year the old homestead was abandoned (2 years = outdated); homestead exemption on January 1 of any of the 3 preceding years; Form **DR-501T** (V-67).
- CS/HJR 1-F (November 3, 2026 ballot; 60%; would apply from January 1, 2027): **not law** (V-23). HB 7031: status not verified.

**Understand**

- Exemptions reduce the value, never the tax directly.
- School taxable value is the larger one, because the second exemption skips school levies.
- SOH caps the growth of assessed value, not the millage; a higher millage can still raise the bill.
- The SOH benefit protects the owner who stays: a sale resets it, and portability lets the owner take part of it along.

## 9. Common misconceptions

- **"Homestead exempts the first $50,000 from all taxes."** No. $25,000 from all taxes; the second exemption covers value above $50,000, non-school only. Value from $25,000 to $50,000 is always taxed.
- **"The widow's exemption is $500."** Outdated; it is $5,000 (V-10).
- **"SOH is 3% or CPI, whichever is greater."** Whichever is **less**.
- **"SOH caps the tax bill."** It caps assessed-value growth; millage can still rise.
- **"The buyer keeps the seller's low assessed value."** It resets to just value (N-142).
- **"Portability has no limit."** Up to $500,000, within 3 years. The November 2026 measure would loosen this for non-school levies only if approved (V-23).
- **"Rentals get Save Our Homes."** No. SOH and the homestead exemption are for homesteads.
