## 2. Why it matters

**In practice.** Two questions come up in almost every listing appointment and every pay day. The seller asks: *"What do I have to sell for to walk away with $X?"* You ask: *"What will I actually receive from this commission?"* Both answers depend on doing the percentages in the right order and on the right base.

**On the exam.** The commission chain and net-to-seller are patterns 2 and 3 of the study manual's twelve math patterns. They sit in Section 14, which carries **6%** of the exam (V-02). The wrong answer choices are built from predictable mistakes, such as multiplying the net by 1.06 or applying the associate's split to the whole commission. If you know the mistakes, you can spot the trap.

## 3. Simple explanation

### Step 1: What it represents

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Total commission** | The brokerage fee for the sale; in exam problems, a percentage of the **sale price**. On the closing statement it is customarily a seller debit, that is, an amount the seller owes (manual; not yet verified). |
| **Listing brokerage** | The brokerage that took the listing (works with the seller) |
| **Cooperating (selling) brokerage** | The brokerage that brought the buyer |
| **Co-brokerage split** | How the total commission is divided between the two brokerages |
| **Associate split** | How a brokerage's share is divided between the brokerage and its sales associate |
| **Net to seller** | What the seller keeps after the commission and the other selling costs |

In exam problems the money flows **down a chain**: sale price → total commission → each brokerage's share → each associate's share.

### Step 2: Units

Everything is in dollars and percentages. The key point about units is that **each split percentage is a percentage of the amount just above it** in the chain, not of the sale price. "The associate receives 60%" means 60% of her brokerage's share.

Convert every percentage to a decimal: 6% = 0.06, half = 0.50, 60% = 0.60.

### Step 3: Formula

**Commission chain (multiply down):**

- Total commission = Price × Rate
- Brokerage share = Total commission × brokerage split
- Associate share = Brokerage share × associate split
- In one line: **Associate share = Price × Rate × brokerage split × associate split**

**Working backwards (divide up):** divide by each split in turn, then use Rate = Total commission ÷ Price, or Price = Total commission ÷ Rate.

**Net to seller:**

**Price = (Net + fixed costs) ÷ (1 − rate)**

**Why this works.** The price is 100%. The commission takes the rate (say 6%) **of the price**, so the seller keeps 94% of the price (1 − 0.06 = 0.94). That 94% must cover the seller's net plus any fixed dollar costs. So 0.94 × Price = Net + fixed costs, and dividing both sides by 0.94 gives the price. It is the percent triangle again: the percentage is taken of the unknown price, so you **divide**.

### Step 4: When it applies

- **Read each split carefully.** "The listing brokerage receives half" = 50% of the total. "The brokerage keeps 40%" means the associate receives 60%. With a split written as "70/30", read the wording to see who receives which part.
- **Net-to-seller** applies whenever the seller names an amount to keep **after** the commission.
- **Fixed costs** are amounts stated in dollars (for example, "$4,500 of other closing costs", or a mortgage payoff when the seller's target is the cash in hand). They go in the numerator, **before** dividing.
- **A cost stated as a percentage of the price** belongs with the rate, not in the numerator: a 6% commission plus a 1% fee means dividing by 1 − 0.07 = 0.93.
- **Rounding.** Follow the question (often to the nearest dollar). After rounding, your check may differ by a few cents; that is rounding, not an error.

### Step 5: Worked example

**A. Down the chain.** A Jacksonville home sells for $360,000 with a 5% total commission. The listing brokerage receives half, and the listing associate receives 70% of her brokerage's share.

1. Total commission: $360,000 × 0.05 = $18,000.
2. Listing brokerage: $18,000 × 0.50 = $9,000.
3. Listing associate: $9,000 × 0.70 = **$6,300**.
4. One line: 360,000 × 0.05 × 0.50 × 0.70 = $6,300 ✓.

**B. Up the chain.** The associate received $6,300, which was 70% of her brokerage's share. The brokerage's share was half of the total commission on a $360,000 sale. What was the rate?

1. Brokerage share: $6,300 ÷ 0.70 = $9,000.
2. Total commission: $9,000 ÷ 0.50 = $18,000.
3. Rate: $18,000 ÷ $360,000 = 0.05 = **5%**.

**C. Net to seller.** A seller wants to net $188,000 after a 6% commission, with no other costs.

1. Price = $188,000 ÷ (1 − 0.06) = $188,000 ÷ 0.94 = **$200,000**.
2. Check: $200,000 × 6% = $12,000; $200,000 − $12,000 = $188,000 ✓.
3. **The common error:** $188,000 × 1.06 = $199,280. That takes 6% of the net, not of the price, and leaves the seller short. At $199,280 the commission is $11,956.80, and the seller nets $187,323.20, which is **$676.80 short**.

**D. Net with fixed costs.** A seller wants to net $277,000 after a 6% commission and $5,000 of other closing costs.

1. Price = ($277,000 + $5,000) ÷ 0.94 = $282,000 ÷ 0.94 = **$300,000**.
2. Check: $300,000 − $18,000 commission − $5,000 costs = $277,000 ✓.

### Step 6: Reasonableness check

- Down the chain every number gets smaller: associate share < brokerage share < total commission < price.
- The net-to-seller price must be **more** than the net plus the costs, and more than (net + costs) × (1 + rate).
- **Always check by taking the commission from your answer:** price − (price × rate) − fixed costs should equal the net.
- Working backwards, each step should get **larger**. If a number shrinks, you multiplied instead of dividing.

### Step 7: Common errors

| Error | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Net × (1 + rate) | $188,000 × 1.06 = $199,280 | Net ÷ (1 − rate) = $200,000 |
| Net × (1 − rate) or net − commission | $188,000 × 0.94 = $176,720 | That is smaller than the net: impossible |
| Applying the associate's % to the total or the price | 60% of the whole commission | Apply it to the brokerage's share |
| Adding fixed costs after dividing | $188,000 ÷ 0.94 + costs | Add them **before** dividing |
| Reading "brokerage keeps 40%" as the associate's share | Associate gets 40% | Associate gets 60% |
| Multiplying when working backwards | $6,300 × 0.70 | Divide by each split |

### Step 10: Exam application

**Commission chain**

1. Write the chain: price → total → brokerage → associate.
2. Mark each split and what it is a percentage **of**.
3. Going down, multiply. Going up, divide.
4. Check that the numbers shrink going down and grow going up.

**Net to seller**

1. Add the fixed dollar costs to the net.
2. Subtract the rate (plus any other percentage-of-price costs) from 1.
3. Divide.
4. Check by taking the commission (and costs) from your answer.

If one answer choice equals net × 1.06 and another equals net ÷ 0.94, the first is the trap.

## 4. Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Commission | The brokerage fee; in exam problems, a percentage of the sale price (the rate comes from the question) |
| Total commission | Sale price × commission rate |
| Listing brokerage | The brokerage that took the seller's listing |
| Cooperating (selling) brokerage | The brokerage that brought the buyer |
| Co-brokerage split | Division of the total commission between the two brokerages |
| Associate split | Division of one brokerage's share between the brokerage and its associate |
| Net to seller | What the seller keeps after the commission and other selling costs |
| Fixed costs | Selling costs stated as dollar amounts, added to the net before dividing |
| 1 − rate | The fraction of the price the seller keeps after the commission (0.94 at 6%) |

## 5. Practical example

**A listing appointment in Sarasota.** The sellers tell Joana, a listing associate, that they need **$464,000** after selling to buy their next home. Their other closing costs are estimated at **$6,000**. Joana's brokerage proposes a **6%** total commission, to be split 50/50 with any cooperating brokerage. Joana receives **60%** of her brokerage's share.

1. **Minimum price:** ($464,000 + $6,000) ÷ 0.94 = $470,000 ÷ 0.94 = **$500,000**.
2. **Check:** $500,000 × 0.06 = $30,000. $500,000 − $30,000 − $6,000 = $464,000 ✓.
3. **Why not "$470,000 plus 6%"?** $470,000 × 1.06 = $498,200, and at that price the sellers would net $498,200 × 0.94 − $6,000 = $462,308, which is $1,692 short.
4. **The chain at $500,000:** total $30,000 → listing brokerage $15,000 → Joana $15,000 × 0.60 = **$9,000**. The cooperating brokerage also receives $15,000. If the buyer's associate is on a 70% split, she receives $10,500.

Joana shows the sellers the check line. Clients trust a number they can verify.

## 6. Portugal comparison

**Familiar principle.** Calculating a commission on a sale price and sharing it between firms and agents is the same arithmetic wherever it happens. The course's Portugal–Florida comparison entries contain **no** Portuguese commission figures or rules, so any specific Portuguese practice (typical rates, whether tax is added, who pays, how commissions are shared) **requires verification**.

**Florida rule (exam).** Commission = the rate given × the sale price. Splits pass down the chain the question describes. Net-to-seller price = (net + fixed costs) ÷ (1 − rate).

**Precise difference.** There is no meaningful difference in the arithmetic.

**Exam implication.** Use only the numbers in the question. Do not bring a Portuguese rate, a tax add-on or a habit of "adding the percentage on top" into a Florida answer.

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

**Memorize exactly**

- Total commission = **Price × Rate**; then multiply through each split. Working backwards, **divide** by each split.
- **Price = (Net + fixed costs) ÷ (1 − rate).**
- Check: Price − commission − fixed costs = Net.

**Understand**

- This lesson sets no legal rates: the rate always comes from the question.
- Each split is a percentage of the amount just above it in the chain.
- The commission is a percentage **of the price**, not of the net, which is why you divide by (1 − rate) and never multiply by (1 + rate).
- The commission is customarily a seller debit on the closing statement (manual; not yet verified).

## 9. Common misconceptions

- **"To net $188,000 at 6%, list at $188,000 × 1.06."** No. That gives $199,280 and leaves the seller short. Divide: $188,000 ÷ 0.94 = **$200,000**.
- **"The associate's 60% is 60% of the commission."** No. It is 60% of **her brokerage's share**.
- **"Fixed costs can be added after dividing."** No. Add them to the net **first**.
- **"Working backwards, multiply by the splits."** No. **Divide** by each split.
- **"A check isn't needed if the answer appears among the choices."** No. The trap answers are designed to appear among the choices.

## 10. Memory sheet

- **Chain:** price × rate → × brokerage split → × associate split. Backwards: **divide**.
- **Each split is "of" the level above.**
- **Net to seller: Price = (Net + fixed costs) ÷ (1 − rate).** $188,000 ÷ 0.94 = $200,000.
- **Never net × 1.06** ($199,280 leaves the seller short).
- Percentage-of-price costs join the rate; dollar costs join the net.
- Always check by taking the commission from your answer.
