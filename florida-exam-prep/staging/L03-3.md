## 2. Why it matters

A Florida license is not permanent. It must be renewed every 2 years, and the first renewal carries a one-time education deadline that can end the license outright. The exam's most-swapped pair lives here: **missed post-license → null and void**, but **missed CE or renewal → involuntary inactive**. Expect questions on the 45, 14 and 28 hours, the 12- and 24-month reactivation bands, and the status words: active, voluntary inactive, involuntary inactive, suspended, revoked, null and void, ineffective.

In practice, the post-license deadline is the one that can end a new career before it starts.

## 3. Simple explanation

### The renewal calendar

Every Florida real estate license expires at midnight Eastern time on **March 31** or **September 30**. A new license is fitted to one of those two dates, so the **first renewal period is 18–24 months**. Every later period is **24 months**.

### Stage 1: post-license education (before the first renewal)

- **Sales associates: 45 hours** (s. 475.17(3)(a)). The statute caps the requirement at 45 classroom hours (50-minute hours, exam included); it is met with one or more FREC-approved courses totaling at least 45 hours. Courses may be classroom or approved distance learning; a distance course requires passing a timed exam.
- If absences exceed **10%** of classroom hours, no completion notice is issued.
- **Missed?** The license "shall be considered **null and void**." To work again, the person must requalify: complete the **63-hour** pre-license course and pass the state exam.
- **Exceptions:** the FREC may allow **6 extra months** for an individual physical hardship (as defined by rule). Holders of a **4-year or higher degree in real estate** are exempt.
- **Attorneys are not exempt** from post-license (per DBPR), even though Florida Bar members are exempt from CE.

Why "void" and not "inactive"? Think of post-license as the last piece of qualifying. If qualification is never finished, there is no license left to put on hold.

### Stage 2: continuing education (every later renewal)

- **14 hours** (50-minute hours) in each 2-year renewal period, starting **after** the first renewal (s. 475.182(1); Rule 61J2-3.009).
- Required split: **3 hours Core Law + 3 hours Ethics and Business Practices + 8 hours specialty**. This is mandatory for licenses expiring on or after March 31, 2025, and Core Law must be taken at least once each renewal period.
- Applies to **active and inactive** licensees. Approved distance learning is allowed.
- Florida Bar members in good standing are exempt from CE.
- Once per renewal cycle, attending one FREC legal agenda session can substitute for 3 hours. This requires 7 days' notice and does not apply if you attend as a party to a disciplinary case.

### Missing a renewal: involuntary inactive (s. 475.183; verified, V-11)

If a license is not renewed on time, for example because CE was not finished, it becomes **involuntary inactive**. The license still exists, but a clock starts:

| Time involuntarily inactive | To reactivate |
|---|---|
| 12 months or less | **14 hours** of commission-prescribed CE |
| More than 12 but less than 24 months | **28-hour** commission-prescribed reactivation course |
| More than 24 months | License **expires** (null and void) |

### The status map

| Status | How it happens | May the person work? | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Active** | Registered with an employing broker (a broker: with an office) | Yes | Renew every 2 years |
| **Voluntary inactive** | The licensee chooses it | No | Renewable indefinitely with 14 hours of CE per cycle |
| **Involuntary inactive** | Failure to renew | No | 2-year countdown (table above) |
| **Suspended** | Discipline: up to **10 years** | No | The license exists but cannot be used |
| **Revoked** | Discipline | No | Permanent cancellation (narrow exceptions) |
| **Null and void** | Post-license missed; involuntary inactive over 24 months | No | The license no longer exists; requalify |
| **Ineffective** | License exists but cannot be used, e.g., associate with no employer | No | Register with an employer (L03-4) |

## 4. Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Renewal period | Time between license expirations: 18–24 months the first time, then 24 months |
| Post-license education | 45 hours (sales associate) due before the first renewal |
| Continuing education (CE) | 14 hours per renewal period after the first: 3 Core Law, 3 Ethics, 8 specialty |
| Core Law | The required 3-hour Core Law part of CE, taken at least once each renewal period |
| Involuntary inactive | Status after failing to renew; void after 24 months |
| Voluntary inactive | Status the licensee chooses while not practicing |
| Reactivation course | 28-hour course for involuntary inactive more than 12 but less than 24 months |
| Null and void | The license has ceased to exist; the person must requalify |
| Ineffective | Exam term for a license that exists but cannot be used |

## 5. Practical example

**Sofia** and **Lucas** are licensed as sales associates in Jacksonville in May 2024. Their first expiration is **March 31, 2026**: the only March 31 or September 30 date that falls 18–24 months later (about 22 months).

| Event | Rule | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Sofia finishes her 45 hours in January 2026 and renews | Post-license done before the 1st renewal | Renewed; next expiration March 31, 2028 |
| Lucas has not finished his 45 hours on March 31, 2026 (no degree, no hardship extension) | Post-license missed | **Null and void**: 63-hour course and state exam again |
| Sofia forgets her CE and does not renew by March 31, 2028 | Failure to renew | **Involuntary inactive** |
| …she reactivates in January 2029 (about 10 months) | 12 months or less | **14 hours** of CE |
| …or in October 2029 (about 18 months) | More than 12 but less than 24 months | **28-hour** reactivation course |
| …or does nothing until after March 31, 2030 | More than 24 months | **Null and void** |

Sofia's 14 hours for 2028 must include **3 Core Law + 3 Ethics and Business Practices + 8 specialty**.

## 6. Portugal comparison

**Familiar principle.** In Portugal, IMPIC's listed requirements for a mediation license are commercial good standing (*idoneidade comercial*), civil liability insurance (or an equivalent guarantee) and fees. No state exam or minimum course hours appear.

**Florida rule.** Florida adds an education pathway with hard deadlines: 63 hours to enter, 45 hours before the first renewal, and 14 hours every 2 years after that.

**Precise difference.** In Florida, missing an education deadline can **void** a license. Whether Portugal has any comparable renewal or continuing-education duty *requires verification* (our comparison data does not cover it).

**Exam implication.** Never assume a license simply continues. Know **63 / 45 / 14** and which miss voids (post-license) and which inactivates (CE).

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

**Memorize exactly**

- First renewal: **18–24 months**; later periods **24 months**; licenses expire **March 31 or September 30**.
- Post-license: **45 hours** before the first renewal → missed = **null and void** → 63-hour course + state exam (s. 475.17(3)).
- Absences over **10%** → no post-license completion notice. Individual physical hardship: the FREC may allow **6 extra months**. Exempt: **4-year real estate degree**. Attorneys **not** exempt.
- CE: **14 hours** per 2-year period after the first renewal: **3 Core Law + 3 Ethics + 8 specialty** (licenses expiring on or after March 31, 2025). Active **and** inactive licensees. Florida Bar members exempt (s. 475.182; Rule 61J2-3.009).
- Missed renewal → **involuntary inactive**. Reactivation: **≤ 12 months → 14 hours**; **more than 12 but less than 24 months → 28-hour course**; **more than 24 months → void** (s. 475.183; Rule 61J2-3.010).
- Suspension: up to **10 years** (s. 475.25(1)).
- Voluntary inactive renewable indefinitely (manual; not yet verified). Its CE duty is confirmed by Rule 61J2-3.009.
- Revocation is permanent with narrow exceptions (manual; not yet verified). "Ineffective" is a course and exam term (manual; not yet verified).

**Understand**

- Older materials describe CE only as "14 hours, including core law". The **3 + 3 + 8** split is the current requirement.
- The study manual mentions only "late CE" for reactivation. The **28-hour course** is a verified addition from s. 475.183.
- The 18–24-month first period comes from an official DBPR sheet (2018). Nothing current contradicts it, but no current-year restatement was found, and the rule citation the manual gives (61J2-1.008 or 61J2-3.020) is unverified.

## 9. Common misconceptions

- **Swapping the two deadlines.** Post-license missed → **void**. CE or renewal missed → **involuntary inactive**.
- **"Involuntary inactive can be fixed any time."** Only for 24 months; after that the license is void.
- **"Attorneys are exempt from post-license, like CE."** Florida Bar members are exempt from CE, but **not** from post-license.
- **"CE is due at the first renewal too."** The first renewal requires post-license; CE starts after it.
- **"Voluntary inactive licensees need no CE."** They complete 14 hours per cycle.
- **"Any 14 hours will do."** It must be 3 Core Law + 3 Ethics + 8 specialty.
- **"A suspended license no longer exists."** It exists but cannot be used. Revoked and null-and-void licenses are the ones that are gone.

## 10. Memory sheet

- **63 to get in · 45 to stay in · 14 to keep going.**
- **POST missed → VOID. CE missed → INACTIVE.**
- First renewal **18–24 months**; then every **24**. Expiry **Mar 31 / Sep 30**.
- CE = **3 Core + 3 Ethics + 8 specialty**.
- Involuntary inactive: **≤ 1 year → 14 · 1–2 years → 28 · past 2 years → dead.**
- Florida Bar members: no CE, **but post-license yes**. 4-year real estate degree: no post-license.
