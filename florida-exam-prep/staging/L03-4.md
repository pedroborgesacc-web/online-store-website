## 2. Why it matters

The DBPR and the FREC can reach you only through the contact details on record, and they expect you to report some events yourself. This lesson gives you the clocks: **10 days** for a change of mailing or e-mail address (and for a change of business address or employing broker), **30 days** to report a criminal conviction, finding of guilt or plea, and **60 days** when a Florida resident becomes a nonresident.

Exam questions test 10 versus 30, the *nolo contendere* and "adjudication withheld" traps, and the classic scenario of an associate who keeps showing homes between brokers. In practice, changing brokers is common, and doing it wrong means working without an effective license.

## 3. Simple explanation

### 1. Keep your contact details current

- Under **s. 455.275(1)**, each licensee is **solely responsible** for notifying the DBPR in writing of their current **mailing address**, **e-mail address** and **place of practice**. Failing to report a change is a violation.
- **Rule 61J2-10.038** sets the deadline: notify the Department in writing of any change to your mailing or e-mail address **within 10 days after the change**. A **first** violation gets a **citation** (a lighter penalty notice instead of a full disciplinary case). **Repeat** violations lead to disciplinary proceedings.
- **s. 475.23** separately requires notice within **10 days** of a change of **business address or employing broker**, on a Commission form.
- **Moving out of Florida** does not end the license (manual; not yet verified). A licensee who changes from Florida resident to **nonresident** must notify the Commission within **60 days**, and the new mailing address is still due within 10 days.

Why so strict? The DBPR can contact you only through the details on record, and the law puts the burden of keeping them current on the licensee alone.

### 2. Report crimes yourself

- **s. 455.227(1)(t)**, which covers every DBPR licensee: report **in writing to the FREC within 30 days** after being **convicted or found guilty** of, or **pleading guilty or nolo contendere** to, **a crime in any jurisdiction**, **regardless of adjudication**.
- **s. 475.25(1)(p)**, the real estate law's own version, is narrower: it covers **felonies** only. Because licensees are bound by both, the rule to learn is the broader one: **any crime**.
- ***Nolo contendere*** means "no contest". The defendant does not admit guilt but accepts the penalty.
- **Adjudication withheld** means the court accepts the plea or finding of guilt but does not formally declare the person convicted. The report is still required.
- The 30 days run from the **plea, finding of guilt or conviction**, not from the arrest.

Why? The FREC judges honesty and trustworthiness throughout a career, not only on the application (L02-3).

### 3. One license, one employer

A sales associate may hold only **one** license and work for only **one** broker or owner-employer at a time. A group license lets a sales associate work for several connected entities of one owner-developer; it never allows working for an unrelated broker at the same time (L03-2).

### 4. Changing brokers

- When employment ends, the associate's license cannot be used until the **new employer's registration is filed**. In the gap, it is **ineffective**.
- Showing listings, listing or negotiating in the gap is **operating without an effective license**.
- The broker must notify the DBPR when an associate leaves.
- The 10-day notice in s. 475.23 is a **reporting deadline**, not a 10-day permission to work unregistered.

## 4. Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Mailing address | The address on DBPR records where official notices are sent |
| Place of practice | Where the licensee practices; it must be on record with the DBPR |
| Nolo contendere | "No contest" plea: no admission of guilt, but the penalty is accepted |
| Adjudication withheld | The court accepts the plea or finding but does not formally declare a conviction |
| Found guilty | A court or jury decides the person committed the crime |
| Employing broker | The broker a sales associate is registered under |
| Ineffective license | A license that exists but cannot be used, e.g., between employers |
| Citation | A lighter penalty notice; used for a first address-rule violation instead of disciplinary proceedings |
| Nonresident licensee | A Florida licensee who does not live in Florida |

## 5. Practical example

**Ana**, a sales associate from Porto, works for Brickell Bay Realty in Miami.

| Date | Event | Duty | Deadline |
|---|---|---|---|
| June 3 | Moves apartments and changes her e-mail | Written notice to the DBPR of the new mailing and e-mail address | **June 13** (10 days) |
| August 1 | Pleads *nolo contendere* to a misdemeanor in Georgia; adjudication withheld | Written report to the FREC (any crime, any jurisdiction, regardless of adjudication) | **August 31** (30 days) |
| September 15 | Leaves Brickell Bay for Coral Gate Realty | Change of employing broker on the Commission form | **September 25** (10 days), but **no work** until Coral Gate's registration is filed |
| The next year | Moves to Georgia and becomes a nonresident | Notify the Commission of nonresidency; update the mailing address | **60 days**; 10 days |

On September 16, before Coral Gate's registration is filed, Ana holds an open house for a former client. Her license still exists, but it is **ineffective**: she is operating without an effective license. The 10-day notice period does not protect her.

## 6. Portugal comparison

**Familiar principle.** In Portugal, IMPIC requires commercial good standing (*idoneidade comercial*) as a condition of the mediation business's license. Integrity matters in both systems.

**Florida rule.** Florida makes integrity and contact details a **personal, continuing** duty of every licensee: crimes reported within 30 days, address and e-mail within 10 days, change of employer on record before working.

**Precise difference.** Whether Portuguese law requires a consultant or agency to report address changes or criminal matters to IMPIC, and on what deadline, *requires verification*; our comparison data does not cover it. In Portugal, consultants usually work under the agency's license (*requires verification*), so moving agencies may feel like a private matter. In Florida, changing brokers is a **registration event with the state**.

**Exam implication.** Answer only with the Florida clocks: 10, 30 and 60 days.

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

**Memorize exactly**

- Mailing or e-mail address change: written notice to the DBPR within **10 days** (Rule 61J2-10.038; s. 455.275(1)). First violation → citation; repeat → discipline.
- Change of business address or employing broker: **10 days**, on a Commission form (s. 475.23).
- Florida resident becomes nonresident: notify the Commission within **60 days** (V-34; no statute section given in our sources).
- Conviction, finding of guilt, or guilty or *nolo contendere* plea to **any crime in any jurisdiction**: written report to the FREC within **30 days**, **regardless of adjudication** (s. 455.227(1)(t)). Chapter 475's own rule covers felonies (s. 475.25(1)(p)).
- One license, one employer at a time for sales associates (manual; not yet verified).
- No services between brokers until the new employer's registration is filed; the broker notifies the DBPR when an associate leaves (manual; not yet verified). Do not read the 10-day notice period as permission to work in the gap.
- The license survives a move out of state (manual; not yet verified).

**Understand**

- The 10-day address deadline is confirmed in the 2020 rule text. The rule was amended again effective 7/18/2024, and that text was not read directly, so the continuing 10-day period is inferred.
- According to the DBPR annual report, that 2024 amendment changed the definition of "current mailing address" by removing "residential", so the brokerage office's physical location can serve. The amended text itself was not read. Older materials that require a **residential** address are likely outdated.
- "Any crime, regardless of adjudication" comes from s. 455.227(1)(t). Chapter 475 alone would require reporting only felonies, but licensees are bound by both.

## 9. Common misconceptions

- **"Nolo contendere is not a conviction, so there is nothing to report."** It must be reported within 30 days.
- **"Adjudication was withheld, so it doesn't count."** The duty applies regardless of adjudication.
- **"Only felonies must be reported."** Under s. 455.227(1)(t), any crime must be reported.
- **"A crime in another state doesn't count."** Any jurisdiction counts.
- **"The 30 days start at the arrest."** They start at the plea, finding of guilt or conviction.
- **"My license is current, so I can keep working between brokers."** It is ineffective until the new registration is filed.
- **"The 10-day notice gives me 10 days to work for the new broker unregistered."** It is a reporting deadline only.
- **"Only my postal address matters."** E-mail address and place of practice must be on record too.

## 10. Memory sheet

- **Address or e-mail = 10. Employer or business address = 10. Become a nonresident = 60.**
- **Crime = 30:** conviction, finding of guilt, guilty or *nolo* plea, **any crime, anywhere, adjudication or not**, in writing to the FREC.
- *Nolo* = report. Withheld = report.
- **No employer = no work.** One license, one employer.
- First address violation = citation; repeat = discipline.
