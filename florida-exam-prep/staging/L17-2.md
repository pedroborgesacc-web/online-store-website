## 2. Why it matters

**In practice.** Florida towns have plenty of small businesses: cafés, salons, dry cleaners, small motels. When the owners sell, they often call the agent who sold their house. Selling a business is not like selling a house. You are selling a working enterprise, and part of the price pays for things nobody can touch. You need to know what is in the package, when a licence is needed and which disclosure rules apply.

**On the exam.** This is a low-priority topic. It shares chapter 17's roughly 2% of the exam (V-02) with investment analysis. Expect at most one question, usually "what is goodwill?" or "does this person need a real estate licence?".

## 3. Simple explanation

### What is being sold: a going concern

**Business opportunity brokerage** means helping someone sell (or buy) a business for compensation. What changes hands is a **going concern**: a business that is operating today and is expected to keep operating, with customers, staff, suppliers and a name people recognise. The buyer pays for the whole working unit because it already produces income. A café with loyal customers is usually worth more than its tables, machines and coffee beans sold one by one.

### The package

| Component | What kind of property | Example: a café | How it usually transfers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Real estate** (if any) | Real property | The land and building, if the business owner owns them | Deed |
| **Lease of the premises** (if the business rents) | A leasehold, which is an interest in real property | The café's lease of a shop unit | Assignment of the lease, subject to the lease's terms |
| **Goodwill** | Intangible | Reputation, regular customers, the café's name | Included in the sale contract |
| **Fixtures** | Items attached to the premises | Walk-in cooler, built-in counters | Listed one by one in the contract |
| **Equipment** | Personal property | Espresso machines, ovens, point-of-sale terminals | Bill of sale |
| **Inventory** | Personal property | Coffee beans, pastries, packaged goods | Bill of sale, usually counted near closing |

**Fixtures need special care.** Whether a counter or cooler goes with the business or stays with the building depends on who owns the building and on what the contract says, so list each item in writing. Fixtures a business tenant installs for its trade (**trade fixtures**) are generally the tenant's personal property.

### Goodwill

**Goodwill** is the intangible value of a business's reputation and customer base: the reason customers keep coming back. A practical way to picture it is as the part of the price that the identifiable assets do not explain. Goodwill exists only while the business keeps running, which is why buyers want a going concern and not a closed shop.

Buyers usually pay for the **profit** a business produces, so the income thinking from investment analysis (L17-1) applies here too: a steady, well-documented income stream supports a higher price.

### When a real estate licence is needed

Under Chapter 475, **brokering business opportunities for compensation requires a real estate licence, particularly when real property is included** (the manual's wording; manual; not yet verified).

Read the manual's wording carefully. "Particularly when real property is included" points to the clearest case. It does **not** say that a business sale without real estate is free of licensing. The course position, and the exam answer, is that a person who sells a business for another person for a commission needs a real estate licence. The exact scope of the statute has not been verified yet.

### Brokerage-relationship disclosures

Earlier lessons covered the brokerage-relationship disclosure rules (s. 475.278): the single agent, transaction broker and no brokerage relationship notices. **Business sales are outside these disclosure rules, except for property with four or fewer residential units** (V-20, verified). So a café in a commercial building is outside them. If the deal also involves property with four or fewer residential units, the exception stops and the rules apply.

### Practical cautions (good practice, not law)

- Ask for the financial records (profit and loss statements, tax returns), because the price rests on income.
- Keep the sale confidential. Staff, suppliers and customers may react badly to rumours.
- List every component and its price in writing: real estate or lease, goodwill, fixtures, equipment, inventory.

## 4. Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Business opportunity brokerage | Selling a business for another for compensation (goodwill, inventory, fixtures, possibly real estate) |
| Going concern | A business that is operating and expected to continue, sold as a working whole |
| Goodwill | Intangible value of a business's reputation and customer base |
| Fixtures | Items attached to the premises (counters, coolers); list them in the contract |
| Trade fixtures | Fixtures a business tenant installs for its trade; generally the tenant's personal property |
| Equipment | Movable business assets (machines, terminals); personal property |
| Inventory | Goods held for sale or use (stock); personal property |
| Leasehold | A tenant's interest in real property under a lease |
| Bill of sale | The document that transfers personal property |
| Deed | The document that transfers real property |

## 5. Practical example

**Rui** is a sales associate in Tampa. **Helena** owns *Café Azulejo* (a fictional business). It is a busy café in a building she also owns. She asks Rui to sell the business and pay him a commission. All figures are hypothetical.

| Component | Agreed value |
|---|---|
| Land and building (commercial) | $780,000 |
| Equipment and fixtures (espresso machines, ovens, walk-in cooler, counters) | $95,000 |
| Inventory (counted near closing) | $25,000 |
| Goodwill (name, reputation, regular customers) | $250,000 |
| **Total price** | **$1,150,000** |

Goodwill here is the part of the price that the other assets do not explain: $1,150,000 − $780,000 − $95,000 − $25,000 = **$250,000**.

**Does Rui need a licence?** Yes. He is brokering a business opportunity for compensation, and real property is included (manual; not yet verified).

**Do the brokerage-relationship disclosures apply?** This is a business sale and the building is commercial, so it is outside the s. 475.278 disclosure rules (V-20).

**Variation 1: the duplex.** Suppose Helena's deal also includes a duplex (two residential units) behind the café. That is property with four or fewer residential units, so the business-sale exception does not reach it and Rui follows the disclosure rules (V-20).

**Variation 2: no building.** Suppose instead that Helena rents her shop space, so the deal is the lease, goodwill, equipment and inventory. Rui should not conclude that he can work without a licence. For exam purposes, brokering a business opportunity for compensation requires a real estate licence. The manual's emphasis on real property is not a safe harbour (manual; not yet verified).

## 6. Portugal comparison

**The familiar principle.** In Portugal you may know the sale of an operating shop or café as a *trespasse*. The Portuguese legal details, and whether a mediador's licence covers this work, **require verification**: this course has no verified comparison entry on business transfers. The commercial idea is the same in both places: the buyer pays for a working business, including its customers and name.

**The Florida rule.** Brokering a business opportunity for compensation requires a Florida real estate licence, particularly when real property is included (manual; not yet verified). Business sales are outside the brokerage-relationship disclosure rules, except for property with four or fewer residential units (V-20).

**The precise difference.** It cannot be stated with verified Portuguese facts. Treat any assumption from Portuguese practice as unverified.

**Exam implication.** A Florida question gets a Florida answer: selling a business for another for compensation means a real estate licence is needed. Portuguese practice never decides the answer.

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

**Memorize exactly**

- Business opportunity brokerage = selling a **going concern**: real estate (if any) + **goodwill** + fixtures + equipment + inventory.
- **Goodwill** = the intangible value of reputation and customer base.
- Under Chapter 475, brokering business opportunities **for compensation** requires a **real estate licence**, particularly when real property is included (the manual's wording; manual; not yet verified).
- Business sales are **outside** the s. 475.278 brokerage-relationship disclosure rules, **except** property with **four or fewer residential units** (V-20).

**Understand**

- A going concern is worth more than its parts because it already produces income. Goodwill measures part of that extra value.
- Real property moves by deed. Equipment and inventory move by bill of sale. A lease is assigned under its own terms.
- "Particularly when real property is included" does not mean "only when real property is included". Do not treat a sale with no real estate as licence-free.
- Source status: the licensing statement rests on the study manual and is not yet verified. The disclosure exception is verified (V-20). The chapter weight comes from V-02.

## 9. Common misconceptions

- **"A business is personal property, so no licence is needed."** Wrong. Brokering a business opportunity for compensation requires a real estate licence, particularly when real property is included (manual; not yet verified).
- **"Only a securities licence is needed to sell a business."** The course answer is a real estate licence.
- **"Only an attorney may sell a business for someone else."** No. This is business opportunity brokerage, which is done under a real estate licence.
- **"Goodwill is the value of the building."** No. Goodwill is intangible: reputation and customer base. The building is real estate.
- **"Goodwill, plottage and equity are the same thing."** No. Plottage is the value gain when parcels are combined. Equity is value minus debt. Goodwill is the business's intangible value.
- **"The brokerage-relationship disclosures apply to every sale."** Business sales are outside them, except property with four or fewer residential units (V-20).
- **"No building in the deal means no licence."** Do not assume it. The manual's "particularly" is not an exemption.
