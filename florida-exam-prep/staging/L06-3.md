## 2. Why it matters

The FREC can fine and suspend a licensee, but it cannot give a harmed consumer any money (L06-2). A consumer can win a court judgment against a licensee and still collect nothing if the licensee has no assets or has disappeared. The **Real Estate Recovery Fund** is the state's last-resort answer to that problem.

The exam asks four things: the **two caps** ($50,000 and $150,000), **what the Fund does not pay**, **who may not claim**, and **what happens to the licensee** when the Fund pays. It is part of Section 6 of the exam outline (3%, shared with L06-1 and L06-2). In practice, a Fund payment caused by your conduct suspends your license until you pay the money back with interest.

## 3. Simple explanation

### From the foundation up

1. A licensee harms a consumer in a brokerage transaction (for example, by misrepresentation).
2. The FREC may discipline the licensee, but it **does not award damages**.
3. The consumer sues in civil court and wins a **judgment** (a court order that the licensee must pay).
4. The licensee cannot or does not pay: the judgment is **unsatisfied**.
5. The consumer applies to the **Recovery Fund**, which pays within strict limits (s. 475.484, F.S.; verified, V-17).
6. The licensee's license is **automatically suspended** until the Fund is repaid in full plus interest (see the note in section 7).

### The conditions

The Fund pays a member of the public who:

- obtained a **final civil judgment** against a **licensee**;
- for a **violation arising from a real estate brokerage transaction**;
- and **cannot collect** it.

### What the Fund pays

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Type of loss | **Actual or compensatory damages** only |
| Not paid | **Treble damages** (triple damages), **court costs**, **attorney's fees**, **interest** |
| Per claim | The lesser of the unsatisfied judgment (actual or compensatory damages) or **$50,000** |
| Per transaction | **$50,000 in total**, however many people claim |
| Per licensee | **$150,000 in total** across all transactions; if claims exceed it, payments are **prorated** (shared out) |

### Who may and may not recover

| Person | May claim? |
|---|---|
| Buyer, seller or other member of the public harmed by a licensee in a brokerage transaction, with a final judgment they cannot collect | **Yes** |
| A licensee harmed while acting **solely as a buyer or seller** (a principal, not an agent) | **Yes**, like any consumer |
| A licensee who acted **as a licensee** in the transaction, for example a commission dispute | **No** |
| A sales associate who sues her broker over an unpaid commission | **No**: she acted as a licensee, and commission disputes are excluded |
| A licensee's spouse, in certain claims | **No** (manual; details not given) |

The logic: the Fund protects the **public** that deals with licensees. Disputes among licensees about their own pay are business disputes between professionals.

### The licensee's consequence

When the Fund pays because of a licensee's conduct, that license is **automatically suspended** until the licensee repays the Fund **in full plus interest**. According to the manual, bankruptcy does not restore the license.

### A second use: protecting a broker who obeys an EDO

When a broker holding a disputed deposit obtains an **Escrow Disbursement Order (EDO)** from the FREC, follows it, and is later sued for obeying it, the Fund also pays the broker's **defense costs** and the judgment (manual; not yet verified).

## 4. Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Real Estate Recovery Fund | State fund that pays consumers who cannot collect a judgment against a licensee arising from a brokerage transaction |
| Judgment | A court's decision ordering one party to pay another |
| Unsatisfied judgment | A judgment that has not been paid |
| Actual (compensatory) damages | Money that makes up for the real loss suffered |
| Treble damages | Three times the actual damages, awarded under some laws as a penalty |
| Attorney's fees / court costs | The cost of the lawyer and of the lawsuit itself |
| Aggregate cap | A maximum for all claims added together |
| Prorate | Share a limited amount out among several claims |
| Principal | A party to the deal (buyer or seller), as opposed to an agent |
| Escrow Disbursement Order (EDO) | FREC order deciding who receives disputed escrow funds held by a broker |

## 5. Practical example

**Lucas**, a sales associate in Clearwater, tells **Mrs. Duarte** that a condo "has never had water damage," knowing the unit flooded twice. She buys it, pays for repairs, and sues him. The court awards her a judgment:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Compensatory damages (repairs and losses) | $41,000 |
| Attorney's fees | $9,000 |
| Court costs | $1,500 |
| Interest | $2,000 |
| **Total judgment** | **$53,500** |

Lucas has no assets, so the judgment is unsatisfied. Mrs. Duarte applies to the Fund.

- **Fund pays:** the lesser of $41,000 (compensatory damages) or $50,000 = **$41,000**.
- **Fund does not pay:** the $12,500 of fees, costs and interest ($9,000 + $1,500 + $2,000).
- **Lucas:** his license is automatically suspended until he repays the Fund $41,000 plus interest.

**Two other claims:**

- **Ana**, a sales associate at Lucas's brokerage, wins a judgment against their broker for unpaid commissions and cannot collect it. She **cannot** claim: she acted as a licensee, and this is a commission dispute.
- **Paulo**, a licensed sales associate, bought a different unit through Lucas for himself, acting only as a buyer, and was misled the same way. After winning his own judgment that Lucas cannot pay, he **can** claim like any consumer. His claim is a separate transaction, with its own $50,000 cap, and counts toward Lucas's $150,000 total.

## 6. Portugal comparison

**Familiar principle.** Both systems want money to be available when a licensed intermediary harms a client. In Portugal, a mediation license is commonly understood to require **civil liability insurance (or an equivalent guarantee)** (*requires verification*: this course has no checked Portugal comparison for this topic).

**Florida rule.** The exam's protection is a **state fund**. It pays only after a **final civil judgment** that cannot be collected, only **actual or compensatory damages**, within **$50,000 per transaction** and **$150,000 per licensee**, and the licensee must repay it (automatic suspension until repaid with interest).

**Precise difference.** The Florida Fund is a last resort that requires a court judgment first, and it is a debt the licensee must repay. Whether and how a Portuguese client claims against a mediator's insurance or guarantee, and any limits on it, *requires verification*.

**Exam implication.** For any Florida question about an uncollectable judgment, apply the Recovery Fund rules. Do not answer "the agent's insurance pays", and never let a Portuguese rule answer a Florida question.

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

**Memorize exactly**

- **$50,000 per transaction**, all claimants combined (s. 475.484; verified, V-17).
- **$150,000 aggregate per licensee**; prorated when exceeded (V-17).
- Pays the lesser of the unsatisfied judgment for actual or compensatory damages or $50,000 (V-17).
- Never pays **treble damages, court costs, attorney's fees or interest** (V-17).
- Fund payment → license **automatically suspended** until repaid **in full plus interest**.
- A licensee who acted **as a licensee** (for example, a commission dispute) cannot recover.

**Understand**

- The caps and the excluded items are confirmed in s. 475.484 (V-17). The automatic suspension appears in the 2010 statute text; the 2026 excerpt checked did not show that sentence, so its current wording is not re-confirmed. It remains the standard exam answer.
- The judgment must arise from a violation in a real estate brokerage transaction, and the claimant must be unable to collect it.
- Who is excluded (licensees acting as licensees, a licensee's spouse in certain claims), the right of a licensee acting solely as a buyer or seller to claim, and the bankruptcy point (manual; not yet verified).
- The Fund's use for a broker sued after obeying an EDO (manual; not yet verified).
- The manual says the Fund never pays "punitive damages or interest". The statute's wording, as checked, is actual or compensatory damages only, with treble damages, court costs, attorney's fees and interest excluded (V-17).

## 9. Common misconceptions

- **"Sales associates can claim for unpaid commissions."** No. A licensee who acted as a licensee is excluded.
- **"The Fund pays legal fees."** No attorney's fees, court costs, interest or treble damages.
- **"Each claimant can get $50,000."** $50,000 is the total for the transaction.
- **"$150,000 per transaction."** $150,000 is the total per licensee; per transaction it is $50,000.
- **"The FREC orders the Fund to pay after its hearing."** The claimant first needs a civil court judgment that cannot be collected.
- **"Once the Fund pays, the licensee is off the hook."** The license is automatically suspended until the Fund is repaid in full plus interest.
- **"The Fund payment revokes the license."** It suspends it until repayment.
- **"Licensees can never claim."** A licensee harmed while acting solely as a buyer or seller may claim.
- **"The cap is $25,000."** $25,000 is not a Recovery Fund cap. Use $50,000 per transaction and $150,000 per licensee (V-17).

## 10. Memory sheet

- **50 per deal, 150 per licensee.** Public only.
- Judgment first → cannot collect → Fund.
- Pays actual or compensatory damages only. **No** treble damages, fees, costs or interest.
- Licensee acting as licensee (commission dispute) = **no claim**. Licensee acting solely as buyer or seller = may claim.
- **Payment = automatic suspension** until repaid in full plus interest.
- EDO followed and broker sued → Fund pays defense costs and judgment (manual).
