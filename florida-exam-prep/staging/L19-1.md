## 2. Why it matters

**In practice.** Zoning decides what a buyer may do with a property: build an addition near the lot line, run a small daycare from home, keep the shop the seller operates. Permit records and the certificate of occupancy show whether a building, or a later addition, was built legally and may be used. The county or city decides these questions, not you. Your job is to spot them and send the client to the local planning or building department, or to a Florida attorney when rights are at stake.

**On the exam.** Section 19, "Planning and Zoning", carries **1%** of the exam (V-02): about 1 of the 100 questions (V-01), shared with L19-2. Expect a classification: police power or eminent domain? Variance, special exception, nonconforming use or rezoning? Building permit or certificate of occupancy? The study manual calls variance versus special exception "a guaranteed question".

### Foundation: regulating land versus taking it

Owning land never means unlimited freedom. Government can **limit how you use** land, or it can **take the land itself**.

| | **Police power** | **Eminent domain** |
|---|---|---|
| What government does | **Regulates** use for public health, safety and welfare (zoning, building codes, permits) | **Takes** private property for public use |
| Legal process | Ordinances, codes, permits | **Condemnation** |
| Owner keeps the property? | Yes | No |
| Owner paid? | **No compensation** | **Just compensation** (fair payment for what is taken) |

(manual; not yet verified)

**Why is one paid and the other not?** A regulation places similar limits on many owners, and those owners also benefit: your home is safer and more pleasant when the neighbour cannot build a factory. A taking forces one owner to give up property so the public can have a road or a school, so the public pays that owner. "Police power" has nothing to do with the police force; it is the general power to make rules for public health, safety and welfare.

**Inverse condemnation.** In a normal condemnation the government starts the case. When a regulation goes so far that it **destroys all the property's value**, the **owner** may sue, claiming the regulation amounts to a **taking** that must be paid for. "Inverse" means reversed: the owner starts the case (manual; not yet verified). This course cites no court decisions on exactly where regulation becomes a taking; know the label and the trigger.

Taxation (Section 18) and escheat (L09-1) are also government powers over property, but not land-use controls.

### The comprehensive plan and zoning

- **Comprehensive plan.** Every Florida local government (county or city) maintains one: its **long-range land-use plan**.
- **Zoning** implements the plan. A local law (**ordinance**) divides land into **districts**: **residential, commercial, industrial, agricultural and special districts**. Each district lists the uses allowed and dimensional rules such as **setback** (minimum distance between a building and a lot line or the street) and **height**.
- **Buffer zones** ease the transition between very different districts, for example offices or a park between a shopping centre and single-family homes.

(manual; not yet verified)

The plan is the strategy; zoning is the rule book that carries it out, parcel by parcel. Zoning is **public** law; a **deed restriction** is a **private** limit recorded in a deed or plat (L09-4).

### When a rule does not fit: four tools

| Tool | What it is | District changes? | Why it exists | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Variance** | Permission to **deviate from a dimensional rule** (setback, height) because of a **hardship unique to the parcel** | No; same use | Rules written for typical lots can be unfair to an unusually narrow or odd-shaped one | Narrow lot: house would sit 3 feet (about 0.9 m) inside the side setback |
| **Special exception** (also **conditional use**) | A use the code **already permits** in the district **with case-by-case approval** | No | Some uses serve a neighbourhood but fit only on the right site | School, church or daycare in a residential district |
| **Nonconforming use** | A use **lawful before the code changed** ("grandfathered") | No | An owner who followed the old rules is not forced out | Corner store opened before the street was rezoned residential |
| **Rezoning** | **Changing the district itself** | **Yes** | The wanted use is not allowed in the district at all | Farmland changed to residential for a subdivision |

(manual; not yet verified)

**Limits of "grandfathered".** A nonconforming use **may continue**, but the code tolerates rather than encourages it: it **usually may not expand**, and if **destroyed or abandoned**, it **may not resume** (manual; not yet verified).

**The manual's exam trap:** hardship + dimensions → **variance**; permitted-with-approval use → **special exception**. **Neither is a rezoning** (manual; not yet verified).

**PUD (planned unit development).** A development planned as one unit that **mixes uses** (homes, shops, offices) and **clusters density** (buildings grouped closer together) **in exchange for open space**, such as parks or preserved land (manual; not yet verified).

### Construction control

1. **Building permit:** needed **before** construction starts.
2. **Inspections:** during the work, against the building codes.
3. **Certificate of occupancy (CO):** issued **after final inspection**; the building may not be used or occupied before it.

Florida **contractors** are licensed by the **CILB** (Construction Industry Licensing Board), not by the FREC. (manual; not yet verified)

## 4. Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Police power | Government power to regulate for health, safety and welfare; no compensation owed |
| Eminent domain | Government power to take private property for public use, with just compensation |
| Condemnation | The legal process for exercising eminent domain |
| Inverse condemnation | Owner's lawsuit claiming a regulation amounts to a taking |
| Comprehensive plan | Local government's long-range land-use plan; zoning implements it |
| Setback | Minimum distance between a building and a lot line or the street |
| Buffer zone | Area easing the transition between very different districts |
| Variance | Permission to deviate from a dimensional rule because of hardship unique to the parcel |
| Special exception (conditional use) | Use allowed in a zone with case-by-case approval |
| Nonconforming use | Lawful use that predates current zoning ("grandfathered") |
| Rezoning | Changing the zoning district itself |
| PUD | Planned unit development mixing uses with clustered density and open space |
| Certificate of occupancy (CO) | Document allowing a building to be occupied after final inspection |

## 5. Practical example

*Names, places and distances are invented for practice. Real setbacks and permitted uses come from each local code.*

Beatriz, a sales associate in Tampa, helps Mr. and Mrs. Moreira find a home where Mrs. Moreira can run a small daycare. Six land-use questions come up. Each time, Beatriz names the issue, sends the clients to the city or county for the facts, and suggests an attorney where rights are at stake.

| Situation | Classification | What follows |
|---|---|---|
| A lot is so narrow that their house plan would sit 3 feet (about 0.9 m) inside the side setback. | **Variance** | They may apply; the residential use does not change. |
| The city code lists daycare as allowed in that residential district with case-by-case approval. | **Special exception** | Mrs. Moreira applies for approval; no rezoning needed. |
| A bakery on the corner opened before the block was rezoned residential; its owner wants to double its size. | **Nonconforming use** | It may keep operating but usually may not expand; if it burns down or is abandoned, it may not reopen. |
| The county takes a 10-foot (about 3 m) strip along the front of another listing to widen the road. | **Eminent domain** | The owner receives just compensation. |
| The city lowers the height limit for every lot on that road; a seller says her lot is now worth less. | **Police power** | No compensation. Only if a regulation destroyed all value could she sue for inverse condemnation; Beatriz refers her to an attorney. |
| The house they choose has a garage converted into a bedroom. | **Permit and final inspection** | Beatriz suggests checking the permit and inspection history with the building department during the inspection period. |

- Why regulation is unpaid and a taking is paid.
- The plan comes first; zoning turns it into district rules.

## 9. Common misconceptions

- **"If zoning lowers my property's value, the county must pay me."** No. Zoning is police power; no compensation. Only a regulation that destroys all value supports an inverse condemnation suit.
- **"Eminent domain is voluntary because the owner is paid."** No. It is an involuntary transfer; just compensation is not consent (L09-1).
- **"A variance lets me use my lot for a different purpose."** No. A variance relaxes a dimensional rule. A different use needs a special exception (if the code lists it) or a rezoning.
- **"Grandfathered means protected forever."** A nonconforming use usually cannot expand and, if destroyed or abandoned, may not resume.
- **"The building permit lets the owner move in."** The permit allows construction; occupancy needs the CO after final inspection.
- **"Deed restrictions are part of zoning."** Deed restrictions are private and recorded; zoning is police power.
