## 2. Why it matters

Fair housing law shapes ordinary moments of the job: which homes you show a buyer, how you word an ad, how you answer "what kind of people live here?", and what you do when an owner gives you a discriminatory instruction. Breaking it exposes you and your broker to a HUD complaint or a lawsuit.

On the exam, Section VII (*Federal and State Laws Pertaining to Real Estate*) is **3%**, about 3 questions shared by the four lessons of this chapter. Fair housing questions come in three shapes:

- **vocabulary:** steering, blockbusting or redlining?
- **dates:** which protected class was added in which year?
- **exemptions:** does one apply, and was it lost?

## 3. Simple explanation

### Two federal laws with different reach

1. **Civil Rights Act of 1866.** Bans discrimination based on **race** in the sale or rental of **all property**, real or personal. It has **no exceptions** and is enforced by a lawsuit in federal court.
2. **Fair Housing Act of 1968** (Title VIII). Covers housing, protects more classes, and has a few narrow **exemptions**.

Why this matters: a Fair Housing Act exemption never protects **racial** discrimination, because the 1866 Act still applies. That is why every exam answer offering a "racial exemption" is wrong.

### The protected classes, in order

- **1968:** race, color, religion, national origin.
- **1974:** **sex** (added by the Housing and Community Development Act).
- **1988:** **familial status** and **handicap** (added by the Fair Housing Amendments Act, enacted September 13, 1988). HUD's rules now say **disability** instead of handicap.

**Familial status** covers households with children under 18, pregnant women, and people in the process of obtaining custody of a child. Age and marital status are **not** on the federal fair housing list. They are credit (ECOA) categories, covered in L07-2.

### What is prohibited

Because of a protected class, no one may refuse to sell or rent, offer different terms, publish a discriminatory ad, or falsely say a home is unavailable. Three practices have names:

- **Steering:** directing buyers or renters **toward or away from** areas because of a protected class. It targets **buyers**.
- **Blockbusting** (panic peddling): persuading owners to sell by suggesting that a protected class is moving into the neighborhood. It targets **sellers**.
- **Redlining:** a **lender** refusing loans, or changing loan terms, in certain areas for discriminatory reasons. It targets **loans**.

### Disability: modification vs accommodation

- **Reasonable modification:** a **physical change** to the unit, such as grab bars or a ramp, generally **at the tenant's expense**.
- **Reasonable accommodation:** a change to a **rule or policy**, such as allowing an assistance animal despite a no-pets rule.

### Exemptions, and how they are lost

| Exemption | Conditions |
|---|---|
| Private owner of single-family houses | Owns **no more than 3** such houses at one time. **No broker, agent or salesperson** is used, and no one "in the business" of selling or renting. **No discriminatory advertising.** An owner who was not living in the house, or was not its most recent occupant, gets the exemption for only **one sale in any 24 months**. |
| "Mrs. Murphy" (owner-occupied) | A building with living quarters for **no more than 4 families** living independently, and the **owner lives in one** of the units. |
| Religious organizations | May limit noncommercial housing they own or operate to members of the same religion, or give them preference, **unless** membership is restricted by race, color or national origin. |
| Private clubs | A club not in fact open to the public may limit lodgings it provides as an incidental, noncommercial part of its purpose to its members. |
| Housing for older persons | May exclude children, so the **familial status** protection does not apply. Two tests: **62+**, where all occupants are 62 or older; or **55+**, where at least **80% of occupied units** have at least one person 55 or older, with published policies and age verification. |

A person is "in the business" if, in the preceding 12 months, they acted as principal in 3 or more dwelling sales or rentals, or as agent in 2 or more.

**The two exemption killers:** using a **broker** and publishing a **discriminatory ad**. For the single-family exemption, both are written into the statute. The manual applies the same rule to every exemption.

### Enforcement

- A complaint goes to **HUD within 1 year** after the discriminatory practice occurred or ended.
- A civil **lawsuit** must be filed **within 2 years**. The clock stops while a HUD proceeding on the same practice is pending, and a person may sue without filing with HUD first.
- Brokerage offices display the **equal housing opportunity poster**.
- Florida has its own Fair Housing Act (Chapter 760, F.S.), which mirrors the federal law. Its details were not verified for this course.

## 4. Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Protected class | A characteristic the law forbids using as a basis for housing decisions |
| Familial status | Household with children under 18, pregnant women, or people obtaining custody |
| Steering | Directing buyers or renters toward or away from areas because of a protected class |
| Blockbusting (panic peddling) | Inducing owners to sell by suggesting that a protected class is moving in |
| Redlining | A lender refusing or changing loans by area for discriminatory reasons |
| Reasonable modification | Physical change to a unit for a person with a disability, generally at the tenant's expense |
| Reasonable accommodation | Change in a rule or policy for a person with a disability |
| "Mrs. Murphy" exemption | Owner-occupied building of no more than 4 units |
| Housing for older persons | 62+ (all occupants) or 55+ (80% of occupied units) housing that may exclude children |
| HUD | U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development, which receives fair housing complaints |

## 5. Practical example

**Daniela** is a sales associate in Tampa. In one week:

| Situation | Lawful? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A couple from Brazil asks for 3-bedroom homes. Without being asked, Daniela shows only homes in "neighborhoods with Brazilian families". | **No** | Steering on national origin, even if the buyers seem pleased |
| An owner of three single-family homes lists one with Daniela and says "no families with kids" | **No** | A broker is used, so the exemption is gone. Daniela must refuse the instruction. |
| An owner lives in one unit of her fourplex and rents another without a broker or ads. She prefers tenants without children. | **Possibly exempt** | Mrs. Murphy exemption (familial status). It would end the moment she hired Daniela or ran a discriminatory ad. |
| The same owner refuses a tenant because of race | **No** | 1866 Act: no exemption for race, ever |
| A tenant with an assistance animal asks to live in a no-pets building | **Must generally allow** | Reasonable **accommodation** (a rule change) |
| A 55+ community in Sarasota County, where 85% of occupied units have a resident 55 or older and which publishes its policies and verifies ages, turns away a family with a toddler | **Lawful** | Housing for older persons. It removes only the familial status protection. |
| A flyer reads: "A family of [ethnic group] just bought on your street. Sell now!" | **No** | Blockbusting |

## 6. Portugal comparison

**Familiar principle.** Portuguese law forbids discrimination in general, through constitutional equality and specific anti-discrimination legislation. The details *require verification*.

**Florida rule.** The exam tests the US federal framework: the 1866 Act (race, no exceptions), the Fair Housing Act's seven classes, three named practices, and narrow exemptions lost when a broker or a discriminatory ad is used.

**Precise difference.** US law works from a **closed list** of classes and **named** practices. General ideas of fairness will not tell you which year sex was added, or whether a fourplex owner is exempt.

**Exam implication.** No exemption ever applies to race. Answer with the federal list and dates, never by analogy with Portuguese law.

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

**Memorize exactly**

- **1866:** race; all property; **no exceptions** (manual; not yet verified).
- **1968:** race, color, religion, national origin. **1974:** sex. **1988:** familial status and handicap (disability).
- Single-family owner exemption: **no more than 3** houses; **no broker** (or anyone in the business); **no discriminatory ad**; a non-occupant owner gets **one sale per 24 months**.
- Mrs. Murphy: **no more than 4** units (families living independently), and the **owner lives in one**.
- Older persons: **62+** for all occupants, or **55+** in at least **80% of occupied units**, with published policies and age verification.
- HUD complaint within **1 year**; lawsuit within **2 years**.

**Understand**

- Religious organizations lose the exemption if membership is restricted by race, color or national origin. Private clubs: only incidental, noncommercial lodgings.
- The 80% counts **occupied** units only; HUD says the other 20% need not be under 55. Older-persons housing is exempt only from the familial status protection.
- The lawsuit clock pauses while a HUD proceeding is pending; suing does not require a HUD complaint first.
- "Broker or discriminatory ad ends the exemption" is in the statute for the single-family exemption. Applying it to every exemption comes from the manual (manual; not yet verified for the other exemptions). On the exam, a broker or a discriminatory ad means no exemption.
- These come from the manual only (manual; not yet verified): the definitions of steering, blockbusting and redlining; the modification/accommodation rules; enforcement of the 1866 Act in federal court; and the poster requirement. The familial status definition is also unverified; the "obtaining custody" part comes from the course notes.
- Florida's Fair Housing Act (Chapter 760, F.S.) has not been verified for this course.

## 9. Common misconceptions

- **"Sex and familial status were both added in 1988."** Sex was added in **1974**. 1988 added familial status and handicap.
- **"Age and marital status are fair housing classes."** Not under the federal Fair Housing Act. They are ECOA (credit) classes.
- **"An owner who qualifies for an exemption can still use a broker."** Using a broker ends the exemption.
- **"An exempt owner may refuse a buyer because of race."** Never: the 1866 Act has no exceptions.
- **"Showing buyers 'where people like them live' is good service."** It is steering, even if the buyer seems pleased.
- **"Blockbusting is done to buyers."** It targets **owners**, pushing them to sell.
- **"The tenant's grab bars are an accommodation."** A physical change is a **modification**. A rule change, like allowing an assistance animal, is an **accommodation**.
- **"A 55+ community needs a person 55 or older in every unit."** It needs one in at least **80% of occupied units**. The "everyone" rule belongs to **62+** housing, where all occupants are 62 or older.
