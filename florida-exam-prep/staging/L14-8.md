## 2. Why it matters

The closing statement is where everything in this chapter comes together: the price, the deposit, the loan, the transfer taxes (L14-6), the prorations (L14-7) and the commission. Buyers want to know the exact **cash to close**, and sellers want to know their **net proceeds**. As a sales associate you will review these figures before closing and explain each line.

The exam usually asks for **one cell** of the statement, for example "How does the binder deposit appear?" or "How is an assumed mortgage entered?" It also asks you to compute the buyer's cash due or the seller's net. Each line follows from simple logic, so learn the logic rather than memorizing a table.

## 3. Simple explanation

### Step 1: What it represents

A **closing statement** (settlement statement) is the accounting of the sale for each party. Each party has two columns:

- **Debit:** an amount the party **owes** at closing (is charged).
- **Credit:** an amount the party **receives** at closing, or has already paid toward what it owes.

At the bottom, the buyer's debits minus credits is the cash the buyer must bring. The seller's credits minus debits is the cash the seller takes home.

### Step 2: Units

- **Money:** dollars and cents.
- **Entries:** each line goes into one or more cells (buyer debit, buyer credit, seller debit, seller credit).
- **Single entry:** the line appears for **one party only**, because the money moves between that party and an outsider (the lender, the tax collector, the brokerage, the title company).
- **Double entry:** the line appears for **both parties**, as a debit to one and a credit to the other for the same amount, because money or value (such as a debt taken over) moves **between buyer and seller**.

### Step 3: Formula

| Result | Formula |
|---|---|
| Cash due **from the buyer** | Total buyer debits − total buyer credits |
| Cash due **to the seller** | Total seller credits − total seller debits |

If the seller's debits are larger than the seller's credits, the result is negative and the seller must bring money to closing.

### Step 4: When it applies

Ask for each line: **does money or value move between buyer and seller (double entry), or between one party and an outsider (single entry)?**

| Item | Buyer | Seller | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Purchase price | Debit | Credit | Buyer owes it; seller receives it (double) |
| Binder (earnest money) deposit | **Credit** (single entry) | — | Already paid into escrow; counts toward the price. The seller is already credited with the full price |
| New mortgage | **Credit** | — | Lender's money pays part of the price for the buyer (single) |
| **Assumed mortgage** | **Credit** | **Debit** | Double entry (V-24, corrected) |
| Existing mortgage payoff | — | Debit | Seller's own debt paid to the lender from proceeds (single) |
| Deed doc stamps | — | Debit | Customarily the seller's tax (single) |
| Note stamps + intangible tax | Debit | — | Customarily the buyer's taxes on financing (single) |
| Unpaid taxes (proration) | Credit | Debit | Seller owes the buyer for the seller's days (double) |
| Accrued interest on an assumed loan | Credit | Debit | Paid in arrears; seller owes the buyer (double) |
| Rent the seller collected in advance | Credit | Debit | Seller holds the buyer's days (double) |
| Prepaid dues or insurance paid by the seller | Debit | Credit | Buyer owes the seller for days after closing (double) |
| Commission | — | Debit (customarily) | Seller customarily pays the listing brokerage (single) |
| Buyer's other closing costs (lender, title) | Debit | — | Paid to outsiders (single) |
| Seller's other closing costs | — | Debit | Paid to outsiders (single) |

**Correction (V-24):** the study manual's table shows the assumed mortgage as a **buyer credit only**. That is wrong. An assumed mortgage is a **double entry**. The buyer is credited because the debt the buyer takes over pays part of the price. The seller is debited because the seller hands over the debt instead of receiving that amount in cash. Compare: a **new** mortgage is a buyer credit only, and the seller's **payoff** of an existing loan is a seller debit only.

### Step 5: Worked example

**An assumption sale (outside Miami-Dade).** Price $200,000; deposit $10,000; the buyer assumes the seller's $120,000 loan; unpaid taxes proration $1,500; commission 6% = $12,000; deed stamps 2,000 × $0.70 = $1,400; note stamps on the assumed note (course convention, V-25) 1,200 × $0.35 = $420; intangible tax $0 (not new money).

| Line | Buyer debit | Buyer credit | Seller debit | Seller credit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Price | 200,000 | | | 200,000 |
| Deposit | | 10,000 | | |
| Assumed mortgage | | 120,000 | 120,000 | |
| Taxes (proration) | | 1,500 | 1,500 | |
| Commission | | | 12,000 | |
| Deed stamps | | | 1,400 | |
| Note stamps | 420 | | | |
| **Totals** | **200,420** | **131,500** | **134,900** | **200,000** |

Buyer brings 200,420 − 131,500 = **$68,920**. Seller receives 200,000 − 134,900 = **$65,100**.

### Step 6: Reasonableness check

- **Quick estimate for the buyer:** start with price − loan − deposit, then add the buyer's other debits and subtract the buyer's other credits. Here 200,000 − 120,000 − 10,000 = 70,000, plus 420 and minus 1,500 = 68,920 ✓.
- **Money in = money out.** Cash coming in (buyer's cash + deposit + any new loan) must equal cash going out (seller's net + payoffs + commission + taxes + other costs). Here, in: 68,920 + 10,000 = 78,920. Out: 65,100 + 12,000 + 1,400 + 420 = 78,920 ✓. Double entries between buyer and seller cancel out and never appear in this check.

### Step 7: Common errors

| Error | Correct |
|---|---|
| Deposit as a seller credit (or a double entry) | Buyer credit only. The seller is already credited with the full price |
| Assumed mortgage as a buyer credit only (the manual's table) | **Debit seller, credit buyer** (V-24) |
| New mortgage as a seller debit | Buyer credit only. The seller's own old loan is the payoff |
| Unpaid-tax proration as debit buyer | Debit seller, credit buyer |
| Seller's payoff on the buyer's side | Seller debit only |
| Subtracting in the wrong order | Buyer: debits − credits. Seller: credits − debits |
| Forgetting the deposit when computing the buyer's cash | The deposit is a credit and reduces the cash due |

### Step 9: Independent practice

A Sarasota home sells for **$260,000**. The buyer's deposit is $13,000, and the buyer **assumes** the seller's loan with a balance of $150,000. Accrued interest owed by the seller on that loan is $375; the unpaid-tax proration is $1,120. The buyer pays note stamps on the assumed note ($525) and other costs of $1,900. The seller pays deed stamps ($1,820), a 6% commission and other costs of $800.

**P1.** How much cash must the buyer bring?

<details><summary>Check your answer</summary>

Debits: 260,000 + 525 + 1,900 = $262,425. Credits: 13,000 + 150,000 + 375 + 1,120 = $164,495. Cash due = **$97,930**.

</details>

**P2.** How much does the seller receive?

<details><summary>Check your answer</summary>

Commission = $15,600. Debits: 150,000 + 375 + 1,120 + 1,820 + 15,600 + 800 = $169,715. Net = 260,000 − 169,715 = **$90,285**.

</details>

**P3.** If you followed the manual's uncorrected table (assumed mortgage as a buyer credit only), what seller net would you get, and why is it impossible?

<details><summary>Check your answer</summary>

$90,285 + $150,000 = **$240,285**. The seller would be "paid" in cash for a debt the buyer took over. Money in ($97,930 + $13,000 = $110,930) would no longer equal money out. Correct entry: debit seller, credit buyer (V-24).

</details>

**P4.** Which of these is a **single** entry: the purchase price, the binder deposit, the unpaid-tax proration, the assumed mortgage?

<details><summary>Check your answer</summary>

**The binder deposit** (credit buyer only). The other three are double entries.

</details>

### Step 10: Exam application

- **One-cell questions:** decide whether money or value moves between the parties (double) or to or from an outsider (single), then who owes and who receives.
- **Most-tested cells:** deposit = buyer credit only. New loan = buyer credit only. Assumed loan = debit seller + credit buyer. Payoff = seller debit only. Unpaid taxes = debit seller, credit buyer. Commission = seller debit (customarily).
- **Cash questions:** write two lists, debits and credits, for the party asked about. Do not mix in the other party's lines.
- If a practice source shows an assumed mortgage as a buyer credit only, it reflects the manual's error. Answer **debit seller, credit buyer**. (If a question asks only about the buyer's side, the buyer's entry is a credit.)

## 4. Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Closing (settlement) statement | The accounting of the sale showing each party's debits and credits |
| Debit | An amount a party owes at closing |
| Credit | An amount a party receives at closing, or has already paid toward the price |
| Single entry | A line that appears for one party only (money to or from an outsider) |
| Double entry | The same amount debited to one party and credited to the other |
| Binder (earnest money) deposit | The buyer's good-faith deposit held in escrow; credited to the buyer at closing |
| Payoff | Paying off the seller's existing loan from the seller's proceeds |
| Assumed mortgage | An existing loan the buyer takes over; debit seller, credit buyer |
| Cash to close | Buyer's debits − buyer's credits |
| Seller's net proceeds | Seller's credits − seller's debits |

## 5. Practical example

**A financed sale in Orlando (Orange County).** Price **$385,000**; closing **August 12** (non-leap year; the buyer owns the closing day under the course convention, V-79). The buyer's **$20,000** deposit is held in escrow by the title company. The buyer has a **new** $308,000 loan (80% of the price; the appraisal was not lower than the price). The seller's existing loan payoff is **$210,000**. The listing agreement provides a **5%** commission. Annual property taxes of **$6,205** are unpaid. The buyer's other closing costs are $4,800, and the seller's are $1,200.

## 6. Portugal comparison

**Familiar principle:** in any sale, someone must account for the price, the loans and the taxes so each party knows what it pays and receives.

**Florida rule:** on the Florida closing statement, deed stamps are customarily a **seller debit**, and note stamps and intangible tax are **buyer debits** on financing.

**Precise difference (PT-09):** in Portugal the buyer pays IMT and Imposto do Selo, so the main transfer taxes sit on the buyer's side. In Florida the main deed tax customarily sits on the **seller's** side. How a Portuguese sale is documented and settled between the parties is not covered by this course's comparison entries and **requires verification**.

**Exam implication:** place deed stamps as a seller debit unless the question says the contract allocates them differently.

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

**Memorize exactly**

- **Debit** = what a party owes. **Credit** = what a party receives.
- Price: debit buyer, credit seller. Deposit: **credit buyer only** (single). New mortgage: **credit buyer only**. Payoff: **debit seller only**.
- **Assumed mortgage: debit seller AND credit buyer** (double entry). This is a correction of the manual's table (V-24).
- Prorations between the parties: double entries. Unpaid taxes: debit seller, credit buyer.
- Deed stamps: seller debit. Note stamps + intangible tax: buyer debit. Commission: seller debit. These reflect custom (manual; not yet verified), and contracts can allocate differently.
- **Buyer's cash = buyer debits − buyer credits. Seller's cash = seller credits − seller debits.**

**Understand**

- V-24 (corrected) verifies the three mortgage lines: assumed = double entry; new = buyer credit only; payoff = seller debit only. The other placements follow the same accounting logic and come from the study manual (manual; not yet verified).
- A double entry moves value from one party to the other, so it changes both nets but cancels out in the money-in = money-out check.
- The deposit is a credit to the buyer and not to the seller, because the seller is already credited with the full price, and the deposit is part of that price.

## 9. Common misconceptions

- **"The deposit is credited to the seller."** It is a **buyer credit only**. The seller's credit is the full price.
- **"An assumed mortgage is just a buyer credit, like a new loan."** It is a double entry: debit seller, credit buyer (V-24 corrects the manual).
- **"The new loan is debited to the seller."** The seller's debit is the payoff of the seller's own old loan, not the buyer's new loan.
- **"Unpaid taxes are a buyer debit."** Debit seller, credit buyer: the buyer will pay the whole bill later.
- **"Commission is split between buyer and seller on the statement."** It is customarily a seller debit.

## 10. Memory sheet

- Debit = owes · credit = receives.
- **Deposit: buyer credit only. New loan: buyer credit only. Payoff: seller debit only.**
- **Assumed loan: debit seller + credit buyer** (V-24 corrects the manual).
- Prorations between the parties: double entries (unpaid taxes → debit seller, credit buyer).
- Deed stamps + commission → seller debits; note stamps + intangible → buyer debits (custom: manual; not yet verified).
- Buyer brings debits − credits; seller gets credits − debits; money in = money out.
