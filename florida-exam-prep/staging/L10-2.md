## 2. Why it matters

Rural Florida land is often priced **per acre** and described by fractions of a section ("the NW¼ of the SE¼ of Section 22"). You must turn that description, or lot dimensions in feet, into acres and then into a price, all in US units, while your Portuguese clients think in hectares.

On the exam, Section 10 "Legal Descriptions" carries about 5% of the questions (V-02), and the study manual treats section-and-township arithmetic as near-certain exam material: "How many acres in ...?", "... AND ...", "What does it cost at $X per acre?", "Where is section 1?"

## 3. Simple explanation

### Step 1: What it represents

The government survey (L10-1) lays a grid over the land (all facts in this step: manual; not yet verified. The township and section figures are N-100 and N-101, listed under V-26):

- A **township** is **6 miles × 6 miles = 36 square miles**, divided into **36 sections** (N-100).
- A **section** is **1 mile × 1 mile = 1 square mile = 640 acres** (N-101).
- Sections are numbered in a **serpentine** pattern (back and forth, like a snake). **Section 1 is in the northeast corner**. Numbering runs **west** to section 6, then back **east** (7–12), west again (13–18), and so on, ending with **section 36 in the southeast** corner. **Section 16** was traditionally reserved for schools.

An **aliquot part** is a fraction of a section named by direction: halves (N½, S½, E½, W½) and quarters (NE¼, NW¼, SE¼, SW¼). Fractions can be nested: "the NW¼ **of** the SE¼" is a quarter of a quarter. One section divided into quarter-quarters:

| ← West | | | East → |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| NW¼ of NW¼ | NE¼ of NW¼ | NW¼ of NE¼ | NE¼ of NE¼ |
| SW¼ of NW¼ | SE¼ of NW¼ | SW¼ of NE¼ | SE¼ of NE¼ |
| NW¼ of SW¼ | NE¼ of SW¼ | **NW¼ of SE¼** | NE¼ of SE¼ |
| SW¼ of SW¼ | SE¼ of SW¼ | SW¼ of SE¼ | SE¼ of SE¼ |

<p class="caption">North is up. Each square is 1,320 ft × 1,320 ft = 40 acres. Each 2 × 2 corner block is a 160-acre quarter section.</p>

**To locate a parcel, read the fractions right to left.** "The NW¼ of the SE¼": first find the SE¼ of the section (the bottom-right block), then take its NW¼ (the highlighted square).

### Step 2: Units

| Unit | Equals | Measures | Memory aid |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 mile | **5,280 feet** | Length | "Five tomatoes": 5-2-80 |
| 1 acre | **43,560 square feet** | Area | 43-5-60 |
| 1 square yard | **9 square feet** (3 ft × 3 ft) | Area | 3 × 3, not 3 |
| 1 square mile | **640 acres** | Area | 640 in a section |
| 1 section | 1 square mile = 640 acres | Area | |
| 1 township | 36 sections = 36 square miles | Area | 6 × 6 |

(Manual; not yet verified, V-26. These are standard measurement definitions, but the course has not yet checked them against an official source.)

Feet and miles measure **lines**. Square feet, square yards, acres and square miles measure **areas**. An area is length × width, so a square unit is the side length multiplied by itself. Check: 1 square mile = 5,280 ft × 5,280 ft = 27,878,400 sq ft, and 640 acres × 43,560 = 27,878,400 sq ft. The same number, so the constants agree.

**Metric intuition (for understanding only):** 1 acre ≈ 4,047 m² ≈ 0.405 ha. 640 acres ≈ 259 ha. 1 mile ≈ 1.61 km (PT-17; arithmetic checked with 1 ft = 0.3048 m exactly). Exam math never uses metric.

### Step 3: Formula

| Task | Formula |
|---|---|
| Acreage of an aliquot part | **640 ÷ each denominator** (same as 640 ÷ the product of the denominators) |
| Description joined by **AND** | Compute each parcel separately, then **add** |
| Square feet → acres | sq ft **÷ 43,560** |
| Acres → square feet | acres **× 43,560** |
| Feet → miles / miles → feet | **÷ 5,280** / **× 5,280** |
| Square feet → square yards | sq ft **÷ 9** |
| Square miles → acres / acres → square miles | **× 640** / **÷ 640** |
| Rectangle in feet | length × width = sq ft |
| Price | acres × price per acre |

**The acreage shortcut in action:**

- NW¼ of the SE¼ = 640 ÷ 4 ÷ 4 = **40 acres**.
- S½ of the NE¼ = 640 ÷ 2 ÷ 4 = **80 acres**.
- "N½ of the NW¼ **AND** the SW¼" = 80 + 160 = **240 acres**.

**Why it works.** Each "of" takes a fraction of the piece before it: half of a quarter is ½ × ¼ = ⅛ of the section, and 640 ÷ 8 = 80. **AND** joins separate parcels. After AND you start again from the full 640-acre section.

### Step 4: When it applies

- **Fractions of a section:** start from 640. The order of the fractions does not change the acreage.
- **"Of" versus "AND":** "of" nests one piece inside another (divide). "AND" adds a separate parcel (add).
- **Where versus how big:** to *locate* a parcel, read right to left. For its *size*, just divide.
- **Dimensions in feet or a price per acre:** get square feet, then acres, then the price.

### Step 5: Worked example

**A. Nested fractions.** "The SW¼ of the NE¼ of the NW¼ of Section 22."

| Read right to left | Piece | Acres |
|---|---|---|
| NW¼ | The northwest quarter of the section | 640 ÷ 4 = 160 |
| NE¼ of that | Its northeast quarter | 160 ÷ 4 = 40 |
| SW¼ of that | Its southwest quarter | 40 ÷ 4 = **10** |

Check: 4 × 4 × 4 = 64, and 640 ÷ 64 = 10 acres.

**B. AND.** "The W½ of the SW¼ AND the SE¼ of the SE¼."

- Parcel 1: W½ of the SW¼ = 640 ÷ 2 ÷ 4 = 80 acres.
- Parcel 2: SE¼ of the SE¼ = 640 ÷ 4 ÷ 4 = 40 acres.
- Total = 80 + 40 = **120 acres**.

Trap: reading it as one long chain gives 640 ÷ (2 × 4 × 4 × 4) = 640 ÷ 128 = 5 acres. That is wrong, because AND starts a new parcel.

**C. Feet to acres to price.** A rectangular tract in Polk County measures 660 ft × 1,320 ft and sells for $12,500 per acre.

- Area: 660 × 1,320 = 871,200 sq ft.
- Acres: 871,200 ÷ 43,560 = **20 acres**.
- Price: 20 × $12,500 = **$250,000**.
- Cross-check: 660 ft is half of 1,320 ft, so this is half of a 40-acre quarter-quarter: 20 acres.

**D. Township in feet and acres.** Each side is 6 miles × 5,280 = **31,680 ft**. The area is 36 square miles × 640 = **23,040 acres**.

### Step 6: Reasonableness check

- Parcels within one section can never total more than **640 acres**.
- Every extra "of" makes the parcel **smaller**. Every "AND" makes the total **larger**.
- A single aliquot part is always one of 320, 160, 80, 40, 20, 10, 5, 2.5 ... acres. A result such as 37.5 acres for one aliquot part means a slip.
- Anchors: **half = 320**, **quarter = 160**, **quarter-quarter = 40** acres.
- The number of acres is far smaller than the number of square feet. The number of square yards is one-ninth of the number of square feet.

### Step 7: Common errors

| Error | Example | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Dividing by 3 instead of 9 for square yards | 43,560 ÷ 3 = 14,520 sq yd per acre (wrong) | 43,560 ÷ 9 = **4,840** sq yd per acre |
| Chaining or multiplying across AND | Example B read as one chain = 5 acres | Compute each parcel, then **add**: 120 acres |
| Multiplying square feet by 43,560 | 871,200 × 43,560 = a huge number | Square feet → acres: **divide** |
| Mixing the mile and the acre | "5,280 sq ft in an acre" | 5,280 = feet in a **mile**. 43,560 = sq ft in an **acre** |
| Reading left to right to locate | In "NW¼ of the SE¼", looking first at the section's NW¼ | Start with the **last** fraction (SE¼) |

### Step 8: Guided practice

**G1.** How many acres are in the E½ of the NW¼ of the SW¼ of a section?
*Hint: three denominators, 2, 4 and 4. Divide 640 by each.*

<details><summary>Check your answer</summary>

**20 acres.** 640 ÷ 4 = 160 (SW¼) → ÷ 4 = 40 (NW¼ of it) → ÷ 2 = 20 (E½). Check: 2 × 4 × 4 = 32, and 640 ÷ 32 = 20.

</details>

**G2.** How many acres are in "the S½ of the NE¼ of the NW¼ AND the SE¼"?
*Hint: find the AND first. Two parcels.*

<details><summary>Check your answer</summary>

**180 acres.** Parcel 1: 640 ÷ 4 ÷ 4 ÷ 2 = 20 acres. Parcel 2: the SE¼ = 640 ÷ 4 = 160 acres. Total 20 + 160 = 180 acres.

</details>

**G3.** A rectangular parcel measures 990 ft by 1,320 ft. How many acres is it?
*Hint: length × width first, then divide by 43,560.*

<details><summary>Check your answer</summary>

**30 acres.** 990 × 1,320 = 1,306,800 sq ft. 1,306,800 ÷ 43,560 = 30 acres. Check: 43,560 × 30 = 1,306,800.

</details>

**G4.** Which section lies directly south of section 16?
*Hint: use the township grid in part 8 of this lesson (Visual explanation). Find section 16's column, then go down one row.*

<details><summary>Check your answer</summary>

**Section 21.** Row 3 runs 18, 17, **16**, 15, 14, 13 from west to east, so 16 is the third column from the west. Row 4 runs 19, 20, **21**, 22, 23, 24, so its third column from the west is 21.

</details>

### Step 9: Independent practice

**I1.** A road frontage of 7,920 ft is how many miles?

<details><summary>Check your answer</summary>

**1.5 miles.** 7,920 ÷ 5,280 = 1.5. Check: 5,280 × 1.5 = 7,920.

</details>

**I2.** A buyer pays $4,500 per acre for the N½ of the SE¼ of the SE¼ of a section. What is the price?

<details><summary>Check your answer</summary>

**$90,000.** Acreage: 640 ÷ 4 ÷ 4 ÷ 2 = 20 acres. Price: 20 × $4,500 = $90,000.

</details>

**I3.** How many acres are in "the E½ AND the NE¼ of the SW¼" of a section?

<details><summary>Check your answer</summary>

**360 acres.** E½ = 640 ÷ 2 = 320. NE¼ of the SW¼ = 640 ÷ 4 ÷ 4 = 40. Total 320 + 40 = 360.

</details>

**I4.** How many square yards are in 1.5 acres?

<details><summary>Check your answer</summary>

**7,260 sq yd.** 1.5 × 43,560 = 65,340 sq ft. 65,340 ÷ 9 = 7,260 sq yd. Check: 1.5 × 4,840 = 7,260.

</details>

**I5.** In which corner of a township is section 31?

<details><summary>Check your answer</summary>

**Southwest.** The bottom row runs 31, 32, 33, 34, 35, 36 from west to east, so 31 is in the southwest and 36 in the southeast.

</details>

### Step 10: Exam application

Exam routine for any section question:

1. Circle every **AND**: each one starts a new parcel.
2. For each parcel, divide 640 by its denominators.
3. Add the parcels.
4. Multiply by any price per acre. For a lot given in feet: length × width ÷ 43,560 first.
5. Run the Step 6 checks.

If the answer choices include both a "divided" result and an "added" result, look again for the word AND. Recall questions ("sections in a township?", "acres in a section?", "corner of section 1?") are answered by 36, 640 and northeast.

## 5. Practical example

**Rui**, a client from Lisbon, wants a small horse farm near Ocala (Marion County). The listing's legal description reads: "the S½ of the NW¼ of the NE¼ AND the NE¼ of the SW¼ of Section 12." The asking price is $15,000 per acre.

| Question | Work | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| Is this one parcel or two? | The word AND | **Two separate parcels** |
| Parcel 1 acreage | 640 ÷ 4 (NE¼) ÷ 4 (NW¼) ÷ 2 (S½) | 20 acres |
| Parcel 2 acreage | 640 ÷ 4 (SW¼) ÷ 4 (NE¼) | 40 acres |
| Total | 20 + 40 | **60 acres** |
| Price | 60 × $15,000 | **$900,000** |
| Where is each piece? (right to left) | Parcel 1: in the NE¼, its NW¼, the south half of that. Parcel 2: in the SW¼, its NE¼ | The two pieces do not touch: a row of quarter-quarters lies between them |
| Where is Section 12? | Row 2 of the grid runs 7 → 12 west to east | On the **east edge**, second row from the north |
| Fence around the 40-acre parcel | 4 sides × 1,320 ft | **5,280 ft = exactly 1 mile** |
| Rui asks: "In hectares?" | 60 × 0.405 | **About 24.3 ha** (intuition only) |

You give Rui every figure in acres and feet, because the contract and the deed use US units. The hectare figure only helps him picture the land.

## 6. Portugal comparison

**The familiar principle (PT-17).** In Portugal you measure land and homes in the metric system: m² and hectares (1 ha = 10,000 m²).

**The Florida rule.** US customary units: feet, square feet, acres (43,560 sq ft), miles (5,280 ft) and square yards (9 sq ft). Exam math never uses metric.

**The precise difference: convert your intuition, not your answers.**

| Florida | Approximate metric |
|---|---|
| 1 sq ft | 0.0929 m² |
| 1,000 sq ft | 93 m² |
| 2,000 sq ft house | about 186 m², a large T4 in Portuguese terms |
| 1 acre | 4,047 m² ≈ 0.405 ha |
| 1 section (640 acres) | about 259 ha |
| 1 mile | 1.61 km |

(PT-17; arithmetic checked with 1 ft = 0.3048 m exactly.)

**Exam implication.** Work in feet and acres from start to finish. Converting to metric and back only adds rounding errors. When a lot is given as two dimensions, the **front feet** are the frontage dimension, the first number given (PT-17). Memory aids: acre = 43,560 ("43-5-60"); mile = 5,280 ("five tomatoes": 5-2-80).

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

**Memorize exactly**

- **Township = 6 × 6 miles = 36 square miles = 36 sections** (N-100; manual; not yet verified, V-26).
- **Section = 1 mile × 1 mile = 1 square mile = 640 acres** (N-101; manual; not yet verified, V-26).
- **Section 1 in the northeast**, serpentine numbering (west to 6, back east 7–12, ...), **section 36 in the southeast**. Section 16 was traditionally reserved for schools (manual; not yet verified).
- **1 acre = 43,560 sq ft · 1 mile = 5,280 ft · 1 square yard = 9 sq ft · 1 square mile = 640 acres** (manual; not yet verified, V-26).
- Acreage: **640 ÷ each denominator**. **AND = compute each parcel and add.** Locate: **read right to left** (manual; not yet verified).
- Metric for intuition only: 1 acre ≈ 4,047 m² ≈ 0.405 ha. 640 acres ≈ 259 ha (PT-17).

**Understand**

- Each "of" takes a fraction of the previous piece, so the denominators multiply and the parcel shrinks.
- AND starts a new parcel from the full section, so the acreages add.
- Square units are side × side: that is why a square yard is 9 sq ft, not 3.
- The constants are consistent: 640 × 43,560 = 5,280 × 5,280 = 27,878,400 sq ft in a square mile.
- Source status: all survey facts and constants come from the study manual and are labelled "not yet verified" (V-26). They are standard definitions, but the course has not yet checked them against an official source. The metric conversions are arithmetic checks (PT-17), not law.

## 9. Common misconceptions

- **"Divide 43,560 by 3 to get square yards."** A square yard is 3 ft × 3 ft = **9** sq ft. Divide by 9 (4,840 sq yd per acre).
- **"Multiply the acreages when the description says AND."** AND joins separate parcels: compute each and **add**.
- **"More fractions make a bigger parcel."** Each added "of" makes it **smaller**.
- **"A township is 640 acres."** A **section** is 640 acres. A township is 36 sections (36 square miles). Do not confuse N-100 with N-101.
- **"Section 1 is in the northwest, like reading a page."** Section 1 is in the **northeast**. Section 36 is in the southeast.
- **"To find where the parcel is, start with the first fraction."** Read **right to left**: the last fraction is the biggest piece.
- **"I'll do the math in hectares and convert back."** Exam math never uses metric. Work in feet and acres.
