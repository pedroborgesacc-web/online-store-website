## 2. Why it matters

"Can the seller's creditors take this house?" In Florida that question has a constitutional answer, and it affects your work directly:

- **Who must sign.** If the home is a homestead and the owner is married, the spouse must join in the deed or mortgage even when title is in one name only (manual; not yet verified). A missing signature is a closing problem you can spot at listing.
- **Which debts follow the house.** A credit-card judgment creditor cannot force a sale of the homestead, and the judgment is not even a lien on it (V-66). Unpaid property taxes, the home's own mortgage and an unpaid contractor can all reach it.
- **Exam.** Property Rights is 8% of the state exam (V-02). Questions test the two size limits, the short list of claims that still reach a homestead, the spouse's role, and, as a favourite trap, whether you keep creditor protection separate from the $25,000 property-tax exemption.

## 3. Simple explanation

### One word, three different rules

In Florida, "homestead" is the name of three separate legal doctrines. They share only the word.

| # | Rule | What it does | Where taught |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Creditor protection** (Florida Constitution, Art. X, s. 4) | Shields the home from forced sale by most creditors | This lesson |
| 2 | **Family protection** (transfer restrictions) | Limits what the owner can do alone with the home when there is a spouse or minor child | This lesson |
| 3 | **Property-tax exemption** | Reduces the taxable value of the home | Chapter 18 |

### Rule 1: the shield against forced sale

Start with two words. A **judgment** is a court decision that a person owes money. A **forced sale** is a court-ordered sale of the debtor's property to pay that judgment.

The Florida Constitution says that a homestead owned by a **natural person** (a human being, not a company) is **exempt from forced sale** under court process, and **no judgment, decree or execution is a lien on it** (V-66, confirmed). In plain terms: an ordinary creditor who wins a lawsuit cannot make the court sell the owner's home, and the judgment does not even attach to it. The aim is to keep the family housed even if the owner falls into debt.

The shield protects a home, not unlimited land, so it has size limits:

| Location | Protected area (V-66) | Extra qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Inside a **municipality** (a city or town) | Up to **1/2 acre of contiguous land** | Limited to the residence of the owner or the owner's family |
| Outside a municipality | Up to **160 acres of contiguous land**, plus improvements | If the land is later brought into a municipality, the 160 acres cannot be reduced without the owner's consent |

**Contiguous** means touching: one connected piece of land, not separate lots. For scale: an acre is 43,560 square feet, so half an acre is 21,780 sq ft (roughly 2,000 m²); 160 acres is roughly 65 hectares. These conversions are only to help you picture the area; the legal figures are 1/2 acre and 160 acres.

### Who still gets through the shield

The constitution lists the claims the shield does **not** stop (V-66; s. 222.01(5), F.S. restates the same exceptions):

1. **Taxes and assessments** on the property, such as unpaid county property taxes.
2. **Obligations contracted for its purchase, improvement or repair.** This covers the mortgage that financed the home ("the mortgage on the home"), and debts for improving or repairing it.
3. **Obligations for house, field or other labor performed on the property.**

Items 2 and 3 together cover **construction liens** (also called mechanic's liens): liens for labor or materials used to improve the home.

The manual adds that **federal tax (IRS) liens** can also reach a homestead (manual; not yet verified).

**Why these?** Look at the pattern. The claims that get through come from the government that taxes this property and from the people who paid for, built or repaired this very house. The shield protects the home from *unrelated* debts (credit cards, medical bills, car loans, business debts), not from the costs of the home itself.

Two smaller verified details from the same section (V-66):

- It also exempts **$1,000 of personal property**. This is a different number for a different thing; do not confuse it with the $25,000 tax exemption.
- The exemptions **pass to the surviving spouse or heirs** when the owner dies.

### Rule 2: the family lock (transfer restrictions)

The second doctrine protects the owner's family from the owner acting alone.

- **The spouse joins.** If the owner is married, the **spouse must join** in any deed or mortgage of the homestead, **even if title is in one spouse's name** (manual; not yet verified). "Join" means the spouse signs the document too.
- **Devise is restricted.** To **devise** is to leave real property by will. If the owner has a **spouse or minor child**, the homestead cannot be freely devised away from them (manual; not yet verified).

**Why?** Without these rules, one spouse alone could sell, mortgage or will away the family's home. This limits the **owner's** power to transfer; it is not a rule about creditors.

### Rule 3: the tax exemption belongs to another chapter

The property-tax exemption (the first $25,000 exempt from all property taxes, plus a second exemption that applies to non-school taxes only; V-09) is taught in Chapter 18. It has its own conditions and numbers. A proposed 2026 homestead property-tax amendment (V-23) is **pending law, not law**. It concerns taxes only and does not change the creditor shield.

## 4. Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Homestead | A Florida owner's primary residence, which carries creditor protection, transfer restrictions and (separately) a tax exemption |
| Judgment / judgment creditor | A court decision that a person owes money / the person who won it |
| Forced sale | A court-ordered sale of a debtor's property to pay a creditor |
| Lien | A legal claim against property that secures a debt |
| Natural person | A human being, as opposed to a corporation or other entity |
| Municipality | A city or town. The 1/2-acre limit applies inside one |
| Contiguous | Touching; forming one connected piece of land |
| Assessment | A charge levied against the property, alongside taxes; it can reach the homestead |
| Construction (mechanic's) lien | A claim against the property by someone owed for labor or materials used to improve it |
| Join | To sign a deed or mortgage together with the owner |
| Devise | To leave real property by will |
| Minor child | A child who has not yet reached adulthood; triggers the devise restriction |

## 5. Practical example

**The Reyes home in Tampa.** Daniel Reyes owns a house on a **0.3-acre** lot inside the City of Tampa and lives there with his wife Sofia and their 9-year-old son. Title is in Daniel's name only. He has fallen behind on several debts. Which creditors can force a sale?

| Creditor | Claim | Can it force a sale of the homestead? |
|---|---|---|
| Credit-card company | Judgment for an unpaid card balance | **No.** 0.3 acre is within the 1/2-acre municipal limit, and the judgment is not a lien on the home |
| Hospital | Judgment for medical bills | **No.** An unrelated debt |
| Bank | The mortgage that financed the purchase, now in default | **Yes.** A purchase obligation: the bank can foreclose |
| Hillsborough County | Unpaid property taxes | **Yes.** Taxes and assessments get through |
| Pool contractor | Unpaid for building a pool; has a construction lien | **Yes.** Improvement and labor on the property |
| IRS | Federal tax lien | **Yes**, according to the manual (manual; not yet verified) |

Daniel decides to sell and asks you to list the house. Two points for you as the agent:

- Sofia is not on title, but she must **join in the deed** at closing (manual; not yet verified). Confirm early that she will sign.
- Daniel mentions that he once planned to leave the house to his brother by will. With a spouse and a minor child, that devise would be restricted (manual; not yet verified). That is a question for his attorney, not for you.

**Twist: the ranch.** Daniel's cousin lives on a 200-acre ranch outside any municipality. The shield covers up to **160 contiguous acres** plus improvements, not all 200 acres. If a city later brings the land inside its limits, the protected 160 acres cannot be reduced without the cousin's consent.

## 6. Portugal comparison

**Familiar principle.** In Portugal, disposing of the family home (*casa de morada de família*) generally needs both spouses' consent (requires verification).

**Florida rule.** Florida homestead is three separate rules:

1. A shield against forced sale by most creditors: 1/2 acre in a municipality, 160 contiguous acres outside (V-66).
2. A family lock: the spouse joins in deeds and mortgages, and devise is restricted when there is a spouse or minor child (manual; not yet verified).
3. A property-tax exemption (Chapter 18).

**Precise difference.** The spouse's participation in a sale will feel familiar. The unusual part is the creditor shield. In Florida an ordinary judgment creditor cannot force the sale of a homestead at all, but the shield does not stop property taxes, the home's own mortgage, or construction liens for work on the home. Our comparison entries do not say whether Portuguese law shields the family home from creditors in a similar way (requires verification).

**Exam implication.** Answer only with Florida rules; a Portuguese rule never answers a Florida question. Keep the creditor shield separate from the tax exemption: a question that mixes "1/2 acre" with "$25,000" is testing whether you know they are different doctrines.

*Memory aid: Homestead = shield, family lock, tax break. Three different rules.*

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

**Memorize exactly**

| Item | Rule | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Size inside a municipality | 1/2 acre of contiguous land; limited to the residence of the owner or the owner's family | Verified (V-66) |
| Size outside a municipality | 160 contiguous acres plus improvements; not reduced without the owner's consent if later brought into a municipality | Verified (V-66) |
| Who is protected | Homestead owned by a natural person | Verified (V-66) |
| Effect | Exempt from forced sale; no judgment, decree or execution is a lien on it | Verified (V-66) |
| Claims that still reach it | Taxes and assessments; obligations for its purchase, improvement or repair; labor performed on the property | Verified (V-66; s. 222.01(5)) |
| Federal (IRS) tax liens | Can also reach it | (manual; not yet verified) |
| Spouse | Must join in any deed or mortgage, even if title is in one name | (manual; not yet verified) |
| Devise | Cannot be freely devised away from a spouse or minor child | (manual; not yet verified) |
| Personal property | $1,000 exempt | Verified (V-66) |
| At the owner's death | Exemptions pass to the surviving spouse or heirs | Verified (V-66) |

**Understand**

- "Homestead" is three rules (shield, family lock, tax break), each with its own source, conditions and numbers.
- The claims that get through are tied to the house itself: its taxes and assessments, its purchase money, and its improvement, repair and labor. Unrelated debts are blocked within the size limits.
- The shield works against **creditors**. The family lock works against the **owner** acting alone.
- "Forced sale", "judgment creditor", "within a municipality" and "contiguous" signal the creditor-protection rule, not the tax exemption.

## 9. Common misconceptions

- **"The homestead limit is the $25,000 exemption."** This mixes creditor protection with the tax exemption. The shield is measured in **land** (1/2 acre or 160 acres). The $25,000 belongs to the tax exemption (Chapter 18).
- **"Homestead blocks the lender who financed the home."** It does not. Obligations for the home's purchase get through, so the lender can foreclose. Taxes and assessments, improvement and repair debts and labor on the property also get through, and the manual adds IRS liens (manual; not yet verified).
- **"160 acres applies everywhere."** Only outside a municipality. Inside one, the limit is 1/2 acre.
- **"Title is only in my name, so I can sell or mortgage alone."** The spouse must join (manual; not yet verified).
- **"The $1,000 in Art. X, s. 4 is the homestead exemption."** It is a personal-property exemption, not a land or tax figure.
- **"In Portugal both spouses consent, so that rule answers the exam."** The exam tests only Florida law.

- Shield (Art. X, s. 4; V-66): natural person · no forced sale · the judgment is not a lien.
- **1/2 acre in a municipality** (residence only) · **160 contiguous acres outside** (not reduced by later inclusion in a city without consent).
- Gets through: **taxes and assessments · purchase, improvement and repair debts (the home's mortgage) · labor on the property (construction liens)** · IRS liens (manual; not yet verified).
- The **spouse joins** the deed or mortgage even if not on title; devise restricted if spouse or minor child (manual; not yet verified).
- $1,000 personal property ≠ $25,000 tax exemption.
