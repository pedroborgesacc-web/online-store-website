## 2. Why it matters

When someone reports a Florida licensee, the case does not go straight to a penalty. It moves through a fixed sequence of steps, and each step belongs to a different body: the DBPR, a FREC panel, the FREC itself, a judge, and an appeals court. The exam tests the **order of the steps**, **who acts at each step**, and the **30-day deadline** to appeal.

This lesson, with L06-2 and L06-3, makes up Section 6 of the official exam outline (*Violations, Penalties and Procedures*, **3%**, about 3 of the 100 questions). In practice, if a letter from the DBPR ever arrives, this lesson tells you where you are in the process and which choices are yours.

## 3. Simple explanation

### Why the process has separate steps

A license is a person's livelihood, so the state does not act on an accusation alone. The people who **investigate** (DBPR) are not the people who **decide** (FREC); a **probable cause panel** screens out weak cases before formal charges; the licensee gets a **hearing**; and an independent **court** can review the result.

### The seven steps

The sequence comes from the study manual and is **partially verified**: the probable cause panel (V-42) and the appeal (V-43) are confirmed from official sources; the other steps are taught as the manual states them (manual; not yet verified).

1. **Complaint.** Filed with the **DBPR**. It must be **legally sufficient**: it alleges facts that, if true, would violate a statute or rule. It need not come from someone who was harmed, and an anonymous complaint can be investigated if it is legally sufficient and substantial.
2. **Investigation.** The **DBPR** investigates. The licensee is generally notified and may respond.
3. **Probable cause.** A FREC **probable cause panel** reviews the investigation and decides, by **majority vote**, whether there is enough evidence to justify formal charges. If not, no formal complaint is filed.
4. **Formal complaint.** If probable cause exists, a **formal (administrative) complaint** is filed against the licensee.
5. **Hearing.** The licensee chooses an **informal hearing** before the FREC when the facts are **not disputed**, or a **formal hearing** before an **administrative law judge (ALJ)** when the facts **are disputed**. The ALJ writes a **recommended order**.
6. **Final order.** The **FREC** issues the final order, which sets any penalty (L06-2).
7. **Appeal.** A party adversely affected may seek review in a **District Court of Appeal**. The notice of appeal must be filed within **30 days after rendition** (official issuing) of the order (s. 120.68, F.S.).

**Withdrawing the complaint does not stop the process.** The logic: discipline protects the public, so once the DBPR has a legally sufficient complaint, the case belongs to the state, not to the person who complained.

### Who sits on the probable cause panel

| Source | What it says |
|---|---|
| s. 455.225(4), F.S.; Rule 61J2-20.009, F.A.C. (verified, V-42) | **At least two members**, including a **present** FREC member; a **former** FREC member may serve. Probable cause is decided by **majority vote**. |
| Flag | The current rule's wording on panel size was seen only in fragments. |
| Exam answer (manual) | **Two members, at least one a current FREC member.** This fits the verified rule. |

### The appeal, precisely

- **Deadline:** notice of appeal within **30 days after rendition** of the final order (s. 120.68; Fla. R. App. P. 9.110(c)).
- **Filing:** with the **agency clerk**; a copy, with the filing fees, goes to the court clerk.
- **Court:** the District Court of Appeal for the district where the agency has its headquarters or where a party resides (or as otherwise provided by law).
- **Late notice:** an **irremediable jurisdictional defect**. The court cannot hear the appeal at all.

## 4. Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| DBPR | Department of Business and Professional Regulation: receives complaints and investigates |
| Legally sufficient | Alleges facts that, if true, would violate a statute or rule |
| Probable cause | Enough evidence from the investigation to justify formal charges |
| Probable cause panel | FREC panel (at least two members, at least one current FREC member) that votes on probable cause |
| Formal (administrative) complaint | The formal charges filed after probable cause is found |
| Informal hearing | Hearing before the FREC when the facts are not disputed |
| Formal hearing | Hearing before an administrative law judge when the facts are disputed |
| Administrative law judge (ALJ) | Judge from the Division of Administrative Hearings who conducts formal hearings |
| Recommended order | The ALJ's proposed decision; not the final word |
| Final order | The FREC's decision in the case |
| Rendition | The official issuing of the order; the 30-day appeal clock runs from it |
| District Court of Appeal | The Florida appeals court that reviews the FREC's final order |

## 5. Practical example

**Renata**, a sales associate in Orlando, sells a townhouse to **Mr. Okafor**. After closing he learns the community has a mandatory homeowners' association with monthly fees. He says Renata wrote to him that there was none, and he complains to the DBPR.

| Step | What happens | Who acts |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Complaint | Alleges a false statement of a material fact: legally sufficient | DBPR |
| 2. Investigation | Listing, contract and emails collected; Renata responds | DBPR |
| (meanwhile) | Mr. Okafor settles privately and withdraws. **The case continues.** | — |
| 3. Probable cause | Two-member panel (one current, one former FREC member) votes yes | FREC panel |
| 4. Formal complaint | Formal charges filed | DBPR |
| 5. Hearing | Renata says a later email corrected her mistake. Facts disputed → formal hearing → recommended order | ALJ |
| 6. Final order | Fine and probation | FREC |
| 7. Appeal | Notice filed with the agency clerk within 30 days after rendition; copy and fees to the court clerk | District Court of Appeal |

**Variations**

- If Renata agreed with the facts, she could choose an **informal hearing** before the FREC.
- A notice filed on day 34 is too late; the court has no power to hear the appeal.
- Mr. Okafor gets no money from this process. The FREC does not award damages; he would need a civil judgment (and possibly the Recovery Fund, L06-3).

## 6. Portugal comparison

**Familiar principle.** You are used to a public regulator supervising real estate intermediaries. In Portugal this is usually associated with **IMPIC, I.P.**, which licenses real estate mediation businesses (*requires verification*: this course has no checked Portugal comparison for this topic).

**Florida rule.** A complaint follows a fixed path split across bodies: DBPR investigates, a FREC panel decides probable cause, the FREC issues the final order, and a District Court of Appeal reviews it if a notice is filed within 30 days.

**Precise difference.** Florida separates investigating, screening, deciding and reviewing, in a fixed order. How a complaint against a Portuguese mediator is investigated, decided and appealed, and with what deadlines, *requires verification*, so no step-by-step comparison is offered here.

**Exam implication.** Answer procedure questions with the Florida sequence and body names only. Never use a Portuguese rule or deadline to answer a Florida question.

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

**Memorize exactly**

- Complaint → investigation → probable cause → formal complaint → hearing → final order → appeal.
- The **DBPR** receives complaints and **investigates**.
- Probable cause: **majority vote** of a panel of **at least two members**, including **at least one current FREC member**; a **former** member may serve (s. 455.225(4); Rule 61J2-20.009; verified, V-42).
- Informal hearing = facts **not disputed**, before the **FREC**. Formal hearing = facts **disputed**, before an **ALJ**, who issues a **recommended order** (manual; not yet verified).
- The **FREC** issues the **final order** and chooses the penalty within s. 475.25(1) (L06-2).
- Appeal: notice within **30 days after rendition**, to a **District Court of Appeal**; filed with the agency clerk, copy and fees to the court clerk (s. 120.68; Fla. R. App. P. 9.110(c); verified, V-43).

**Understand**

- The pipeline as a whole is manual-based and **partially verified**: the panel (V-42) and the appeal (V-43) are verified; the order of the other steps comes from the manual.
- Legal sufficiency, complaints from people who were not harmed, anonymous complaints, notice to the licensee, the DBPR filing the formal complaint, the FREC issuing the final order, and "withdrawal does not stop the case" (manual; not yet verified).
- A missed 30-day notice is an irremediable jurisdictional defect (verified, V-43).
- Panel size: the verified rule is "at least two members, including a present FREC member" (V-42); the current rule's wording on size was seen only in fragments. The manual's "two members, at least one a current FREC member" fits it and is the exam answer.

## 9. Common misconceptions

- **"Probable cause comes first, then the investigation."** The DBPR investigates first; the panel decides probable cause on the results.
- **"The formal complaint starts the case."** An ordinary complaint starts it; the formal complaint comes after probable cause.
- **"If the client withdraws the complaint, the case ends."** It continues; the case belongs to the state.
- **"Only a harmed client can complain."** Anyone may file a legally sufficient complaint; even an anonymous complaint can be investigated if it is legally sufficient and substantial.
- **"The FREC investigates."** The DBPR investigates; the FREC decides.
- **"The ALJ decides the penalty."** The ALJ recommends; the FREC issues the final order.
- **"Informal hearing means disputed facts."** Reversed: disputed facts go to an ALJ.
- **"Appeals go to the Florida Supreme Court or a county court, and a late notice can be excused."** A District Court of Appeal, and a late notice is fatal to the appeal.

## 10. Memory sheet

- **Complaint → Investigation → Probable cause → Formal complaint → Hearing → Final order → Appeal.**
- Traps: **I before P** (investigation before probable cause); **P before F** (probable cause before the formal complaint).
- **DBPR digs, panel screens, FREC decides, court reviews.**
- Panel: at least **2** members, at least **1 current** FREC member, **majority vote**.
- Facts agreed → **informal** (FREC). Facts disputed → **formal** (ALJ → recommended order → FREC).
- Appeal: **30 days after rendition** → District Court of Appeal. Late = no appeal.
- Withdrawn complaint ≠ closed case.
