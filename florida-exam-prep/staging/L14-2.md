## 2. Why it matters

**In practice.** Florida listings give living area in **square feet**, land in square feet or **acres**, and distances in **miles**. Lots on a street or on the water can be compared by the **front foot**, and carpet or concrete is often priced by the **square yard**. A buyer from Lisbon will ask you "how many square metres is that?", and a seller will ask "what is my lot worth per front foot compared with the one next door?". The first needs a quick metric conversion; the second is pure US-unit math.

**On the exam.** Area is one of the study manual's twelve math patterns (pattern 11). It belongs to Section 14, which carries **6%** of the exam (V-02). These questions test unit conversion as much as geometry: dividing square feet by 3 instead of 9 is the classic wrong answer.

## 3. Simple explanation

> **Source status.** The measurement constants below (square yard, acre, mile, square mile) come from the study manual and have not been independently checked by this course: **(manual; not yet verified, V-26)**. V-26 notes that they are standard definitions. The metric equivalents are verified arithmetic (1 ft = 0.3048 m exactly; PT-17).

### Step 1: What it represents

| Measure | What it measures | Units | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Length / perimeter** | Distance along a line, or all the way around a shape | Inches, feet (ft), yards (yd), miles | Fencing around a lot |
| **Area** | A flat surface | Square feet (sq ft), square yards (sq yd), acres | Living area, lot size |
| **Volume** | Space in three dimensions | Cubic feet, cubic yards | Concrete for a slab |
| **Front feet** | The length of a lot's side along the street or the water | Feet | A 75' × 150' lot has 75 front feet |

Symbols: **'** means feet and **"** means inches, so 75' × 150' means 75 feet by 150 feet.

### Step 2: Units

**Constants (manual; not yet verified, V-26)**

| Unit | Equals | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 foot | 12 inches | Definition |
| 1 yard | 3 feet | Definition |
| 1 square yard | **9 square feet** | 3 ft × 3 ft |
| 1 cubic yard | **27 cubic feet** | 3 ft × 3 ft × 3 ft |
| 1 mile | **5,280 feet** | = 1,760 yards (5,280 ÷ 3) |
| 1 acre | **43,560 square feet** | Definition |
| 1 square mile | **640 acres** | Definition |

**Why 9 and not 3?** A square yard is 3 feet long *and* 3 feet wide, so it contains 3 × 3 = 9 square feet. A cubic yard adds a third dimension: 3 × 3 × 3 = 27.

**Direction rule.** Going from a **bigger** unit to a **smaller** one, multiply (5 acres × 43,560 = 217,800 sq ft). Going from a **smaller** unit to a **bigger** one, divide (12,600 sq ft ÷ 43,560 ≈ 0.29 acre).

*Metric aside (never used in exam math):* 1 sq ft ≈ 0.0929 m²; 1 acre ≈ 4,047 m² ≈ 0.405 ha; 1 mile ≈ 1.61 km.

### Step 3: Formula

| To find | Formula |
|---|---|
| Area of a rectangle (or square) | length × width |
| Area of a triangle | ½ × base × height (the height is measured at a right angle to the base) |
| Area of a composite shape | Split it into rectangles and triangles, compute each, then add (subtract any cut-out) |
| Perimeter | Add all the sides; for a rectangle, 2 × (length + width) |
| Volume (if asked) | length × width × height; cubic yards = cubic feet ÷ 27 |
| Price per front foot | price ÷ front feet |
| Price per sq ft (or per acre) | price ÷ sq ft (or ÷ acres) |
| Total price from a unit price | area in the price's unit × unit price |

### Step 4: When it applies

- **Same unit first.** Every dimension must be in the same unit before you multiply. Inches → feet: divide by 12 (6 in = 0.5 ft; 4 in ≈ 0.333 ft).
- **Match the price's unit.** If the price is per square yard, convert the area to square yards (÷ 9). If it is per acre, divide by 43,560.
- **Front feet.** The frontage is the dimension along the street or water. It is **usually the first number given**: a 75' × 150' lot has 75 front feet, and 150 is the depth. If the question tells you which side faces the street or water, use that side.
- **Triangles.** Use the perpendicular height, not a slanted side, and do not forget the ½.
- **Volume** only when a thickness or depth appears (a concrete slab, fill dirt).

### Step 5: Worked example

**A. Rectangle and price per square foot.** An Ocala lot is 90 ft × 140 ft and sells at $14 per sq ft.

1. Area: 90 × 140 = **12,600 sq ft**.
2. Price: 12,600 × $14 = **$176,400**.
3. In acres: 12,600 ÷ 43,560 = 0.289, about **0.29 acre**.

**B. Composite (L-shaped) house.** The main block is 50 ft × 32 ft and a wing is 18 ft × 14 ft.

1. Main block: 50 × 32 = 1,600 sq ft. Wing: 18 × 14 = 252 sq ft.
2. Total: 1,600 + 252 = **1,852 sq ft**.

**C. Triangle.** A corner parcel has a base of 180 ft and a height of 220 ft. Area: ½ × 180 × 220 = **19,800 sq ft**.

**D. Front foot.** An 80' × 125' lot sells for $112,000. Front feet = 80. Price per front foot: $112,000 ÷ 80 = **$1,400**.

**E. Volume.** A slab is 30 ft × 20 ft and 6 inches thick. Thickness: 6 ÷ 12 = 0.5 ft. Volume: 30 × 20 × 0.5 = 300 cubic ft. In cubic yards: 300 ÷ 27 ≈ **11.11 cubic yd**.

### Step 6: Reasonableness check

- The number of square yards is about **one-ninth** of the number of square feet, and the number of acres is far smaller still.
- A quarter acre is 43,560 ÷ 4 = **10,890 sq ft**, so a typical lot of about 10,000 sq ft is "a bit under a quarter acre". One acre is roughly a square about 209 ft on each side.
- For the same lot, the price per front foot is much larger than the price per square foot, because each front foot carries the whole depth behind it.
- Work backwards: 1,600 + 252 = 1,852 ✓; $1,400 × 80 = $112,000 ✓.

### Step 7: Common errors

| Error | Correction |
|---|---|
| Dividing sq ft by **3** to get sq yd | Divide by **9** (3 × 3) |
| Dividing cubic ft by 9 to get cubic yd | Divide by **27** (3 × 3 × 3) |
| Mixing feet and yards (or inches) in one multiplication | Convert every dimension to one unit first |
| Forgetting the **½** for a triangle | Area = ½ × base × height |
| Using the depth as the frontage | Front feet = the street or water side, usually the first number |
| Rounding acres early (0.29 instead of 0.289…) | Keep full decimals until the last step |

### Step 9: Independent practice

**I1.** A triangular parcel has a base of 240 ft and a height of 160 ft. Find its area in square feet and in acres (two decimal places).

<details><summary>Check your answer</summary>

½ × 240 × 160 = **19,200 sq ft**. 19,200 ÷ 43,560 = 0.4408, about **0.44 acre**.

</details>

**I2.** A building footprint is a 60 ft × 40 ft rectangle with a triangular extension on the 40 ft side (base 40 ft, height 30 ft). What is the total area?

<details><summary>Check your answer</summary>

Rectangle: 60 × 40 = 2,400. Triangle: ½ × 40 × 30 = 600. Total: **3,000 sq ft**.

</details>

**I3.** A 3.25-acre parcel sells for $2.10 per square foot. What is the price?

<details><summary>Check your answer</summary>

3.25 × 43,560 = 141,570 sq ft. 141,570 × $2.10 = **$297,297**.

</details>

**I4.** An 85' × 130' lot will be fenced on every side except the 85 ft street front. Fencing costs $28 per linear foot. What is the cost?

<details><summary>Check your answer</summary>

Full perimeter: 2 × (85 + 130) = 430 ft. Without the front: 430 − 85 = 345 ft. Cost: 345 × $28 = **$9,660**.

</details>

**I5.** A rectangular 2-acre lot has 330 ft of frontage. How deep is it?

<details><summary>Check your answer</summary>

2 × 43,560 = 87,120 sq ft. Depth = 87,120 ÷ 330 = **264 ft**. Check: 330 × 264 = 87,120 ✓.

</details>

**I6.** A driveway slab is 40 ft long, 12 ft wide and 4 inches thick. How many cubic yards of concrete are needed (two decimal places)? And how many feet are in a 2.5-mile road?

<details><summary>Check your answer</summary>

Thickness 4 ÷ 12 = 0.333 ft. Volume: 40 × 12 × 4/12 = 160 cubic ft. 160 ÷ 27 = **5.93 cubic yd**. Road: 2.5 × 5,280 = **13,200 ft**.

</details>

### Step 10: Exam application

1. **Sketch** the shape and label every dimension.
2. **Convert** all dimensions to one unit (usually feet).
3. **Compute the area** by shape. Split composite shapes; use ½ for triangles.
4. **Convert the area** to the unit of the price: ÷ 9 for square yards, ÷ 43,560 for acres.
5. **Multiply** by the unit price, or **divide** the price by the units (front feet, sq ft, acres).
6. **Check** by working backwards and by size: square yards ≈ one-ninth of square feet.

Expect wrong answer choices built from ÷ 3 instead of ÷ 9, from a missing ½, and from using the depth instead of the frontage.

## 5. Practical example

**A canal lot in Cape Coral.** A couple from Lisbon is looking at a lot with **80 feet on the canal** and **125 feet of depth**, listed at **$240,000**. A second canal lot nearby has 90 feet of frontage, is 110 feet deep, and is listed at $256,500.

| | Lot 1 | Lot 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Front feet | 80 | 90 |
| Price per front foot | $240,000 ÷ 80 = **$3,000** | $256,500 ÷ 90 = **$2,850** |
| Area | 80 × 125 = 10,000 sq ft | 90 × 110 = 9,900 sq ft |
| Price per sq ft | $240,000 ÷ 10,000 = $24.00 | $256,500 ÷ 9,900 ≈ $25.91 |
| Acres | 10,000 ÷ 43,560 ≈ 0.23 | 9,900 ÷ 43,560 ≈ 0.23 |

Lot 2 costs more in total but **less per front foot**, which is exactly the comparison front-foot pricing is designed for. Per square foot it is dearer, because its shallower depth gives it less area for a higher price. For the clients' intuition you add that Lot 1 is about 929 m² (10,000 × 0.0929). Then you return to feet: Florida works in US customary units, and the exam uses nothing else.

They also want a paver patio of 24 ft × 18 ft, quoted at $65 per square yard: 24 × 18 = 432 sq ft; 432 ÷ 9 = 48 sq yd; 48 × $65 = **$3,120**.

## 6. Portugal comparison

**Familiar principle.** In Portugal you work in the metric system: m² and hectares (1 ha = 10,000 m²) (PT-17).

**Florida rule.** Florida uses US customary units: feet, square feet, acres (43,560 sq ft), miles (5,280 ft) and square yards (9 sq ft) (constants: manual; not yet verified, V-26). Exam math never uses metric.

**Precise difference.** Only the units, but you need new intuition for them:

| US unit | Metric equivalent (PT-17) |
|---|---|
| 1 sq ft | ≈ 0.0929 m² |
| 1,000 sq ft | ≈ 93 m² |
| 1 acre | ≈ 4,047 m² ≈ 0.405 ha |
| 1 mile | ≈ 1.61 km |

A 2,000 sq ft Florida house is about 186 m², a large T4 in Portuguese terms.

**Exam implication.** Work in feet and acres, and never convert to metric. Front feet = the frontage dimension (the first number given). For example, a 435,600 sq ft parcel is 435,600 ÷ 43,560 = **10 acres**. Memory aids: acre = 43,560 ("43-5-60"); mile = 5,280 ("five tomatoes": 5-2-80).

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

**Memorize exactly**

- **1 sq yd = 9 sq ft** · **1 acre = 43,560 sq ft** · **1 mile = 5,280 ft** · **1 sq mile = 640 acres** (manual; not yet verified, V-26; standard definitions). Also 1 ft = 12 in and 1 yd = 3 ft.
- Rectangle = length × width · triangle = **½ × base × height** · composite = split and add.
- Cubic yards = cubic feet ÷ **27**.
- Price per front foot = price ÷ front feet.

**Understand**

- No statute is involved: these are measurement definitions and geometry.
- Squaring the unit squares the factor: 3 → 9 for areas, and 3 → 27 for volumes.
- Front feet is the side along the street or water. It is *usually* the first number, but a question that names the frontage side overrides that habit.
- Convert first, calculate second, and match the unit the price is quoted in.

## 9. Common misconceptions

- **"A square yard is 3 square feet."** No. It is 3 ft × 3 ft = **9 sq ft**.
- **"Cubic yards = cubic feet ÷ 9."** No. Divide by **27**.
- **"Front feet is the longer side."** No. It is the side along the street or water, usually the **first** number given.
- **"Triangle area = base × height."** No. Multiply by **½** as well.
- **"I can multiply feet by yards and fix it later."** No. Convert first.
- **"I can work in metric and convert the answer back."** No. Exam math uses US units only, and converting back and forth adds rounding errors.
