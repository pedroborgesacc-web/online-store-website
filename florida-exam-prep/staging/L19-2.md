## 2. Why it matters

**In practice.** Every sale or lease of a home built before 1978 triggers federal lead-based paint paperwork, and the agents must make sure it is done. Every sale or rental of **any** building needs Florida's radon notice. Buyers of a former gas station or industrial site face a larger risk: under federal cleanup law, a new owner can pay for contamination someone else caused.

**On the exam.** Section 19, "Planning and Zoning", carries **1%** of the exam (V-02), about 1 of the 100 questions (V-01). Typical questions:

- Lead-based paint rules apply to housing built before which year? (**1978**, not 1988, the year familial status and handicap were added to fair housing law, V-56.)
- How many days does a buyer get for a lead inspection, unless waived? (**10**.)
- Can a new owner be liable for contamination she did not cause? (**Yes**, under CERCLA, unless she qualifies for a defense.)
- Intact asbestos insulation? (**Encapsulation** is often safer than removal.)

### Foundation: which government makes which rule

Two levels of law apply at the same time. **Federal** rules apply in every state; **Florida** statutes add state rules. Rules made by federal agencies are published in the **CFR** (Code of Federal Regulations). The **EPA** is the US Environmental Protection Agency; **HUD** is the US Department of Housing and Urban Development.

| Topic | Level | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Lead-based paint disclosure | Federal | EPA rule 40 CFR 745 Subpart F; HUD's identical rule 24 CFR Part 35 Subpart A (V-60) |
| Radon notice | Florida | s. 404.056(5) (V-73) |
| CERCLA (Superfund) | Federal | manual; not yet verified |

### Lead-based paint (V-60)

**Which housing? "Target housing"** = housing built **before 1978** (40 CFR 745.103). 1978 is the cut-off year the rule uses.

- **Excluded:** housing for the elderly or persons with disabilities, and **0-bedroom dwellings** (no separate bedroom, such as a studio), **unless a child under 6 lives or is expected to live there**.
- **Exempt transactions** (745.101): **foreclosure sales**; **leases of 100 days or less with no renewal**; leases of housing **certified lead-free**; **renewals** where disclosure was already made.

**What the seller or landlord (lessor) must do** (745.107, 745.113, 745.110):

| Duty | Sale | Lease |
|---|---|---|
| Give an EPA-approved pamphlet, such as *Protect Your Family From Lead in Your Home* | Yes | Yes |
| Disclose **known** lead-based paint and lead-based paint hazards | Yes | Yes |
| Provide **available** records and reports | Yes | Yes |
| Disclose the same to **each agent** | Yes | Yes |
| Include the **Lead Warning Statement** and **signed acknowledgments** in the contract or lease | Yes | Yes |
| Allow a **10-day** opportunity for a risk assessment or inspection **before the purchaser is bound** | **Yes** | **No** |

**Timing.** If the disclosure comes after an offer, it must be made **before the seller or lessor accepts** that offer. The point is that the buyer or tenant has the information while still free to walk away.

**The 10-day opportunity.** Before the buyer is bound by the contract, the seller must allow 10 days for a lead risk assessment or inspection, **unless the parties agree in writing on a different period**. The buyer may also **waive** the opportunity **in writing**. The manual's short version, "may be waived", is correct but incomplete. **Tenants** get the disclosure but **not** the 10-day period.

**Your duty as a licensee.** Licensees share responsibility for compliance: the rule requires **agents to ensure compliance** (745.115). Do not treat lead paperwork as only the seller's or landlord's job.

**What the rule does not say.** The duties verified in V-60 are duties to inform: pamphlet, known information, available reports, warning statement, and the buyer's inspection opportunity. V-60 describes no duty to test for lead or to remove paint.

### Radon (V-73)

**Radon** is a naturally occurring radioactive gas. Florida requires the statutory **radon notice** on **at least one document signed at or before** a **sale contract or rental agreement**, for **any building** (s. 404.056(5); V-73).

- **Any building:** new or old, residential or commercial; **sale or rental**.
- The manual's version, "in every sale and lease contract", points the right way. The verified wording is more precise: at least one document signed at or before the contract or rental agreement.
- It is a **notice**. This course's sources describe no testing requirement.

Other Florida disclosures around the contract (flood, property tax summary, energy-efficiency rating information) are in L11-4. Flood, for example: residential sales at or before contract (V-06); residential leases of 1 year or longer, since October 1, 2025 (V-07).

### Asbestos *(manual; not yet verified)*

**Asbestos** is a fibrous mineral used in older building materials, especially **insulation** (pre-1980s). It is dangerous when **friable**: crumbling, so fibres can get into the air and be breathed in. When the material is intact, **encapsulation** (sealing or covering it so fibres cannot escape) is often **safer than removal**, because removal disturbs the material and can release fibres.

### CERCLA / Superfund *(manual; not yet verified)*

**CERCLA**, the Comprehensive Environmental Response, Compensation, and Liability Act, known as **Superfund**, is the federal law on cleaning up contaminated sites. Its liability has two features:

| Feature | Meaning | Consequence for an owner |
|---|---|---|
| **Strict** liability | Liability without fault | An owner can be liable **without having caused** the contamination |
| **Joint and several** liability | Each liable party can be made to pay the whole cleanup cost, not only its own share | One owner may face the entire bill |

**The innocent-landowner defense.** A current owner can be liable **unless she qualifies for a defense**, such as the innocent-landowner defense. That defense requires appropriate **due diligence before purchase**, for example a **Phase I environmental audit** (an environmental review of the site and its history before buying).

**Why so strict?** It gets contaminated land cleaned up and pushes buyers to investigate first: a buyer who skipped the investigation cannot meet the defense's due-diligence requirement.

### Other exam words *(manual; not yet verified)*

| Term | Meaning | Florida-style example |
|---|---|---|
| **Brownfield** | Abandoned or underused contaminated site being redeveloped | An old industrial lot in Jacksonville cleaned up and rebuilt as apartments |
| **Wetlands** | Land that is wet or flooded much of the time, such as a marsh or swamp; development there is subject to wetlands permitting | A lot near a marsh in Collier County |

## 5. Practical example

*Names, dates and properties are invented for practice.*

Marta, a sales associate in Tampa, has five files this month.

| File | Lead-based paint rule? | Radon notice? | Other points |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1.** Sale of a 1962 bungalow to a couple with a 3-year-old | **Yes**: target housing. Pamphlet; known hazards; the seller's old inspection report (an available report); Lead Warning Statement and signed acknowledgments; **10-day** inspection opportunity | Yes | The seller signs the disclosure after the offer arrives, **before** he accepts: on time. The buyers may waive the 10 days in writing, or both sides may agree a different period in writing. |
| **2.** 12-month lease of a 1970 apartment | **Yes**: pamphlet and disclosure, **no** 10-day period | Yes | Lease of 1 year or longer: flood disclosure too (V-07) |
| **3.** 90-day lease of a similar 1970 apartment, no renewal | **No**: lease of 100 days or less with no renewal is exempt | **Yes** | Radon applies to any rental agreement |
| **4.** Sale of a house built in 2024 | **No**: not built before 1978 | **Yes** | Radon is not limited to older buildings |
| **5.** Investor buying a closed gas station lot to build shops | Not housing | Yes (there is a building) | Possible **USTs**. Under **CERCLA** the new owner could be liable for old contamination. Marta recommends a **Phase I environmental audit before closing**, which the innocent-landowner defense requires. After cleanup, the lot could become a **brownfield** redevelopment. |

In file 1, the inspector also finds intact, non-crumbling asbestos insulation on old pipes: encapsulation is often safer than removal. Marta refers the buyers to qualified professionals rather than giving a technical opinion.

Lead and radon points: verified (V-60, V-73). CERCLA, UST, brownfield and asbestos points: manual; not yet verified.

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

**Memorize exactly**

- Lead: target housing = built **before 1978** (V-60).
- Seller or lessor: EPA-approved **pamphlet**; disclose **known** lead-based paint and hazards; provide **available** records and reports; disclose the same to **each agent**; **Lead Warning Statement** and **signed acknowledgments** in the contract or lease (V-60).
- Disclosure after an offer → **before acceptance** (V-60).
- Buyer: **10 days** for a risk assessment or inspection **before being bound**; a different period if agreed **in writing**; may **waive in writing**. Tenants: disclosure but **no 10-day period** (V-60).
- Excluded: housing for the elderly or persons with disabilities and 0-bedroom dwellings, **unless a child under 6** lives or is expected to live there (V-60).
- Exempt: **foreclosure sales**; leases of **100 days or less** with no renewal; leases of housing certified lead-free; renewals where disclosure was already made (V-60).
- Agents must **ensure compliance** (40 CFR 745.115; V-60).
- Radon: **any building**, **sale or rental**; statutory notice on at least one document signed **at or before** the contract or rental agreement (s. 404.056(5); V-73).
- CERCLA: **strict, joint and several** liability; an owner can be liable **without causing** the contamination; innocent-landowner defense requires **due diligence before purchase** (Phase I environmental audit) (manual; not yet verified).
- Asbestos: dangerous when **friable**; **encapsulation** often safer than removal (manual; not yet verified).

**Understand**

- Why **1978** (lead) must not be confused with **1988** (fair housing: familial status and handicap, V-56).
- Lead and CERCLA are **federal**; the radon notice is **Florida** law.
- Brownfields, wetlands permitting, EIS for major federal actions, USTs near gas stations (manual; not yet verified).

## 9. Common misconceptions

- **"The lead paint year is 1988."** No: **1978**. 1988 is when fair housing added familial status and handicap (V-56).
- **"Tenants also get 10 days to inspect."** No. Tenants get the pamphlet and disclosure only (V-60).
- **"The seller can disclose after accepting the offer."** No. If the disclosure comes after an offer, it must be made before acceptance (V-60).
- **"The lead rule forces the seller to test or remove the paint."** The verified duties are to inform: pamphlet, known information, available reports, warning statement, buyer's inspection opportunity (V-60).
- **"Lead compliance is only the seller's problem."** Agents must ensure compliance (745.115).
- **"The radon notice is only for older homes, or only when radon is found, or only for sales."** It is for the sale or rental of any building (V-73).
- **"A new owner who did not cause the contamination cannot be liable."** Under CERCLA she can be, unless she qualifies for a defense such as innocent landowner (manual; not yet verified).
- **"Asbestos must always be removed at once."** Intact, non-friable asbestos is often better encapsulated (manual; not yet verified).
