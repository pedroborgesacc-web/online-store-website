## 2. Why it matters

Every Florida sale that is closed with a deed pays **documentary stamp tax**. When the buyer finances, there are two more taxes: documentary stamps on the **note** and the **intangible tax** on the mortgage. Sellers ask "what will I net?" and buyers ask "what are my closing costs?", and you cannot answer either question without these three figures. They also appear as lines on the closing statement (lesson L14-8).

For the exam, these are Florida-specific rates that you must know exactly. The questions test four things: the rate, the base (price or loan), the **rounding up** to the next $100, and who customarily pays. Wrong answer choices are built from rounding down, the wrong base, or the intangible tax charged on an assumed loan.

## 3. Simple explanation

### Step 1: What it represents

Florida collects three taxes on the documents of a typical financed sale:

| Tax | What it taxes | Source (verified) |
|---|---|---|
| **Documentary stamp tax on the deed** ("deed stamps") | The deed, measured by the **total consideration**, meaning the full price, including any existing mortgage the buyer takes over | s. 201.02; Miami-Dade surtax s. 201.031 (V-18) |
| **Documentary stamp tax on the note** ("note stamps") | Promissory notes and other written obligations to pay money | s. 201.08 (V-18) |
| **Nonrecurring intangible tax** | Obligations secured by a mortgage or lien on Florida real property: a **one-time** tax paid when the mortgage is recorded | s. 199.133 (V-19) |

### Step 2: Units

- **"Per $100 or portion thereof"** (V-18): count how many $100 units the amount contains. Any part of a $100 unit counts as a full unit. In practice: **round the amount UP to the next $100**, then divide by 100.
- **Mill** = one-thousandth = $1 per $1,000 = 0.001. **2 mills = 0.002**.
- The intangible tax is **not rounded**. Multiply the exact loan amount by 0.002.
- All results are in dollars and cents.

### Step 3: Formula

| Tax | Formula |
|---|---|
| Deed stamps | (Price rounded UP to next $100) ÷ 100 × **$0.70** |
| Deed stamps, Miami-Dade, single-family residence | Same, × **$0.60** |
| Deed stamps, Miami-Dade, other property | Same, × ($0.60 + $0.45 surtax) = **$1.05** |
| Note stamps | (Loan rounded UP to next $100) ÷ 100 × **$0.35** |
| Intangible tax | New mortgage amount × **0.002** (no rounding) |

### Step 4: When it applies

| Situation | Deed stamps | Note stamps | Intangible tax |
|---|---|---|---|
| All-cash sale | Yes, on full price | No | No |
| Sale with a **new** mortgage | Yes, on full price | Yes, on the new loan | Yes, on the new loan |
| Buyer **assumes** the seller's mortgage | Yes, on full price; the assumed balance counts as consideration (V-18) | Yes, on the assumed balance: course convention, confirmed by Florida DOR (V-25) | **No**: not due again on an assumed mortgage when it was paid originally and the debt is unchanged (s. 199.145(3), V-25) |

Two more qualifications:

- **Miami-Dade:** deed stamps are $0.60 per $100 instead of $0.70. The $0.45 surtax applies to documents **other than** those involving only a single-family residence, so for that other property the combined rate is $1.05 per $100 (V-18; s. 125.0167 limits the surtax to Miami-Dade).
- **Cap on note stamps:** the $2,450 cap applies **only to unsecured documents**. A note secured by a mortgage has **no cap** (V-18).

**Who pays (custom, not the tax statute):** the seller customarily pays deed stamps; the buyer customarily pays note stamps and intangible tax on financing. The sales contract can allocate them differently. (Customary allocation: manual; not yet verified. V-18 and V-19 verified the rates and bases, not who pays.)

### Step 5: Worked example

**Manual example, Broward County:** $412,480 sale; buyer takes a new $330,000 loan.

| Tax | Work | Result | Customarily paid by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deed stamps | $412,480 → $412,500 → 4,125 × $0.70 | **$2,887.50** | Seller |
| Note stamps | $330,000 (already a whole $100) → 3,300 × $0.35 | **$1,155.00** | Buyer |
| Intangible tax | 330,000 × 0.002 | **$660.00** | Buyer |

**Same sale, but the buyer ASSUMES a $330,000 mortgage instead:** deed stamps are unchanged ($2,887.50, on the full price). Note stamps still apply ($1,155). Intangible tax: **$0**, because this is not new money.

### Step 6: Reasonableness check

- Deed stamps are about **0.7% of the price**: 412,480 × 0.007 ≈ 2,887 ✓.
- Note stamps on the same amount are **half** the deed rate; intangible tax is **0.2%** of the loan, which is always less than the note stamps.
- **Divisibility check:** a correct deed-stamp answer divided by 0.70 gives a whole number (2,887.50 ÷ 0.70 = 4,125 ✓). A note-stamp answer divided by 0.35 gives a whole number. If it does not, you skipped the rounding. (A rounded-*down* answer passes this check, so also confirm you went **up**.)
- When the price is not a round $100, the right answer is slightly **higher** than the "plain" multiplication, never lower.

### Step 7: Common errors

| Error | Example | Correct |
|---|---|---|
| Rounding down or to the nearest $100 | $199,950 → 1,999 × 0.70 = $1,399.30 | 2,000 × 0.70 = **$1,400** |
| Deed stamps on the loan or on the cash paid | Assumption sale taxed only on the cash | Full price, including the assumed mortgage |
| Intangible tax on an assumed mortgage | $150,000 × 0.002 = $300 | **$0** (not new money) |
| Rounding the intangible tax | Loan rounded up first | Exact loan × 0.002 |
| $0.60 on any Miami-Dade property | Office building at $0.60 | $0.60 single-family only; other property $1.05 |
| Forgetting a second note | Only the first mortgage taxed | Note stamps on every note (new and assumed) |
| Seller and buyer reversed | Buyer pays deed stamps | Customarily seller: deed; buyer: note + intangible |

### Step 10: Exam application

- **Identify the document first:** deed → $0.70 on the price; note → $0.35 on the loan; new mortgage → 0.002.
- **Check the county:** Miami-Dade single-family → $0.60. Any other county → $0.70.
- **Round up stamps, never the intangible tax.** The classic wrong choice is the rounded-down figure ($1,399.30 instead of $1,400). The divisibility check catches only answers computed with no rounding at all.
- **Assumption questions:** deed stamps still on the full price; note stamps yes; intangible tax $0.
- **"Who customarily pays":** seller for deed stamps; buyer for note stamps and intangible tax. Contracts can allocate differently, so follow the facts if the question gives a contract term.

## 4. Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Documentary stamp tax | Florida excise tax on documents: deeds ($0.70/$100) and notes ($0.35/$100) |
| Deed stamps | Documentary stamp tax on the deed, based on the total consideration |
| Note stamps | Documentary stamp tax on a promissory note or written obligation to pay |
| Consideration | What the buyer gives for the property: cash, new financing and any mortgage assumed |
| "Or portion thereof" | Any part of a $100 counts as a full $100, so round up |
| Intangible tax (nonrecurring) | One-time tax of 2 mills on obligations secured by a mortgage or lien on Florida real property; taught as: **new** mortgages only |
| Mill | One-thousandth (0.001); 2 mills = 0.002 |
| Assumed mortgage | An existing loan the buyer takes over from the seller |
| Surtax (Miami-Dade) | Miami-Dade only: extra $0.45/$100 on deeds other than those involving only a single-family residence |

## 5. Practical example

**A sale in Jacksonville (Duval County).** Marta lists a home that goes under contract at **$358,900**. The buyer obtains a new loan for 80% of the price, which is **$287,120**. The contract follows the usual custom for transfer taxes.

| Item | Work | Amount | On whose side |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deed stamps | $358,900 → 3,589 × $0.70 | **$2,512.30** | Seller |
| Note stamps | $287,120 → $287,200 → 2,872 × $0.35 | **$1,005.20** | Buyer |
| Intangible tax | 287,120 × 0.002 | **$574.24** | Buyer |
| Buyer's transfer taxes | 1,005.20 + 574.24 | **$1,579.44** | Buyer |

When Marta prepares the seller's net sheet, she shows $2,512.30 of deed stamps as a seller cost. If the buyer had instead assumed a $287,120 mortgage (debt unchanged, intangible tax paid originally), the deed stamps and note stamps would be identical, and the intangible tax would be $0. If the contract had shifted the deed stamps to the buyer, Marta would follow the contract rather than the custom.

## 6. Portugal comparison

**Familiar principle (PT-09):** in Portugal the **buyer** pays IMT (progressive, on the higher of the price and the taxable property value) and Imposto do Selo at 0.8%. Young buyers up to 35 have exemptions up to set limits (330,539 € in the 2026 tables for full exemption). Decreto-Lei n.º 97/2026 sets a 7.5% IMT rate for non-residents buying housing, with exceptions.

**Florida rule:** the main tax on the transfer, documentary stamp tax on the **deed** ($0.70 per $100; Miami-Dade single-family $0.60), is customarily the **seller's** cost. The buyer customarily pays note stamps ($0.35 per $100) and intangible tax (2 mills on new mortgages) when financing.

**Precise difference:** the payer is reversed for the main tax, and Florida's rates are **flat per $100** and rounded **up** to the next $100, with no progressive brackets.

**Example:** a $350,050 sale in Orange County → round up to $350,100 → 3,501 × $0.70 = **$2,450.70** in deed stamps, normally charged to the seller.

**Exam implication:** do not let the Portuguese reflex ("the buyer pays the transfer tax") answer a Florida question. Round stamps up; charge intangible tax only on new money.

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

**Memorize exactly**

- Deed stamps: **$0.70 per $100 or portion thereof** of the total consideration; round **UP** to the next $100; full price, including any mortgage assumed (s. 201.02, V-18).
- Miami-Dade: **$0.60** per $100 for a single-family residence; other property $0.60 + **$0.45** surtax = $1.05 (ss. 201.02 and 201.031, V-18). The manual gives these figures and V-18 confirms them.
- Note stamps: **$0.35 per $100 or portion thereof**, on new **and** assumed mortgages (s. 201.08; course convention confirmed by V-25). Cap of $2,450 only for unsecured documents; no cap for mortgage-secured notes (V-18).
- Intangible tax: **2 mills (0.002)** × the **new** mortgage; no rounding; one-time, paid when the mortgage is recorded (s. 199.133, V-19). Not due again on an assumed mortgage when it was paid originally and the debt is unchanged (s. 199.145(3), V-25).
- Customarily: seller pays deed stamps; buyer pays note stamps + intangible tax (manual; not yet verified). Contracts can allocate differently.

**Understand**

- "Or portion thereof" is the legal reason for rounding up: a partial $100 is taxed as a whole $100.
- The deed is taxed on everything the seller receives, including debt the buyer takes over. That is why an assumed mortgage is part of the deed-stamp base.
- The intangible tax is a one-time tax on the mortgage debt, so an unchanged debt that already paid it is not taxed again. The note stamps, by contrast, apply to the assumed obligation (V-25).

## 9. Common misconceptions

- **"Deed stamps are on the cash the seller receives."** No. They are on the full consideration, including any assumed mortgage.
- **"Round to the nearest $100."** No. Any portion of $100 counts, so always round **up**.
- **"Assumed mortgage → no taxes on the loan."** Note stamps still apply (V-25); only the intangible tax is not due again.
- **"Miami-Dade is always $0.60."** Only for a single-family residence; other property pays $0.60 + $0.45.
- **"Note stamps are capped at $2,450."** Only unsecured documents are capped; a mortgage note has no cap.
- **"The buyer pays the transfer tax, as in Portugal."** In Florida the seller customarily pays deed stamps.
