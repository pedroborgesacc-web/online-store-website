## 2. Why it matters

Many Florida licensees lease and manage rentals for owners, and almost every sales associate eventually writes a lease. The Florida Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (Chapter 83, F.S.) sets the deadlines a landlord or property manager must meet. Missing one can cost the landlord the right to keep any of the deposit.

On the exam (Section VII, **3%**, shared with L07-1 to L07-3), this lesson is mostly **day counts**: **30** (deposit notice), **15 / 30 / 15** (move-out) and **60 / 30 / 30 / 7** (ending tenancies). Several rules differ from the study manual: the month-to-month notice (15 days in the 2014 statute and in older books, 30 days now), e-mail delivery of deposit notices (since July 1, 2025) and a new flood disclosure for leases (since October 1, 2025).

## 3. Simple explanation

### Holding the tenant's money

A **security deposit** protects the landlord against damage or unpaid rent. **Advance rent** is rent paid before the period it covers. In both cases the landlord is holding money that came from the tenant, so the law says how the landlord must hold it and how the tenant must be told.

- **How it is held.** The money is kept in a separate Florida bank account, which may or may not pay interest, or the landlord posts a **surety bond** (a guarantee from a bonding company) instead.
- **Written notice, in the lease or within 30 days of receipt.** The notice gives:
  - the **name and address of the depository** (bank) holding the money, or a statement that a **surety bond** was posted;
  - whether the tenant is **entitled to interest**;
  - the disclosure text required by the statute.
- **How the notice is delivered.** In person or by mail. Since **July 1, 2025** it may also go by **e-mail under s. 83.505**, but only if both parties signed the statutory e-mail addendum.
- **Changes.** If the landlord later changes how or where the money is held, the tenant must be told **within 30 days after the change**.
- **Exception.** This notice rule does **not** apply to a landlord who rents **fewer than five** individual dwelling units.
- **Rent is still due.** A missing notice is **not** a defense to paying rent on time.

### After the tenancy ends: 15 / 30 / 15

The clock starts when the tenancy ends. V-63 counts from the end of the **rental agreement**; the study manual says "after the tenant moves out". When the tenant leaves at the end of the lease, these are the same day.

1. **No claim.** If the landlord will not claim any of the deposit, it is returned, with interest if required, **within 15 days**.
2. **Claim.** If the landlord intends to keep some or all of it, the landlord sends **written notice within 30 days**, stating the reason. The notice goes by **certified mail** (mail with proof of mailing and delivery) to the tenant's last known mailing address. Since **July 1, 2025**, it may also go **by e-mail**, if both parties signed the s. 83.505 e-mail addendum and gave e-mail addresses.
3. **Objection.** The tenant has **15 days after receiving the notice** to object. Without an objection, the landlord deducts the claim and sends the balance **within 30 days after the date of the notice**.
4. **Missed deadline.** A landlord who misses the 30-day notice **forfeits** the right to claim against the deposit and may not set the damage off against it. The landlord may still **sue for damages** after returning the deposit.

In a dispute over the deposit, the winning party recovers attorney's fees.

The idea behind the deadlines: the tenant should not wait indefinitely for their own money. A landlord who stays silent past day 30 loses the right to take any of it from the deposit.

### Ending a tenancy without a specific term (s. 83.57)

Some tenancies have no end date. They simply renew each week, month, quarter or year. To end one, **written notice** must be given at least this long **before the end of a period**:

| Tenancy | Minimum written notice |
|---|---|
| Year to year | **60 days** |
| Quarter to quarter | **30 days** |
| Month to month | **30 days** |
| Week to week | **7 days** |

**Correction (V-04).** The study manual lists **15 days** for month to month and **45 days** for quarter to quarter. Current law says **30 and 30**. Older books show 15 days for month to month because the statute later changed (the 2014 text had 15 days). No version of the statute with 45 days was found. If a practice question uses 15 days for month to month, it reflects the old law: answer with current law (30 days) unless the question says otherwise.

### Flood disclosure for leases (s. 83.512, since October 1, 2025)

- **When.** Before or at the signing of a residential lease for a term of **1 year or longer**. Shorter leases are not covered.
- **Form.** A **separate document** on the statutory form. It covers:
  - flood damage the landlord **knows of** during the landlord's ownership;
  - **flood insurance claims**, including claims under the NFIP (National Flood Insurance Program);
  - federal **assistance** received for flood damage, such as from FEMA (Federal Emergency Management Agency);
  - notices about flood insurance.
- **Remedy.** If the landlord does not comply, and the tenant suffers a **substantial loss** of personal property from flooding, the tenant may **end the lease**. A substantial loss means repair or replacement costs of **50% or more** of the property's market value. The tenant ends the lease by **written notice** and by giving up possession **within 30 days after the damage**. The landlord refunds rent paid in advance for the period after termination.
- **Origin.** The rule was enacted by CS/CS/SB 948 (ch. 2025-166, Laws of Fla.). A companion House bill, HB 1015, was tabled. Older books will not mention it.

### Other lease disclosures (taught elsewhere)

- **Radon:** the statutory radon notice appears on at least one document signed at or before a rental agreement for any building (L11-4, L19-2).
- **Lead-based paint**, for housing built before 1978: lessors give the disclosure (EPA pamphlet, known lead paint and hazards, available reports), but tenants do **not** get the buyer's 10-day inspection opportunity (L19-2).

## 4. Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Security deposit | Money the tenant pays to secure performance of the lease |
| Advance rent | Rent paid before the period it covers |
| Surety bond | A bond posted by the landlord instead of holding the deposit in an account |
| Depository | The bank or institution holding the deposit |
| Certified mail | Mail sent with proof of mailing and delivery |
| E-mail addendum (s. 83.505) | Signed lease addendum allowing notices by e-mail (since July 1, 2025) |
| Notice of intention to impose a claim | The landlord's written notice, within 30 days, stating why part of the deposit will be kept |
| Forfeit | Lose a right by failing to meet a condition or deadline |
| Tenancy without a specific term | A periodic tenancy that renews week to week, month to month, quarter to quarter or year to year |

## 5. Practical example

**Carla**, a sales associate in Gainesville, manages 8 apartments for one owner. A tenant signs a **12-month lease** running July 1 to June 30 and pays a **$2,000** deposit.

| Moment | What Carla must do | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| At or before signing | Give the flood disclosure form | Lease of 1 year or longer |
| In the lease (or within 30 days of receiving the deposit) | Give the name and address of the bank holding the deposit (or state that a surety bond was posted) and say whether interest is paid | Owner rents 5 or more units |
| Lease ends June 30, no damage | Return the $2,000 by **July 15** | 15 days |
| Lease ends June 30, broken door ($400) | Send notice of the claim by **July 30**, by certified mail (or e-mail if the addendum was signed) | 30 days |
| Notice sent July 10, received July 13 | The tenant may object until **July 28** | 15 days after receipt |
| No objection | Deduct $400 and send **$1,600** by **August 9** | 30 days after the date of the notice |
| Notice sent August 4 instead | Too late: no claim against the deposit. Return the full $2,000; the owner may sue for damages separately. | Forfeiture |

In the same building, a **month-to-month** tenant's period ends September 30. To end that tenancy, the owner gives written notice **at least 30 days** before September 30, for example on August 25. A notice sent 15 days ahead follows outdated law.

A snowbird's **6-month** seasonal lease does **not** require the flood disclosure.

## 6. Portugal comparison

**Familiar principle.** In Portugal, urban leases are governed by the Civil Code and the NRAU. Their written-form and registration requirements *require verification*.

**Florida rule.** A lease of **1 year or less** may be oral. A longer lease must be in writing (statute of frauds), but leases no longer need witnesses (s. 689.01, V-05). Florida sets exact day counts: **60 / 30 / 30 / 7** to end tenancies without a specific term, **30 days** for the deposit notice, and **15 / 30 / 15** at move-out.

**Precise difference.** The exam tests these specific Florida day counts. Portuguese notice periods and deposit customs must not be carried over.

**Exam implication.** A month-to-month tenant in Orlando gets written notice at least **30 days** before the end of a monthly period. Use the current s. 83.57 periods.

## 7. Legal and numerical requirements

**Memorize exactly**

- Deposit notice: **in the lease or within 30 days** of receipt: the depository's name and address (or a surety bond) and whether interest is paid. **Not required** for landlords who rent **fewer than 5 units**. A change in how it is held: notice within **30 days** (V-62).
- Move-out: **15 days** to return (no claim) · **30 days** to send the claim notice (certified mail, or e-mail with a signed s. 83.505 addendum since **July 1, 2025**) · tenant objects within **15 days** of receipt · balance within **30 days** of the notice date (V-63).
- A missed 30-day notice **forfeits** the claim against the deposit. A separate lawsuit for damages remains possible.
- Termination notices (s. 83.57): **60 / 30 / 30 / 7** (year / quarter / month / week) (V-04).
- Flood disclosure (s. 83.512, since **October 1, 2025**): residential lease of **1 year or longer**, **at or before signing**, separate statutory form (V-07).

**Understand**

- **Outdated figures:**
  - 15 days (month to month) and 45 days (quarter to quarter) are **outdated or incorrect**.
  - "Certified mail only" is outdated since July 1, 2025 (ch. 2025-16, Laws of Fla.).
  - The old wording, notice of "the manner in which the deposit is held", has been replaced by the current disclosure, but the answer is still **30 days**.
- Flood remedy: a substantial loss (**50% or more** of market value) lets the tenant terminate within **30 days** after the damage, with a refund of prepaid rent.
- Leases over 1 year must be in writing (statute of frauds), and leases need no witnesses (V-05).
- From the manual only (manual; not yet verified): the separate Florida account, interest-bearing or not, as a way to hold the deposit; attorney's fees for the prevailing party in a deposit dispute.

## 9. Common misconceptions

- **"Month to month needs 15 days' notice."** That is the old law. It is **30 days**.
- **"Quarter to quarter needs 45 days."** It is **30 days**. No version with 45 days was found.
- **"A late claim notice still lets the landlord deduct, because the damage is real."** A missed 30-day notice forfeits the claim against the deposit. The landlord must sue separately.
- **"The landlord has 30 days to return the deposit in every case."** With no claim, it is **15 days**.
- **"The tenant has 15 days from move-out to object."** The 15 days run from **receiving the notice**.
- **"Only certified mail is valid."** Since July 1, 2025, e-mail also works if both parties signed the e-mail addendum.
- **"Every landlord must send the deposit notice."** Landlords who rent fewer than 5 units are exempt.
- **"No deposit notice means the tenant can stop paying rent."** It is not a defense to paying rent.
- **"The flood disclosure applies only in flood zones, or to every lease."** It applies to residential leases of **1 year or longer**, with no flood-zone condition.

## 10. Memory sheet

- Deposit notice: **in the lease or 30 days** · bank or surety bond · interest yes/no · exempt if **<5 units**.
- Move-out: **15 return · 30 claim · 15 object** · balance within 30 days of the notice. Late notice = **forfeit**.
- Notice delivery: certified mail **or** e-mail with a signed addendum (since 7/1/2025).
- End a tenancy: **60 / 30 / 30 / 7** (year / quarter / month / week). 15 and 45 are outdated.
- Lease of **1 year or more** → flood form **at or before signing** (s. 83.512, since 10/1/2025).
